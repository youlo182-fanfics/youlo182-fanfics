Only this time, Maya wasn't alone. Her girlfriend had come to collect her from the academy on this last Friday of September.

Maya felt her neck stiffen.

Lucy had finally opened the file and in front of her. And the one on the screen.

- "To summarize simply: we're dealing with two overlapping problems. Severe, highly progressive endometriosis and stage II gynecological cancer. Both influence each other, and both count in the decisions we're going to make."

Carina had inhaled slowly, as if preparing to take it in.

"What we want is to treat you effectively, Maya. And protect you in the long term." continued Lucy.

"In your case, the safest option medically remains a wider surgery. A hysterectomy, possibly combined with the removal of one or both ovaries, depending on what the next tests show."

Maya had tightened her fingers on her own hands.

- "And... what if we don't do that?" she asked, her voice low.

Lucy hadn't looked away.

- "There are other options. Hormonal treatments, sometimes chemotherapy depending on the exact course. But with the rapid progression of your endometriosis, and the fact that cancer cells have already been found, these options are less protective in the long term. They can delay, control... but not offer the same security."

A silence had fallen.

Carina had finally asked:

- "Concretely... what does this mean for her in the coming months?"

Lucy had taken the time to answer.

- "It means that first we'll complete the workup: imaging, analysis, to pinpoint the exact extension. Then we'll decide together on the timetable and strategy. Nothing is done in absolute haste... but we're not dragging our feet either."

Maya had the impression of hearing all this from a great distance.

- "And... after the operation?" she asked.

"There will be a convalescence. Maybe complementary treatments depending on what we find. And regular follow-up. The goal is remission. And that it lasts."

Carina nodded slowly. Then, after a moment's hesitation, she leaned forward slightly.

- "And... for..."

She'd paused, searching for words, then resumed, more gently:

"To... have children... one day..."

Maya had turned her head toward her, abruptly.

- "Carina!"

There was something surprised in her voice. Almost shocked. Lucy, on the other hand, understood at once.

"It's called fertility preservation."

She had paused, then continued:

Maya had looked down at her hands.

Maya had looked up.

"Today, we inform. We think. And we're taking it one step at a time. Together."

Carina had discreetly slipped her hand over Maya's. And the blonde had squeezed it a little tighter than necessary.

She still hadn't cried «for herself». But for the first time in a week, she felt that all this was no longer just a word on a file. It was an entire life being reorganized, piece by piece. Her life.

Lucy had looked at the blonde for a few seconds, as if assessing whether she still had the energy to hear anything other than weighty decisions.

Maya had nodded.

Carina had squeezed her hand a little tighter, then reluctantly let go as Lucy got up to go prepare for the exam.

Maya had exhaled, without realizing it.
