The days following the weekend at Dean's had been strangely quiet.

On Monday, Maya had practically slept all day. She'd returned to the DeLuca house exhausted. Not just physically - although her body felt like it weighed a ton - but mentally too.

The weekend had been... a lot.Lots of noise.Lots of conversation.Lots of emotions.

Her body had been crying out for rest. And for once, Maya Bishop had listened. She slept. For a long time.

Carina had visited the room several times. She made sure she'd had a drink and something to eat. She drew the curtains to let in the soft late afternoon light. And Maya had slept again.

Tuesday had been much the same. Between naps, she'd gotten up to eat something, then gone back to bed. In the evening, she'd spent hours in Carina's arms, simply breathing against her. And slowly... very slowly... Her body began to recover.

By Wednesday, she'd felt a little more stable. Less breathless. The dizziness had become less frequent. She wasn't in top form - far from it - but for the first time in weeks, she felt as if her body was no longer fighting against her with every movement. As if rest had finally won a battle.

And on Thursday morning, when she'd woken up, she'd even felt like she had... energy. Not a lot of energy. But enough to stay on her feet without the room spinning around her. Enough to tell herself that maybe... maybe she was right. Maybe it would all work out in the end.

The American had straightened up in bed and looked around the room. And that's when the guilt had caught up with her. Because Carina had been... different since the weekend. Not cold. Never cold, just... more cautious. Quieter.

She still touched her. She still spoke to her with the same gentleness. She took care of her as always. But something had changed. Something Maya had noticed. All because she hadn't followed her into the bathroom that lunchtime at Dean's house. She hadn't joined her to apologize. And that she hadn't said anything since their return about it either.

And Maya knew why. Because she'd blown her off - that at the time she'd found it justified and that apologizing - again - was tantamount to admitting her fault. But in a way, her girlfriend was at fault too. She hadn't stopped mothering her. In front of their friends - showing that she was weak. But she also knew she'd been hard on Carina. When she'd probably only done it out of love and concern.

And she hadn't gone to her when Carina had returned with Andy, her eyes red. Because she'd raised her voice. Because she'd refused her help in front of everyone. And because she hadn't had the sense to apologize afterwards. She'd been obnoxious to Carina. And since then, the Italian was still a little hurt.

The Olympic champion had sighed.

- "Well done, Bishop..."

She'd run a hand over her face. Then she'd looked around. Carina's room was... a bit of a mess. Nothing dramatic. But enough to show that the last few weeks had been devoted to something other than housework.

The sheets were wrinkled. Clothes were folded on a chair. A few books were stacked on the bedside table.

Maya smiled. If Carina had taken care of her for weeks... she could at least do this to thank her.

So she stood up. Slowly. She'd taken the sheets off the bed and carried them to the washing machine. She threw in a load of laundry. Then she'd gone back upstairs to look for clean sheets in Carina's wardrobe.

She opened the big door and the familiar smell of detergent and Italian perfume wafted out.

Maya began rummaging through the shelves. Sweaters folded.

A few dresses hung up. Boxes. Then she crouched down to look at the bottom shelf.

And that's where she'd seen them. In a corner of the wardrobe. Almost hidden behind a pile of sheets. A dozen notebooks. All identical. Black covers. Lined up one on top of the other.

Maya had frowned. She reached out and took one. The leather was slightly worn around the edges. As if it had been handled a lot.

Maya held the notebook in her hands for a moment. She knew she shouldn't open it. She turned it over in her hands. Then, almost without thinking... She opened it anyway. Without reading again. Without paying attention to the words. She'd just caressed the words on the paper. Carina's handwriting. Italian words. She had read the word «Partenza per Seattle» [Departure for Seattle].

Diaries were probably the most private thing a person could have. And yet...

Carina had never mentioned them. Maya didn't even know that her girlfriend was the kind of person who wrote down her thoughts in black and white - and kept everything «as a souvenir».
