Alone. Otherwise... I'll keep you..."

"There!"

- "I wish you could stay... But you have a life outside of me, cucciola..."

Maya's first day at the academy had been... strange.

At once exactly what she'd expected for years, and at the same time, deeply frustrating.

She had spent most of the day sitting in classrooms, listening, taking notes, observing. She had watched the other recruits run, carry, climb, sweat - while she had to content herself with standing back, her medical dispensation prominently displayed.

Her body kept reminding her why she was here - in this state. Every time she stood up too quickly, a dull pain shot through her belly. Every step she took in too much of a hurry brought a grimace she tried to hide. The painkillers helped, but they also left her in a slightly blurred state, as if she were looking at her own life through a glass window.

She'd held on. All day long.

Without complaint. Without going home early. But when she'd finally found herself alone in her room that evening, exhaustion had caught up with her all at once - a strange, almost humiliating exhaustion, for someone who was used to ending her days covered in sweat rather than drained by a single chair and a few steps down a corridor.

She had called Carina. She'd told her she was «fine». That wasn't entirely true. But it wasn't entirely untrue either.

The week followed the same wobbly rhythm. In the mornings, Maya got up slowly, with a persistent stiffness in her stomach - and sometimes in her shoulders. She swallowed her medication, put on her uniform and left, telling herself it was only temporary.

At the academy, she attended theory classes, took notes for herself and sometimes for others, watched training sessions from the sidelines. She already knew a lot, but she pretended to discover, as if that would make up for the fact that she couldn't participate.

The hardest part wasn't the pain. It was watching the others move forward while she stood still. Physically, there were small improvements. She walked a little straighter. She stood up a little faster. But some evenings, all it took was too much effort - too much walking, too long - for her body to give her a brutal reminder that she hadn't yet recovered. So she'd end up sitting on her bed, folded in half, breathing slowly, waiting for it to pass. Carina and her talked every day. She said she was managing. Carina would tell her to slow down. They pretended to meet in the middle.

Friday arrived too slowly - as always. And when it finally came, Maya realized something both simple and disturbing: she'd made it through the week... but she'd made it through gritted teeth.

When Carina had arrived by car, the blonde had been sitting outside, in front of the building. She had her bag with her and looked like a child going home for the weekend to wait for her parents.

The Italian had done her best not to jump out at her. But when Maya stood up, she ran her hands over her cheeks and came to kiss her. Intensely.

- "I've missed you so much..." Carina whispered.

- "You too, baby. So much."

The Italian had grabbed Maya's bag from the floor. Then she'd led the blonde to the car, holding her by the arm.

- "You're walking better!"

- "It's starting to get better."

- "I'm proud of you."

- "Because I'm walking better?"

- "Because you're paying attention."

They'd gone back to Lucia's that weekend. It was one floor, but at least it wasn't the three floors of her place. Besides, Andy still didn't know about the surgery.

Carina had spent her weekend lovingly looking after her girlfriend. Taking care of her. Preparing food for her. Making sure she didn't make any false moves - and on Monday, she'd dropped her off at the academy again.

The following Friday, Maya had taken an Uber from Tacoma Academy to Seattle Grace. She'd had that strange feeling of being late for something important, without really knowing for what. Her belly still tugged a little below the belt, but that wasn't what was occupying her. It was that lump, somewhere under his sternum. Not fear. Not yet. More like a heavy, indefinable expectation. In the waiting room, she'd stared at the clock too long. Then her phone. Then the clock again. She almost regretted telling Carina she wanted to do this alone. She knew she was somewhere in this hospital - but she also knew she was working. Now it was too late to change her mind, and ask her to come and hold her hand.

When the cabinet door had finally opened, Lucy Field had appeared.

- "Maya Bishop?"

She had risen at once.

- "Yes."

Dr. Field hadn't smiled at her... not her usual self. Not really. She'd just waved her in. The office was quiet. Too quiet. And Charlotte King was already there too. Maya had paused when she saw her. She had frowned slightly before collecting herself.

"Er... Hello..."

Lucy had closed the door behind her.

- "Settle down, Maya."

The tone was different. More composed. More... serious. And the blonde wasn't sure she liked the change of mood. Maya had sat down slowly, her hands resting on her thighs. She'd glanced from Lucy to Charlotte, then from Charlotte to Lucy.

- "It's... bad that there are two of you, isn't it?"

No one had laughed. Charlotte had sat across from her, hands folded on the desk. Not closed to discussion, but clearly ready for a speech.

Maya had nodded, as if talking about the weather. Doctors always had this habit of speaking with a vocabulary too complicated for the common mortal.

Too calm.

But it also means that it's not just a small isolated lesion - I insist on that. And it's still in the pelvic region. We're not dealing with a disease that's spread all over the body."

That's the sample that came back positive."

Silence had returned. Maya hadn't cried. She didn't even feel like she was really breathing. She was in that blur. In that moment when you wonder if you're dreaming - or awake. That vague fog you can sometimes feel when drugs are too strong. Then she had resumed:
