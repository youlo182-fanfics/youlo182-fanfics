By mid-afternoon on Friday, September 7th, Maya had walked through the doors of Seattle Grace with the same confident stride she used before a competition. The same head carriage. The same control mask.

Except here, there was no starting line, no stopwatch, no audience. Just the overly clean smell of the corridors, the muffled sound of the carts, and that strange sensation of entering a place where her body no longer quite belonged to her.

At reception, everything had been quick. Too quick. Her name. Her date of birth. A bracelet around her wrist.

Then a nurse led her to a room. A bright, impersonal room, with a too-well-made bed, too-white sheets, and a small rolling table on which already sat a folder with her name on it.

- "You need to take a shower with this product, it's Betadine. Then you'll have to change and put on the gown. Your surgeon will be in to see you in twenty minutes, then the anesthetist."

Maya had nodded, as if someone had just explained a training protocol to her.

When the door had closed, silence had taken over.

She'd stared at the gown for a long moment before coming to terms with it. She had taken off her clothes. Carefully folded what she was wearing. As if order could still give her the illusion of control. Then she'd gone to the shower, her skin and hair turning blood-red. Then she'd gone outside, dried herself with a disposable towel and put on her blouse - that blouse where her back was open.

When she'd sat down on the bed, reality had suddenly caught up with her. This wasn't a test. It wasn't a challenge. It wasn't another trial to be won.

It was an operation.

And for the first time in a long time, Maya had felt a pang of fear creep under her ribcage. But fortunately, someone had knocked on the door. Snapping her out of her thoughts. Or perhaps, preventing her from thinking too much.

- "Come in."

It wasn't a doctor. No. It was her lover.

Carina. She'd stood on the threshold for a second, as if afraid to disturb, then her gaze had fallen on Maya. On the hospital gown. On the bracelet. On the bed that suddenly made the situation all too real.

- "Hey..." she'd whispered.

Maya had sketched a smile.

- "See... I'm wise. I've prepared myself."

Carina had approached without answering. She had placed her bag on the chair - a sports bag containing clothes for Maya. Then she'd approached her lover, leaned towards her girlfriend. She'd wanted to caress her cheek, but she knew she'd had her disinfectant shower. She'd just come to kiss her on the lips. A chaste kiss. Then she'd come and sat on the edge of the bed. Her hand had found Maya's, clung to it a little harder than necessary.

- "Are you okay?" she'd asked, even though the question was almost pointless.

Maya had shrugged a shoulder.

- "As much as I can be."

She was trying to look light. Almost normal. But Carina knew her too well not to see the tension in her shoulders, the way her jaw tensed-when she thought no one was looking.

- "I'm staying with you until they take you away, okay?"

Maya had clenched her fingers.

- "I wanted to ask you... But I thought maybe you were going to work..."

- "You didn't even have to ask. Of course I was going to come. And I'd be here when you woke up..."

And for a few seconds, in that too-white, too-quiet room, they'd stayed like that. Just their hands tied. Just their breathing. As if they were both trying to scratch out a little more time before everything fell apart.

Then someone had knocked on the door again.

- "Come in."

Charlotte King had entered first, file under her arm, followed by Lucy Field. Two familiar figures. Two faces Maya knew too well - for all the wrong reasons.

Maya had blinked, then smirked.

"Wow..." she'd huffed.

"Is it because I'm a celebrity, or is it because it's really bad - my case, that I get two renowned surgeons?"

Lucy had rolled her eyes with a barely concealed sigh, but a corner of her mouth had betrayed a smile.

- "You have a sense of humor. That's a good sign in your case."

Charlotte, for her part, had set the file down on the rolling shelf.

- "We'll say it's because your case deserves to be done seriously and properly. No VIP treatment... just caution."

Carina, sitting on the edge of the bed, hadn't said anything. But her hand had tightened slightly around Maya's. She knew that tone. She knew that kind of joke. It was the one Maya used when she was scared.

Lucy had approached.

- "How are you feeling?"

- "Like before an Olympic final..."

Maya had shrugged a shoulder.

"I'm already thinking about the end... Except this time, I can't «wake up» by running faster."

Charlotte had taken over, calmer, more composed.

- "Let's go over this again quickly, shall we? The laparoscopy will allow us to see exactly what's going on. We'll remove the cyst, look at the extent of the lesions and take the necessary samples. The aim is both to relieve you - by removing certain adhesions... and to understand."

Maya had nodded, suddenly more serious.

- "And... it's under general anesthetic?"

- "Yes." replied Lucy.

"The anesthesiologist will be by to see you in a few minutes. You'll be completely asleep. You won't see, feel, hear anything."

Maya had exhaled slowly.

- "I don't really like the idea of... not being in charge anymore..."

Carina had looked up at her.

- "For once, you don't have to control everything, Bambina."

Maya had offered her a small smile, more fragile than her previous ones.

- "After six years, I think you know me..."

Dr. King had closed the folder.

- "We'll take good care of you, Maya. Both of us."

Dr. Field had nodded.

- "And I'll be here when you wake up."

A silence had fallen. Not heavy. But charged with all that was left unsaid.

Carina had finally asked, her voice a little lower:

- "How long before they pick her up?"

- "About twenty minutes," Charlotte replied.

"Just long enough for the anesthesiologist to come by and for us to do the final checks."

The blonde had looked at the brunette.

- "You're staying, aren't you?"

- "I'm not moving, amore." the Italian had replied without hesitation.

Lucy had given Maya one last look.

- "Try to relax. And... avoid trying to control everything - or convince everyone in the operating room. We're all here to take great care of you. No need to play the strong woman, the one who won an Olympic medal during your endometriosis crisis."

- "Promise."

Maya had sketched a smile.

- "Although... I could take selfies and sign autographs.... It would lighten the mood..."

The two doctors had looked at each other as if to say «with the way she's taking her situation, this isn't going to be easy».

By the time the two doctors had left, the room was calm again. Carina had moved closer to the bed. She had slid her hand over Maya's cheek.

- "You don't have to be strong with me."

The blonde had closed her eyes for a second, then rested her forehead against her girlfriend's.

- "I know."

A breath had left her.

"But I'm a little scared anyway."

Carina had taken her in her arms, gently, as if afraid of breaking her.

- "Lo so, bambina and that's normal. But you're not alone."

They stayed like that, for a few more minutes. Counting breaths. Delaying, as long as possible, the moment when someone would come knocking to take her to surgery.

But that moment had arrived, and someone had knocked gently on the door.

- "Come in." Maya said, rising from her lover's arms.

A man in scrubs had entered, accompanied by a nurse. He had a file in his hand and a professional, reassuring smile.

- "Hello, Maya. I'm Dr. Harris, the anesthesiologist. We're going to have a little chat before the procedure, okay?"

Maya had nodded.

- "Enjoy it now because afterwards I might be a bit groggy..."

Carina had sketched a smile, but her hand hadn't left Maya's. She was really nervous to start making lame jokes like that. The anesthesiologist had glanced at the chart.

- "Any known allergies?"

- "No. But I don't really like tomatoes."

The nurse had laughed lightly and Carina had made big eyes at Maya - squeezing her fingers. As if to tell her to «stop it!».

- "Any history with general anesthesia?"

- "I've never been completely asleep."

Maya had shrugged a shoulder.

"This is a first."

- "Okay. The principle is simple: we'll put you completely to sleep, you won't feel a thing, and you'll wake up in the recovery room after the procedure. It lasts a few seconds at the time of the injection, and then... black hole."

- "Am I going to do it like in the movies? I'll count backwards, but in reality I'll never reach zero?"

- "Something like that." Had replied the anesthesiologist, trying not to show that he heard this at least three times a day.

Carina had tilted her head, falsely serious.

- "So in a nutshell... this is the first time you're going to dope legally?"

Maya had turned her head toward her, eyebrows raised.

- "Seriously?"

- "Cosa?"

Carina had shrugged.

"Olympic champion, banned substances... There at least, it's prescribed by a doctor. That's a big first in your career."

- "I've never doped!"

The doctor let out a small laugh.

- "I reassure you, we're a long way from sports doping. But technically... yes, we're going to inject you with something quite powerful."

Maya had smiled awkwardly.

- "Hey, it's for your health, baby. It's to heal you."

- "Sai di cosa ho bisogno «per curarmi»" [You know what I need «to cure myself»].

Carina had squeezed her hand a little tighter, her cheeks turning red. She knew exactly what Maya was talking about.

The anesthetist had resumed, more seriously:

- "Have you had anything to eat or drink today?"

- "No. I got up in the middle of the night on purpose, but I'm on a twelve-hour fast.... Besides, try making Luc Léger on an empty stomach..."

- "Perfect. We'll start you on a drip, if you haven't already, and I'll drop by again just before I take you into surgery. You might feel a little warm, a little dizzy... and then you'll sleep."

Maya had swallowed.

- "You tell me if I've reached zero.... I'm very good at not listening to my body... So I might manage to counteract the effects of the anesthetic."

- "I'd double the dosage! But on an empty stomach... I think you'll fall asleep at eight with the cocktail I've prepared for you."

Carina had laughed lightly.

- "Traitor!" Maya whispered to her lover.

The anesthetist had closed her file.

- "You're going to be fine. We'll take care of you."

He'd nodded to the nurse, then they'd gone out again, leaving the room in an eerie calm once more.

- "It scares me a little to entrust my conscience to a syringe.... I saw the film Awake, you know. Some people are conscious, others feel everything..."

Maya lay back on the bed in a semi-sitting position and stared at the ceiling for a few seconds. Carina had risen from the mattress. She had approached her girlfriend and pressed her forehead to hers.

- "You don't entrust your conscience to a syringe. You entrust it to a really good team. And..."

She'd brushed her thumb across his cheek.

"And most importantly, it's just a movie."

Maya had closed her eyes for a moment.

- "Yes, but it can still happen... And if having... «it» makes me understand something, it's that it doesn't just happen to other people..."

- "I'll be here when you wake up..."

- "Promise?"

- "Promise."

She'd kissed her softly, for a long time, as if to etch this moment - just before everything stopped... and started all over again differently.

There had been another knock on the door, this time more firmly.

- "Maya Bishop?"

Two nurses had entered with a stretcher. One had a chart in his hand, the other was already gently pushing the gurney into the room.

"It's time."

The word had sounded louder than anything else.

Maya had turned her head towards Carina. For a second, her smile had wavered.

- "Wow... You really don't waste any time, do you."

- "Let's get you to the OR," the nurse had said gently.

"You can stay with her until we get to the hallway." He pointed out to the brunette.

Carina had immediately stood up, as if her body had reacted before her head. She grabbed Maya's hand and gave it a firm squeeze.

- "It's okay, baby bear."

Maya had nodded.

- "I know."

Then, in that slightly too light tone she'd added:

"Frankly, I've been in more stressful competitions... Here, at least, I don't have to do anything." Maya had tried to be reassuring.

- "That's exactly what's scaring you," Carina had murmured. To prove that she knew her.

Maya had smirked.

- "A little, yeah."

The nurses had begun to prepare for the transfer. One had locked the bed, the other had checked the IV.

- "Let's take it slow, shall we?"

They had helped her slide onto the stretcher. The sheet had been pulled up over her - like a too-thin barrier against whatever lay ahead.

Carina had walked beside her as they exited the room. The Seattle Grace corridor was too bright, too white, too quiet. And Maya watched the ceiling scroll above her.

- "You know..."

She'd turned her head toward Carina.

"I'm glad you came. I know I always play the strong woman... But I'll always need you. You're my whole life, Carina DeLuca."

Carina had smiled a small, trembling smile.

- "Per l'éternita..." she sighed to keep from bursting into tears.

They had arrived in front of the block's doors. The air seemed colder, more serious. More definitive. One of the nurses had stopped.

- "This is where we part ways..."

Carina had felt her throat tighten.

- "I can..."

She had searched for her words.

"Can I stay just a minute longer?"

- "One minute." the nurse had replied, nodding.

Carina had leaned over the stretcher. She'd placed both hands on Maya's cheeks, forcing her to look at her.

- "You're coming back. Can you hear me? You're coming back to me. I'll be here."

Maya had swallowed.

- "I think if there's anything sure between us.... It's that we always come back to each other..."

- "I'll wait for you here - or in your room..." Carina replied with tears in her eyes.

- "I want your face to be the first thing I see when I wake up... It will be the most beautiful sight..."

They kissed. Not a hurried kiss. Not a passionate kiss. A kiss that said «come back, I'm waiting for you, I love you.»

Carina had rested her forehead against Maya's.

- "I love you. More than my own life."

- "I love you bigger than the whole universe..."

Then the nurses had gently taken over the stretcher.

- "We'll take it from here."

Carina had stood there, motionless, while Maya disappeared behind the swinging doors of the operating theater.

The doors had closed with a muffled sound. And for the first time in a long time, Carina found herself alone with her fear. Alone with this silence that was too clean, too white, too calm.

She sat on a plastic chair, her hands clasped together, her eyes fixed on a clock that seemed to have decided to torture her. Every minute passed too slowly. Every second was too long.

She looked at her phone. Then put it down. Then picked it up again. Then put it back again. To breathe. Just breathe. That was all she had to do. It was all she had to do.

In the operating room, the light was harsh, almost unreal. Maya was already asleep, motionless under the sterile sheets. The anesthetist had finished his checks and stepped aside. Charlotte King and Lucy Field faced each other, concentrating.

- "We're going for an exploratory laparoscopy with cyst removal and adhesion release," Charlotte had said calmly.

- "Yes," Lucy had replied.

"But I've got a bad feeling about this... When she came to see me a month ago, the endometriosis had progressed... You have to expect anything... Maya is a warrior... She prefers to suffer in silence."

The first gestures had been precise, mechanical. The screen lit up, revealing the interior.

And very quickly, the silence had changed its nature.

- "... Damn it." Charlotte had murmured.

Lucy had moved closer to the screen.

- "They're everywhere..."

She'd clenched her jaw.

"A lot more than last time."

- "In a month..." Charlotte had huffed.

"It shouldn't have developed so quickly."

They had continued, methodical. But the further they went, the more cautious their movements became.

"Look at that..." Charlotte had said, pointing at the screen.

Lucy had squinted.

- "The intestines..."

She had paused:

"It's not pretty."

- "She must be in tremendous pain," Charlotte had blurted out in a low voice, eyebrows furrowed.

- "And yet she claims to be fine." had replied Lucy, bitterly.

"She's forcing it. She always does."

In the corridor, Carina had stood up for the third time in less than ten minutes. She'd taken a few steps, then returned to her seat. She looked at the clock. It had already been longer than she'd been told. Much longer. She had crossed her arms over her chest, as if to stop herself from trembling. Just then, Lucia had appeared at the end of the corridor.

- "Tesoro."

The brunette had raised her head immediately, as if she'd been waiting for that precise face.

- "Mamma..."

Her voice had betrayed something. A little too much. Lucia had sat down beside her, without asking any unnecessary questions. She'd just placed a hand on her forearm.

- "Ci vuole più tempo del previsto?" [Is it taking longer than expected?]

Carina had nodded.

- "Sì... E lo detesto." [Yes... And I hate it.]

- "Gli interventi chirurgici non seguono mai proprio gli orari." [Surgeries never really respect the clock.] Lucia had murmured softly.

"Ma stanno facendo quello che devono. Se non è ancora finita, è perché si stanno prendendo cura della tua ragazza." [But they're doing the right thing. If it's not over, it's because they're taking good care of your lover].

Carina had closed her eyes for a second.

- "Ho paura." [I'm afraid.]

Lucia had squeezed her arm a little tighter.

- "È normale, Tesoro" [It's normal, Tesoro].

They stayed like that for a few minutes. Then Lucia had to go back to work. And Carina was left alone again. With the clock. That damned clock.

Back in the OR, the atmosphere had grown heavier.

- "The cyst is there," Charlotte had said.

- "We're removing it," Lucy had replied.

"And we're cleaning as much as we can."

They worked in silence for a while. Then Charlotte froze.

- "Doctor Field... Do you see this?"

Lucy had leaned over.

- "Yes..."

She'd paused briefly.

"That's not good."

- "It looks like..."

Charlotte hadn't finished her sentence.

- "Yes... I'm afraid so..." Lucy had cut in.

"I'm seeing the same thing you are."

They'd looked at each other over the masks.

- "We can't make this decision without further tests." Charlotte said cautiously.

- "I know." Lucy had replied. Then, lower down, she'd added:

"But I work with her girlfriend. And her mother-in-law. I can't see this... and pretend it never happened."

- "Doctor Field..."

- "I take responsibility." She made - her gaze determined.

"We'll take out what we can now. I'll tell her then."

There had been a tense silence in the operating room. The two surgeons stared at each other blankly. The anesthetist, the nurses, no one dared speak. Then Charlotte had nodded.

- "All right, then. Do it."

They resumed.

"Her adhesions..." murmured Charlotte.

"It's impressive that she's holding up."

- "She's not holding up," Lucy had replied.

"She survives by gritting her teeth... And by lying to herself."

In the corridor, Carina had gasped as a nurse passed by. Her heart was beating too fast. She looked at the clock. Again. It was far too long.

She'd typed a message and deleted it. Then another. Also deleted. It wasn't as if she could send a message to Andy, or even Mason, or Andrea, or even Vic or Taryn, or Jo.

Because nobody knew. The people who knew were in this hospital... Her mother, Maya and her surgeons - and her, of course.

She'd finally dropped her phone into her bag and buried her face in her hands.

- "Torna, amore mio..." [Come back, amore mio...] she had whispered to herself.

In the OR, concentration was at a peak.

- "We've removed the cyst," Charlotte had announced.

- "And a good part of the adhesions." had added Lucy.

"Not all of them.

But the maximum without doing more damage."

They had exchanged a look heavy with meaning.

- "It wasn't a simple surgery."

- "No." Lucy had confirmed.

"And it clearly wasn't just «run-of-the-mill endometriosis»."

She'd blown into her mask.

"She's going to wake up in recovery room."

- "And then..." Charlotte had exhaled slowly.

"After that, we're going to have to talk."

And in the corridor, Carina was still waiting. Unaware that, behind those doors, something had just changed.

The Italian had risen to her feet at the slightest movement at the end of the corridor. When she saw Dr. Lucy Field appear in her scrubs, her heart leapt into her throat. For a split second, she'd been afraid she'd read something terrible on her face.

Lucy had approached her, removed her cap, then sketched a tired little smile.

- "It's over."

The breath Carina had been holding for hours had finally escaped.

- "Is she... Is she all right?" she asked at once, her voice trembling.

- "Yes." replied Lucy without hesitation.

"She's stable. She's in recovery right now. The anesthesia is slowly wearing off."

Carina had brought a hand to her mouth, her eyes shining.

- "Thank you... Madonna, thank you..."

Lucy had watched her for a moment, with that professional gentleness she had when she had to remain both human and doctor.

- "Carina... The operation took a little longer than expected." she added cautiously.

"But Maya coped well with the surgery. Vitals are good."

The brunette had nodded, trying to read between the lines.

- "Can I see her?"

Lucy had tilted her head slightly. A slight smile on her lips. The love they had for each other was visible a mile away.

- "Not just yet. The recovery room is very supervised. We're waiting until she's wide awake and can be taken back up to her room. As soon as that's done, we'll come and get you."

There had been a silence. Carina had wanted to tell her that Maya hoped to see her face first when she woke up. But she hadn't dared. She too wanted to become an OB-GYN. She knew there were rules.

Carina had clasped her hands together.

- "She... she'll be in pain?"

Lucy had chosen her words.

- "She's still under the effects of anesthesia and painkillers.

She'll be watched closely. We'll manage her pain, okay?"

It wasn't a real answer. And Carina understood that.

- "Okay..." she murmured.

Lucy had placed a brief hand on her arm.

- "I know it's hard to wait. But the most important thing is that the operation is over and she's okay."

She had paused, then added, more gently:

"She's going to ask you as soon as she opens her eyes."

Carina's lips had trembled in a fragile smile.

- "She'd better!" She made a joke, to keep herself from crying.

Lucy had nodded - a smile on her lips, then straightened up, becoming a little more formal again.

- "We'll come and get you as soon as she's settled in her room."

Carina had watched her walk away, then slowly sat back in her chair - biting her lip.

The world had been a blur at first. Heavy. Cottony. Maya felt as if she'd just surfaced after being underwater too long. There were distant beeps, muffled voices, a strange sensation in her belly... and above all this immense fatigue sticking to her bones. She had tried to open her eyes. But it had been too difficult. So she had simply frowned.

"Maya?" a soft voice had asked.

"The operation's over. Everything went smoothly. You're in recovery."

Maya had moved her lips. Her throat was dry.

Her voice was a mere murmur.

- "... Carina?"

Lucy had smiled, relieved to see her conscious.

- "She's here. She's waiting for you. We'll take you back up to your room and go get her, okay?"

Maya had half-opened her eyes, pupils still lost.

- "Carina..." she'd repeated, more insistently, as if it were the only thing that really mattered.

Lucy had leaned a little closer to her.

- "Yes, Maya. I'll look for her. I promise I will. But first, let's get you comfortable."

Maya had shaken her head very slightly, a crease of anxiety between her eyebrows.

- "Carina..." she'd practically moaned, her throat and whole body aching.

There was something almost childlike in her voice. Something fragile. As if, until she saw it, nothing was really over. Lucy had then exchanged a look with the nurse.

- "Here we go. Let's get her upstairs."

The journey to the room had seemed interminable for Maya, even if, in reality, she was slipping back into a semi-somnolence at times. But every time she reopened her eyes, it was the same word that came back, obstinate:

- "Carina..."

When at last the bed had been set up in the room, the nurses had bustled around her, adjusting the drips, the monitors. Then the door opened. The Italian had entered almost too quickly. It was as if she'd run the length of the corridor. For a second, she froze, seeing Maya in that bed, pale, still a little lost. Then she'd rushed over to her.

- "Bambina..."

Her hands had slid over Maya's cheeks, with infinite caution, as if she'd feared she'd disappear, if she touched her too hard. The blonde had opened her eyes. And when she'd seen Carina, a trembling breath had escaped her lips.

- "There you are..." a tear had fallen from one of her eyes, to fall on her girlfriend's hand.

Carina had leaned over and kissed it. A soft, trembling kiss, full of restraint and everything she hadn't dared say during those hours of waiting. Then she'd rested her forehead against Maya's, her eyes shining.

- "It was too long..." she murmured.

"I was so scared... I've been waiting for you for hours... it was longer than a week at the academy..."

Maya had sketched a tired little smile.

- "I told you I was fine..."

- "You always lie when you say that..." breathed Carina, her voice broken.

She'd left her forehead pressed against his, her hands still framing her face.

"I love you..." She said simply.

"You scared me so much..."

Maya had raised a hand, a little heavy, and rested it against Carina's cheek.

- "I'm here..." she murmured.

"It's you I wanted to see first..."

They had stayed like that for a few seconds. The world reduced to their mingled breaths. To that contact. To this simple certainty: they were together again, on the same side of fear.

Then, slowly, the door opened again. Lucy Field and Charlotte King had opened the door, giving the two women a few more seconds to speak. When they came in more openly, Carina didn't move away from the bed. She'd stayed there, one hand resting on Maya's, as if letting go could still make her disappear.

- "How did it go?" had asked Carina, her voice still a little shaky.

Lucy had taken a calm, professional breath.

- "The operation's over and Maya's woken up well, which is already a very good thing."

She had looked at Maya, then given her a reassuring little smile.

"We removed the cyst and treated as many adhesions as possible. The operation took a little longer than expected, but everything went smoothly with no immediate complications."

Maya had blinked slowly.

- "So... I'm whole?" she'd murmured, with a slightly tired attempt at humor.

Charlotte had sketched a discreet smile.

- "Yes. And tired. Very tired. You'll especially need to rest now."

Carina had squeezed Maya's hand a little tighter.

- "Is she going to be sore?"

- "There will be pain, yes," Lucy had answered honestly.

"But they'll be taken care of. And above all, the most important thing is that she recovers quietly. We'll come and see you later to review, okay?"

Maya had nodded weakly.

- "As long as..." She'd turned her eyes to Carina.

"As long as she can stay there..."

- "I'm staying, amore." had replied Carina at once, leaning over to place a kiss on her forehead.

Charlotte had jotted something down in the folder, then closed it gently.

- "We'll let you get some rest. If you need anything, just call."

Lucy had given Maya one last look, then Carina.

- "You get some rest. I'll be back later."

They left the room in silence. In the hallway, the door had closed behind them with a soft click. Charlotte had stopped, then turned to Lucy, her face suddenly much more serious than in the bedroom.

- "Why didn't you tell her about what we took out..."

Lucy had clenched her jaws for a moment before answering.

- "Because I got to know Maya..."

She'd sighed, running a hand over her face.

"She's a skeptic. She always needs proof. Numbers. Results."

Charlotte had crossed her arms.

- "But you know very well it wasn't trivial."

- "Precisely." Lucy had replied, her gaze hard:

"I want the anapath results. I want it in black and white before I tell her anything. I'm not going to let her panic over an assumption."

She had paused.

"And I can't leave her two weeks in the dark without certainty either. Not her. Not like this."

Charlotte had nodded slowly.

- "So we wait for the results?"

- "Yes." Lucy had confirmed.

Then, lower down she'd added:

"And we're hoping that what we saw isn't what it looked like."

They had remained silent for a moment in the corridor, each with her own thoughts. While behind the door, Maya slept. And Carina kept watch.
