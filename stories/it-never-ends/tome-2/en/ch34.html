The next morning, Carina woke up with a strange feeling. Not unpleasant, but different. As if the San Francisco air allowed her to breathe a little more freely. She hadn't dreamt about Maya last night. It was an insignificant detail, yet she'd dwelt on it for a moment before banishing the thought.

After a quick shower, she had joined Jackson in the lobby of their hotel. He was already ready, looking delighted.

- "You're going to love what I've planned for this morning," he said with a knowing smile.

They had headed for Chinatown, one of San Francisco's liveliest and most authentic neighborhoods. As soon as they arrived, the smell of spices and steaming tea enveloped them, instantly immersing them in a timeless atmosphere. Signs with golden lettering shone in the morning sun, and the red lanterns hanging between the buildings added an almost cinematic touch.

Jackson had led them to an address he'd carefully scouted: a small restaurant renowned for its dim sum. The interior was bustling, with families and regulars sharing bamboo baskets filled with steaming morsels.

"Trust me, it's all good here," Jackson assured as he ordered a varied selection.

Carina had allowed herself to be surprised by the flavors, appreciating the sweetness of the har gow, the richness of the baozi, and the delicacy of the xiao long bao that let out a scorching broth with every bite.

- "you know Maya loves Asian food?" she blurted out without really thinking.

Jackson raised an eyebrow but made no comment. He'd merely smiled indulgently, before raising his teacup.

- "To our culinary explorations." he said simply.

Carina had bitten the inside of her cheek. Why did she always have to bring Maya back into the conversation?

After breakfast, they had purposely lost themselves in the bustling alleys of Chinatown, exploring traditional stores filled with delicate porcelain, rare teas and carefully hand-drawn calligraphy.

Their next destination was Lombard Street, the famous zigzag street. From the top, the view was impressive, the sharp bends forming an almost unreal layout between the flower-filled houses.

- "are you ready to test the descent?" Carina had asked with a smirk.

- "If you want we can even do it on a skateboard!" replied Jackson with a laugh.

- "uh... in the car will do this time!"

The ride had been as fun as it had been unexpected, the twists and turns making driving almost playful. Once at the bottom, they continued their exploration as far as Fisherman's Wharf and Pier 39.

Here, the atmosphere was totally different. The smell of grilled seafood wafted through the air, mingled with the murmur of tourists and the cries of seagulls. But what immediately caught their attention were the sea lions stranded on the floating pontoons, stretching lazily in the sun or vying for places with raucous growls.

- "Look at that one, it looks like me after a heavy meal." joked Jackson, pointing to a sea lion slumped on its side.

Carina laughed outright. She loved this lightness, this simplicity in their exchanges.

But her smile had faded slightly when she felt her phone vibrate in her pocket. She hesitated before pulling it out.

It was one of Maya's Instagram stories. She rarely posted, and she'd felt a small pang of disappointment when she saw that it wasn't her face, or even something that might have reminded her of her, like she had when she'd posted the Little Bear.

Still, her heart had missed a beat. She'd stared at the screen for a moment before tucking her phone away in her pocket.

Jackson hadn't asked why she received a notification when the blonde posted.

To take their minds off things, they'd decided to take a boat ride around the bay. The sea air whipped at their faces as the ferry pulled away from the harbor, offering a breathtaking view of the San Francisco skyline. The passage near Alcatraz had added a touch of mystery to the trip, as the abandoned prison seemed to float in the mist, isolated from the rest of the world.

Carina leaned against the rail, letting the wind blow through her hair.

"Feels good, doesn't it?" breathed Jackson beside her.

She nodded. Yes, it felt good.

That evening, they'd booked a table at a restaurant with a panoramic view of the bay.

The atmosphere was subdued, the dimmed lights giving way to the golden glow of the sunset reflected on the water. Carina had stared at the horizon for a moment, absorbed in the beauty of the moment.

"You realize we're almost done with our road trip?" remarked Jackson, taking a sip of wine.

- "already?"

- "yeah. And I have to say, I really like the version of you I've been seeing these past few days."

She'd looked at him, intrigued.

- "What do you mean?"

- "You're lighter. Like... you're learning to breathe again."

Carina had looked down, playing with the stem of her glass. Was this for real? Was she really moving forward, or was she just pretending?

But watching Jackson, the genuine smile on his face, the softness in his gaze, she'd caught herself thinking she didn't want to be anywhere else. Not tonight.

So she'd raised her glass in turn, agreeing to let herself be carried along a little longer.

- "To lightness, then."

And for the first time in a long time, she'd almost believed it.

The next morning, the air was fresher, laden with that typical San Francisco haze that made the horizon blurred, almost unreal. Carina and Jackson had boarded the ferry bound for Alcatraz, the prison island that had seen some of the most feared criminals in American history.

The boat ride offered a breathtaking view of the San Francisco skyline, but Carina was elsewhere. Sitting by the rail, her phone in her hands, she was mechanically scrolling through Instagram when a photo caught her eye.

It was Jo who had posted it in a publication of several photos. But in the one that had appeared first, Maya was alone, and the shot had probably been taken after a workout. Her blond hair had started to grow back since she'd cut it to a bob, now forming a soft, unkempt length that suited her perfectly. She was wearing the tight tank top she usually wore for running, her skin still glistening with effort, her gaze turned skyward, as if caught in a moment of reflection. Or scanning the clouds, as they had once caught themselves doing together, for funny shapes.

Carina felt her heart clench. She was too beautiful. Always too beautiful.

Her fingers had grazed the screen, as if to capture something intangible, a presence that eluded her. She would have liked to see those blonde locks more closely, to slide them through her fingers as before. She would have liked to hear her grumble about how tired she was after training, to tease her by stealing a sip of her drink. She missed her. Terribly.

A silence had settled over her, an emptiness that wasn't being filled by anything, despite all she was striving to live.

She had slowly looked up at Jackson, sitting across from her, arms crossed, gazing out over the water. He had finally realized her observation and had given her a slight smile.

Carina had forced herself to return it. It was getting easier every day, because he was there. Because he offered her this stable presence, this benevolence without pressure.

But when he was an hour's drive away... would the proverbial « out of sight, out of mind » still hold true?

She'd sighed softly and locked her phone.

- "Ready to visit the world's most famous prison?" asked Jackson, handing her a headset for the immersive tour.

Carina had nodded and stood up. It was too early for answers.

After a quick lunch on the harbor, where they'd enjoyed typical local crab sandwiches, Jackson had insisted on an activity he considered a must: the Cable Car.

"We ride outside, otherwise it doesn't count!" he declared adamantly.

Carina raised an eyebrow but didn't protest. A few minutes later, she found herself clinging to the streetcar's outer railing, the wind lifting her hair, as the Cable Car climbed a steeply inclined street.

- "do you realize it would have been so much easier to take an Uber?" she chuckled, trying not to let go at a particularly sharp turn.

- "That's not the point, Carina! It's the experience that counts!"

She hadn't been able to stop laughing. It was true, it was fun. Seeing the city go by like that, feeling the fresh air on your face, letting yourself be carried along without thinking...

The streetcar had dropped them off at Nob Hill, a chic residential district with streets lined with elegant Victorian houses. They strolled around for a while, stopping at a cosy café where Carina enjoyed a cappuccino, gazing at the sloping streets through the bay window.

She was feeling good. A little lighter.

That evening, they decided to dine in a livelier neighborhood: the Mission District. The place vibrated with a different energy, with its brightly colored murals, laid-back atmosphere and omnipresent Latin influences.

"Is it just me, or are you starting to appreciate California as much as Sicily?" teased Jackson as they settled into a small Mexican restaurant.

- "You shouldn't exaggerate either." she replied with a smile.

She'd raised her glass to her lips and let the tangy flavor of her Virgin margarita titillate her palate.

The meal had been pleasant, punctuated by jokes and moments of sincere complicity. Jackson knew exactly how to make her laugh, how to offer her moments when she could forget, if only for a few hours, the weight of her heart.

After dinner, they'd hesitated to go straight home.

- "Shall we have a nightcap at a rooftop bar?" offered Jackson.

- "Why not."

A few minutes later, they'd found themselves high up, cocktail in hand, watching the lights of San Francisco twinkle before their eyes.

Carina had stared at the illuminated skyscrapers for a moment, lost in thought.

In three days' time, they would finally be approaching Berkeley. Another chapter was about to be turned.

The morning mist clung to the cliffs of Lands End, enveloping the coastal path in a hushed atmosphere. Carina advanced at a slow pace, her gaze lost on the infinite expanse of the Pacific, revealed between the wind-twisted cypress trees.

But she wasn't there. Not really.

Maya.

Carina had resumed her walk, trying to concentrate on the raw beauty of the landscape. But even here, Maya was everywhere.

She could easily imagine her here, running along this path, stopping to look out over the ocean, her hair a mess, her eyes shining.

She had briefly closed her eyes and inhaled deeply. But the pain hadn't evaporated.

After a coffee break at the Sutro Baths, where the waves crashed against the forgotten ruins, they had headed for Golden Gate Park.

The place was vast, lush, a bubble of calm in the heart of the city. Jackson had offered to rent bikes, and Carina had accepted, hoping that the movement, the fresh air, would help her chase away the shadow that had followed her since morning. But even as she pedaled, she couldn't get Maya out of her head.

Carina had stopped near the Japanese garden, leaving her bike against a railing. She wished she'd been there with her. She wished she could turn her head and see Maya smiling, hear her remark on the calm of the place, feel her brush her hand without even thinking about it.

They'd ended the day at Baker Beach, where the sun was slowly beginning its descent behind the Golden Gate.

With her feet in the cold sand, Carina had watched the water, lost in silent contemplation.

Jackson had settled down beside her, without speaking. He understood that she needed space. The wind played with her hair, and she'd shivered slightly.

It was ironic.

She'd come to California to find herself, to move on, to heal. But tonight, faced with the immensity of the sky and the ocean, she felt as if she'd come full circle back to the same point.

Maya. Always Maya.

She had raised her Waterdrop gourd to her lips, trying to put out the fire in her throat.

« Out of sight, out of mind. »

Tonight, she didn't believe it at all.

The Haight-Ashbury district was slowly awakening under a pale sun, bathing the colorful facades of the Victorian houses in a soft light. The air still carried the scent of freshly-brewed coffee and wilted flowers forgotten on porches.

Carina and Jackson were seated at a terrace table in a café that looked like a bohemian hangout, surrounded by hanging plants and framed vinyl records on the walls.

Jackson had chosen an oat milk latte, Carina had opted for a simple espresso, she needed something that would bring her back to reality. To her life before all this. To Sicily.

But today, she was elsewhere. She stirred her coffee absent-mindedly, gazing at the busy street ahead. Because she'd done it. She had looked for Maya.

This morning, as the sun barely filtered through the curtains of her hotel room, she'd opened Instagram, still numb with sleep. She'd scanned Jo's stories, Vic's stories, Taryn's stories. She'd been watching for a sign, an image, a fragment of life that would tell her where Maya was, how she was doing, what she was doing.

But nothing. Nothing, except that damn absence that weighed more and more.

Jackson put down his phone and gave her a sidelong glance.

- "You seem out of it this morning..."

She'd shrugged, sketching what she hoped was a convincing smile.

- "I'm enjoying the quiet, we've got nothing this morning..."

Carina had taken a sip of her espresso, bitter and burning.

They'd spent the afternoon wandering around San Francisco's less-frequented streets, with no specific agenda in mind.

In Clarion Alley, Carina had stopped in front of a fresco bursting with color, a mixture of abstract shapes and looks frozen in paint, imbued with raw emotion.

She felt a shiver run down her spine. Maya would have loved this place. She'd probably have taken a photo, shared an anecdote about a street artist, found a detail no one would have noticed.

The wind had caressed his skin as they'd reached the top of Twin Peaks, the illuminated city stretching out beneath them like an ocean of moving lights.

Jackson had inhaled deeply, hands in his pockets, savoring the moment.

- "it's crazy how quiet everything looks from up here."

Carina had closed her eyes for a moment, taking a deep breath.

Today, she wanted to be here. Completely. No distractions, no thoughts to tear her away from the present moment. Just her, Jackson, and this town that was about to become practically home for the next four years. After all, she'd only be twenty minutes away.

Carina slipped her arm under Jackson's, and he gently squeezed his hand.

Tomorrow, they would leave for Berkeley. And tomorrow... everything would really fall into place, everything would become real. Not Seattle's Washington University, but Berkeley. Not Maya, but Jackson. Tomorrow, everything she'd tried to put off would become real.
