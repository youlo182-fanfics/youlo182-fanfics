Carina's awakening had been slow, lulled by the sounds of the campus waking up behind her half-open window.

Her first morning alone at Berkeley.

She stretched for a long moment before glancing around. Her roommate would probably be arriving today or in the next few days. Her room was already looking a little more like home, thanks to the boxes Jackson had brought.

She'd smiled when she thought of him, a few yards away in his hotel. She'd see him tonight.

But today, it was time to immerse herself in her new reality.

Berkeley wasn't just a university. It was a city within a city, an eclectic mix of neo-classical and modern architecture, vast lawns where sophomores and juniors were already studying in small groups, lively cafés and imposing libraries.

Carina let her instincts guide her, strolling between buildings, noting the names of lecture halls and classrooms.

She passed the Sather Tower, the famous carillon that dominated the campus, and paused for a moment to observe the hurried students who seemed already accustomed to this environment.

She, on the other hand, still felt a stranger to the place. But it was only a matter of time.

Between August twenty-first and twenty-eighth, students had the opportunity to explore certain classes, attending introductory sessions to familiarize themselves with the program before the official start of the semester.

Carina, curious as she was, chose to take advantage of this opportunity and really make the right choices for her.

Her first stop was an art history class, where she sat discreetly at the back of the amphitheatre.

The professor, a man with greying hair and a steady voice, was talking passionately about the Italian Renaissance.

The brunette felt a thrill of excitement. This was what she was here for. To learn. To evolve. Admittedly, it had nothing to do with medicine, but she'd thought it would be nice to have a little escape from anatomy and medical courses.

For an hour, she had forgotten everything else, listening to this man proudly pronounce the names of Italian buildings and artists.

And after one last look, he'd climbed into the car, started it up, and disappeared down the road.

Carina had stood there for a moment. Alone.

This time, it was official. Her new life could truly begin.

The day had passed without her having time to think about the emptiness. She had attended a few trial classes, had let herself get carried away by the rhythm of the campus.

But that evening, when she was alone in her room because her roommate had already left to explore the first student parties, an idea had crossed her mind.

Maya was turning eighteen in three days. Her heart clenched, she wasn't going to be there for her. She would never be there for her again, as she would like, but she would at least have wanted to be there for her birthday party.

She wanted it to be perfect, because even if Maya never read it as she'd hoped, she wanted her to feel every word.

I hope that one day you'll forgive me for my cowardice.

A te per sempre e l'eternità, baby Bear.

PS: I hope this soccer jersey will be an eighteenth birthday present worthy of yours...

The next afternoon, Carina had walked to the post office, the envelope tucked neatly into her bag.

Inside: a carefully folded soccer jersey and the letter, finally completed.

She wrote down the address, which she knew by heart, and felt a wave of emotion run through her.

On August twenty-sixth, Maya would receive this package, and even if Carina wasn't there to hand-deliver it, she'd be with her.

The brunette had wandered aimlessly through the streets around campus. She needed a moment alone, with no schedule, no obligations.

When she'd finally found a patch of grass on campus, in the shade of a tree, she'd settled there, reclining against the huge tone, and watched the sky.

The silence had done her good, until her fingers brushed against her phone. Without much thought, she'd gone to her photo folder. She didn't even know why she was doing it. Maybe to find something.

Jo, Jack, Taryn, Ryan, Vic, Andy, Dean, Travis, Jackson... And all the others. And of course, Maya. A beautiful year, despite everything. Despite the chaos caused by her father, despite the doubts, despite the distance.

But also snapshots of her stay in Sicily, surrounded by her brother, her cousin Graziella, Gabriella, her cousins, her friends from the international high school and others. Each publication was a little piece of her story.

But the last one... It was different. Firstly, it was the only publication to contain a single photo, and secondly, it was a bit of a symbol. The last publication, the one that closed all the others. The one that concluded this year, but also the one she wanted to bring to this new adventure that was university.

Carina had taken the photo of her necklace. The one she'd sent to Maya the morning after she'd received it as a gift. That infinity necklace, the one Maya had given her. No one knew where it came from; they thought she'd bought it during her stay. Only Maya, Andrea, Graziella and Gabriella knew where it came from, what it meant.

She had raised her hand. She was still wearing it. She had caressed it before taking her phone back with both hands. And under the photo, she had simply written:

« per sempre ».

The next morning, the twenty-sixth, Carina had received an e-mail from the post office. The parcel had been delivered.

Maya had indeed received her soccer jersey, flocked with her name and lucky number, as well as the letter.

Carina had reread the e-mail several times, her stomach in knots. What had she expected? An immediate message from Maya? A thank you?

She'd spent her afternoon wandering the campus, her mind elsewhere. She had consulted her phone too often, as if staring at the screen would make her appear.

Carina hadn't wanted to send to the group. She wanted it to be just between them. She had also sent her a message at midnight « to be the first », but it had remained unread.
