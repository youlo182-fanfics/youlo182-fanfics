Saturday night, everyone was there. All their friends. They had taken a big table in a downtown bar/restaurant.

There was a lot of laughter, lively conversation and joking among themselves.

But someone was missing.

Maya.

Carina knew this even before she saw her message in the group chat. She knew the blonde wasn't coming. And yet, while they were all at the table laughing, she'd opened their WhatsApp group chat again. As if to make sure Maya hadn't sent anything since. In case she'd missed a message, or a notification. She had traced the thread back to the sprinter's last message.

Maya: Sorry, I can't make it, I'm going to my grandparents'.

That's all. Carina felt her stomach twist.

She didn't know if it was true or just an excuse, but if it was really the case, Maya wasn't going to answer all weekend, her grandparents were anti-phone. Which meant no news. No messages. No status updates on Instagram.

No nothing.

Just silence.

Anyway it wasn't as if Maya would have given her any news of her own volition herself.

Jackson had put an arm around her shoulders, and she'd offered him an automatic smile, but part of her wasn't really there.

On Monday morning, Carina was walking to her classroom, hand-in-hand with Jackson, when she'd spotted a familiar figure at the end of the hall. Her heart missed a beat.

Maya. Maya... on crutches, with a knee brace.

- "Porca miseria!"

Without thinking, she'd let go of Jackson's hand and walked directly toward her.

"Maya..."

The blonde had raised her head, her expression indecipherable.

- "Oh. Hi."

Carina had frowned.

- "what happened to you?!"

- "Nothing serious... I guess I shouldn't have gone to practice Friday lunchtime... but I didn't really have my mind on anything else... It's just a sprain."

Maya spoke in a nonchalant tone, as if it were nothing, but Carina could see the pain in her eyes.

- "Do you want me to do something for you?" she asked softly. She'd started to reach out her hand to rest it on her shoulder but quickly snapped back.

Maya had had a bitter little laugh.

- "No, it's okay. Vic's picking me up and taking me back, just like at the beginning of the year."

Sting. The implication was clear. Just like at the beginning of the year, when they'd carpooled with Vic and Travis, before Carina had chosen Jackson.

The Italian had felt a pang of guilt pierce her chest, but the blonde hadn't given her time to respond. She'd turned her head and taken a heavy step forward, leaning on her crutches.

Carina had watched helplessly as she walked away, and her heart had clenched even tighter.

At the end of the day, in the school corridors, the brunette had quickened her pace to catch up with Maya, taking advantage of the fact that Jackson had left for soccer practice. She wanted to catch up with her before she joined Vic and Travis.

She hadn't thought about it. Her body had just moved on its own. When she reached her, she opened her mouth, then hesitated. But she'd taken her courage in both hands.

- "Maya..." she murmured.

The blonde had stopped, slightly surprised, and looked at her. She'd thought she'd made herself clear this morning.

- "Mmh?"

Carina could feel her heart beating too fast.

- "How was your day?"

A silence. A long silence. Maya had planted her eyes in hers, then, shrugged.

- "good."

A simple « good », without emotion, without depth, without sincerity, then, she'd gone on her way, without another word.

Carina had watched her walk away, fists clenched.

The next day, the Italian had decided not to give up, so when she'd bumped into the blonde in the self-service line, she'd tried again.

- "hey..."

Maya had looked up at her and frowned. Carina never ate at the self-service restaurant, so what was she doing there, with a tray?! The Italian had cleared her throat.

"Your knee... are you feeling better?"

Maya had stared at her, her gaze inscrutable.

She'd slid her tray onto the rails, shrugged and answered exactly as she had the day before:

- "yes".

Then she'd turned away and moved on.

- "let me take your tray!" Said she, hastily joining her.

- "It's okay. I'll manage." She replied without even looking at her.

Carina had felt an unpleasant shiver run up her spine. She'd lost something. Something she might never get back.

On Wednesday, she had once again bumped into the sprinter at the locker. She didn't want to ask, she knew she'd be rejected, but she couldn't help it.

- "Maya, are you-"

- "Yes. I'm fine."

This time, Maya hadn't even looked at her. Carina had felt her heart clench so tightly, she'd thought it would burst.

Too much. Too much. On Thursday, Carina was tired of being ignored. She couldn't stand being pushed away.

So that day, on her way out of gym class, where the blonde had remained on the bench, and now limping slightly on her way to her locker, the Italian had grabbed her by the arm.

- "Maya, wait!"

The blonde had stopped. But she hadn't turned around. The brunette had felt her heart beating faster.

"Can you give me a real answer?" she asked, pleading.

Maya had tensed, then swung around gently. Her gaze was icy.

- "A real answer?" she repeated with a bitter chuckle.

Carina had frowned.

- "yes. I just want to know how you're doing. Really!"

A silence, a heavy silence, then, Maya had exploded.

- "it's your fault!"

Carina had taken a step back, stunned.

- "cosa?!"

- "all of it!" spat Maya: "If I can't train, it's your fault!"

Carina froze.

- "What do you mean? Why is it my fault?! I didn't do anything!"

Maya had clenched her jaws. Her gaze was more hurt than ever.

- "Because I was looking at you!"

Her voice was trembling and Carina had felt her breath catch. The blonde had taken a step toward her.

"You were in the stands. You were ignoring me. You only had eyes for him."

Her tone was accusatory. The Sicilian had shaken her head.

- "Maya, I-"

- "All week, I saw you there! And not once did you look at me!"

Carina had felt her throat knot. She'd opened her mouth, but Maya had cut her off again.

"I just wanted one second. Just one second where you'd lay eyes on me."

Her voice was a heartbreaking whisper. Carina had felt a tear well up. Maya had finally pulled back.

"But you didn't..." She'd laughed coldly.

"So I looked away. I looked away from you and... I didn't see that my shoelace was untied."

A stab. A fatal blow. The brunette had brought a hand to her mouth, horrified.

Maya had inhaled deeply, then breathed out:

"All this... just to get your attention."

Carina couldn't breathe and the blonde had shaken her head, then, with a last breath, finished her off.

"But I get it."

Carina had raised her wet eyes to her and Maya had then offered her one last broken look.

"It's over."

Her tone was almost gentle. Resigned.

"And it's for the best."

Then she'd turned and left, without another word, without giving the brunette a chance to explain.

Carina had felt her heart sink. Her breath was short. She felt as if all the oxygen on the planet had just been sucked out. There was nothing she could say. There was nothing she could do. Because Maya was right. Because it was over. And because it hurt like never before.

The hall was magnificent. The organizers had chosen the theme « Starry Night ».

Fine strings of lights fell from the ceiling like a shower of light. Projectors diffused a light bluish glow, giving the room an enchanting atmosphere. Tables were set with black velvet tablecloths, embellished with glittering centerpieces, and the dance floor shimmered under the reflections of the lights.

It was magical. It was perfect.

But Carina couldn't breathe. Because Maya was there. Because the blonde had just arrived on the arm of Travis, her « official date » for the evening, while right behind her, the Italian hadn't been paying attention, but there was Maya and Travis's real date. The famous Sophomore who had been drooling over Maya since the start of the school year, and Travis's boyfriend Michael.

The blonde hadn't taken her crutches. She was limping slightly, her splint visible under the fitted pants of her midnight-blue three-piece suit, matching the theme perfectly.

She looked incredible.

Her jacket was perfectly tailored, her vest molded her torso elegantly, and her immaculate shirt made her even more irresistible.

Carina felt her breath catch.

The one and only time the Italian had seen the blonde dressed in a three-piece suit had been on New Year's Eve. An outfit that had never left her thoughts since all that time.

But tonight... She was different. Her bob had been straightened, giving her a strict look, and the color brought out her blue eyes. Carina had loved seeing her in a dress for the senior prom, but there was no denying that Maya in tuxedo was really something to behold.

However, there was a huge shadow over the picture. Tonight, Maya hadn't dressed for her... And above all, she'd come with someone else. Tonight, the Italian wasn't the American's date, and that probably bothered her more than anything else. She wasn't the one who was going to enjoy this evening. She wasn't the one who was going to be treated like a queen. She wasn't the one who was going to spend the evening with the woman she loved, and she definitely wasn't the one who was going home with Maya.

Elle Kingsley, Maya's date, the Sophomore. Brunette. Pretty. And now confident. Still, she was Maya Bishop's unofficial date, Maya Freaking Bishop, the future Olympic runner.

Unofficial because at their high school, tickets had to be bought for a boy and a girl. Travis had given the name Elle and Maya the name Michael. So, although on paper she was with Travis, in reality she was with the blonde... And she was completely hypnotized by her.

Carina had immediately hated the way Elle had put her hand on Maya's forearm after passing through « security ». The Italian had even come to think that it was a shame Maya's father, Lane, hadn't offered to be a chaperone tonight. It was just like him to want to stop his daughter from living, but obviously, to Carina's great misfortune, not tonight.

At least, if he'd been there, the blonde would have had to play the perfect little straight girl. Or she'd have had her evening the way she wanted it, with a girl and her father would have found out, she'd have had to come out and then she could have blown it all off and told Maya she was in love with her.

But of course, all this was utopian.

The Sophomore acted as if the sprinter belonged to her. As if she had the right. As if Maya was hers now.

Carina had clenched her jaws and Jackson, beside her, had squeezed her hand, as if to bring her back to him. As if to remind her that she was with him now.

But it was too late, her heart had already sunk.

They were all together. Maya, Elle, Travis, Michael, Jackson, Carina, Andy and Ryan on the same table. Obviously, it had to be this way.

At the next table, Taryn and Mika were laughing together, Jack was whispering something to Inarra, Vic was giving Lucas knowing looks, while Dean was trying to impress JJ with his soccer scores.

Everything was normal. Everyone was happy. Except for Maya and Carina. The blonde was flirting with Elle and the brunette was trying to ignore the pain.

But it was impossible. She could see everything.

Maya leaning slightly towards Elle to whisper something in her ear; Elle blushing; the sprinter nonchalantly running her hand over the Sophomore's shoulder; Elle laughing out loud, as if Maya were the funniest person in the world.

Carina was no longer hungry. She'd forced herself to bring a mouthful of food to her mouth, but it all tasted like heartbreak.

Nevertheless, on several occasions, their eyes had met. Maya, behind her mask of insolence, had stared at her furtively several times.

The Italian felt each glance like an electric shock. But she refused to give in. She refused to give in. So, when the blonde looked at her, she turned her head. She forced herself to smile at Jackson. To pretend. To pretend she didn't feel her heart tearing a little more by the second.

When the music had started playing and couples had formed on the dance floor, Jackson had stood up too.

- "Would you like to dance?" he asked, a soft smile on his lips.

Carina had glanced quickly at Maya. She was still seated. Her knee wouldn't let her dance. That was something, this Elle Kingsley wouldn't have the leisure to put her filthy paws on « her wife' s ! » body. The Italian swallowed, Maya wasn't her wife, she never had been. But at least she wouldn't have the privilege of dancing with the blonde; because even if Maya didn't like dancing, she did it wonderfully.

But the problem was, she wasn't alone. Elle Kingsley was glued to her, as if she hadn't been able to dance that one!

Carina looked away immediately. She'd grabbed Jackson's hand.

- "Of course!"

They had joined the dance floor. And Carina would have loved to enjoy the moment. She really did. She'd watched Teen Movie so many times, and it was probably the only thing that had thrilled her when she'd arrived in the States, the thought that she was going to get to experience it.

Besides, Jackson was perfect. He guided her gently, his hands firm but tender on her waist. She rested her head against his shoulder, closed her eyes and made a wish: that Maya would disappear from her mind.

But when she had opened them again, her gaze had immediately fallen on Elle Kingsley. Her fingers were resting on Maya's thigh and Carina had felt a sharp pain explode in her chest.

A little later, the announcement of the King and Queen of the ball was approaching, but Maya had decided she wasn't going to wait. Especially since the Italian girl and her Prince Charming were among the nominees.

Carina had seen the American get to her feet. She had a slight limp, but Elle was helping her to walk. They walked to the edge of the dance floor and their eyes met one last time. A furtive glance. Broken.

Maya had finally intertwined her fingers with Elle's, then they had gone out together, and part of Carina had gone out with them. The Italian had felt herself faint.

- "Carina?" whispered Jackson.

She'd come back to reality. She'd shaken her head slightly and sketched a fragile smile.

- "It's all right."

A few minutes later, the verdict was in. Madame Yang, the Senior Assistant had climbed onto the stage where the band was playing.

- "And the queen and king of the two-thousand-and-twenty-four promo are.... Carina DeLuca and Jackson Avery!"

Everyone had stood up and applauded. Everyone was happy. Everyone was laughing and celebrating. Except Carina.

They'd both gone up on stage hand in hand, and when Madame Yang had placed the crown on her head... She hadn't reacted at all. Her gaze was blank.

Because at that very moment, somewhere in a car on the other side of Seattle... Maya was kissing Elle Kingsley. And she'd felt it deep in her gut.

Jackson's voice had echoed through the speakers, but Carina could only hear a dull thud. She'd only come back to herself when Madam Yang had spoken her first name, and Jackson had handed her the microphone.

She took it and immediately put on her mask. She'd had her commercial smile.

- "thank you all."

The senior assistant had raised her eyebrows as if to say, « is that all?! » but the brunette had handed her the microphone.

- "well then, let's leave the dance floor for the king and queen of the ball!" Madam Yang said, and Jackson took his girlfriend's hand to get down from the stage so they could dance.

The music had started up in the Garfield High School gymnasium, allowing Carina and Jackson to « open the ball » and several miles away, in a place where Seattle kids came to flirt, Maya and Elle were in the latter's car, their lips joined.

A fevered kiss. A desperate kiss. A kiss that should never have happened. A kiss that was just another lie.

And as photo flashes exploded around them, Carina felt her heart implode.

The hotel had been stormed by students. It was tradition. After the ball, all the Seniors booked a room to continue the party, and Jackson was no exception.

But unlike the others, he hadn't expected Carina to ask him, of her own accord, to go. She'd had a hard time with Maya's departure, but once she'd got past the pain stage, she'd come to enjoy her end of the evening, and even her boyfriend had noticed that she was much more relaxed and had enjoyed herself more.

- "Did you get us a room?" The brunette asked.

- "Do you want us to... go upstairs?" he asked, surprised.

Carina had nodded slowly. She didn't feel like staying in this room where everything reminded her that Maya wasn't in this hotel, that Maya had left with someone else hours ago now.

She needed to forget. Jackson took her hand and led her to the elevator. They hadn't said a word the whole way. But when the door to their room had closed behind them, Carina had come to kiss him. Without hesitation. Without restraint. As if she wanted to prove something to herself. As if she wanted to convince herself.

And that night, they made love. And again. Well, for the second time.

But this time... She hadn't heard her own voice begging in Italian. She hadn't heard her heart racing. She hadn't felt the burning that consumed her, like when it was Maya against her skin. She'd felt nothing but emptiness. But she'd carried on anyway. Because it was what she had to do.

The last week of classes had finally arrived and the school had changed. Everyone was more relaxed. It was their last week of classes.

Summer was coming. Life would go on. But for Maya and Carina, there was nothing more to go on. They no longer spoke to each other. They didn't even look at each other.

On Monday morning, however, Jackson had gathered his courage. He approached Maya in the corridors. She was alone. She was putting her things away in her locker, her brace still clearly visible on her knee.

- "Maya, can we talk?"

She'd slammed her locker shut.

- "Do you remember that I exist?!" she sneered, but Jackson wasn't fazed.

He knew she was going to react like this.

- "just two minutes... Please."

She'd sighed, then run a hand through her hair and followed him on his crutches to a quieter corner.

He'd looked at her for a few moments and she'd held his gaze, cold.

"I'm sorry." he finally said.

Maya had raised an eyebrow.

- "Sorry for what?"

- "For... all of it."

She'd crossed her arms.

- "Be more specific, Avery."

He'd taken a breath.

- "when I first became interested in Carina, I didn't realize you were... experiencing something."

Maya had tensed. Her heart had quickened.

- "We weren't experiencing anything." she replied automatically.

- "Maya..." Jackson had breathed.

She had looked away.

"When I realized there was something... I didn't think you cared... Like with all girls..."

He had hesitated, then added, lower:

"But I finally understood."

Maya had clenched her fists.

- "Understood what?"

- "That you had feelings for her."

- "I didn't!"

He'd smiled sadly.

- "if it wasn't true... you wouldn't have reacted like that."

She hadn't answered. She couldn't.

"I'm sorry..." he repeated.

Maya had looked up at him and, for the first time, seemed to hesitate. Jackson, on the other hand, had continued.

"You're one of my best friends. And I feel like... it's not the same between us anymore."

Maya had run a hand over the back of her neck, then looked away.

- "I don't blame you, Jackson."

He'd stared at her and she'd shrugged.

"Carina's a big girl and she chose. She chose you. And I can't blame you for falling for her. I understand... I understand a little too well..."

There was silence between them, then he resumed:

- "so we're still friends?" he tried.

Maya had hesitated, then shrugged.

- "You and me, yes."

Jackson had nodded, relieved. But Maya had immediately added:

"But with Carina... I can't. It's beyond my strength..."

He hadn't insisted. He'd simply nodded, then left, and Maya had felt her heart clench a little tighter.

For the rest of the week, Maya and Carina didn't speak again. They had become strangers. They didn't even look at each other.

Maya was talking to Jackson again, and everyone had noticed that there was something strange between them. But no one understood what, or even why.

On Friday, the auditorium was packed. Students filed on stage, one by one, receiving their diplomas to great applause.

Maya was still walking with difficulty, her brace visible under her graduation gown.

Carina, on the other hand, felt like she was living a waking dream. It was all over. High school was over. A page had been turned.

But as she looked at the blonde, she realized that another page had also definitively closed. And that page was them.

That evening, everyone had gathered at the restaurant to celebrate the end of high school.

Maya had come. With Elle, again.

Carina hadn't flinched. She had nothing to say after all, so she pretended it didn't affect her. As if Maya didn't exist anymore.

She'd laughed at Jackson's jokes. She'd even been more affectionate than usual. She'd put on a perfect smile, becoming the Carina she'd been before May. But deep down... It was all a lie.

Maya had remained discreet. She watched from a distance. She knew she'd lost. Carina was somewhere else. With someone else.

And they... They were nothing. Broken. More than. More than official. They were nothing to each other.

The first few days of vacation had begun gently. Seattle was bathed in late-June heat. Exams were behind them. Their diplomas in their pockets. It was the summer of freedom. The one where everything was supposed to be light. The summer they were supposed to enjoy.

And yet... Carina still didn't feel completely free.

On June thirty, Jackson had invited everyone to his house. His mother was away in Boston, and he had the house to himself. With a swimming pool, of course. Maya included.

Carina didn't want to dwell on her; she was concentrating on Jackson, on their friends. On the coolness of the water, on the bursts of laughter, on the silly games they were all playing together.

Jackson had dived under the water and emerged right in front of her, a mischievous smile on his lips.

- "You're too quiet, DeLuca."

She'd raised an eyebrow.

- "I'm enjoying, is that a crime?"

- "Not at all. But..."

A flash of mischief had passed into his eyes, and without warning, he'd grabbed her and lifted her up.

- "JACKSON NO!"

But it was too late, he'd thrown her into the water. She'd come to the surface laughing.

"You're dead!"

She'd swum over to him, splashed him and pulled him under. Their laughter echoed all around them. Everything was light. All was normal.

And yet... A few yards away, Maya was watching them. Without really seeing them.

It was another tradition: for the Fourth of July, like every year, they went to the amusement park. Once again, everyone was there.

The sun was beating down, the music was blaring, the smell of churros and cotton candy filled the air.

- "What do we do first?" shouted Vic.

- "The roller coaster!" replied Andy, excited as a child.

Everyone had nodded except Maya, and the Italian couldn't help herself.

- "Maya, are you coming?"

The blonde had shrugged.

- "I'll wait here."

- "Because of your knee?" asked Carina, slightly worried.

Maya had raised an eyebrow and a smirk had passed over her lips.

- "Do you care now?"

Carina had bitten the inside of her cheek. She shouldn't have asked.

- "Forget it."

She'd joined the others, but on the roller coaster, as Jackson held her hand, one thought had refused to leave her mind: had Maya tried to get close to her with that smile?

The vacation had been going on for ten days or so, and little by little, she was getting used to it. She was getting used to no longer being close to Maya. No longer sharing these small, harmless gestures. No longer being the first person she turned to.

She was learning to live with her feelings. To bury them under light moments. Under the evenings at Jackson's, when he made her laugh until she cried. Under the days when they stayed in her room, listening to music while hanging out on their phones. Under the nights he kissed her, touched her, possessed her with tenderness.

He made her happy. He really did. But sometimes... Just sometimes... When she closed her eyes after a perfect day... She could still feel Maya's shadow in the hollow of her chest.

On the fifteenth of July, two days before the Italian's eighteenth birthday, they had gone to Dean's for a pool party.

It was a new day with friends, a new excuse to have fun. They'd all gathered at the Millers', who had an even bigger pool than Jackson's.

Carina hadn't been drinking, but she was feeling light. She laughed, she talked loudly, she lived.

She could feel Jackson's arms around her waist, and she'd let herself go against him.

And then... Her gaze had met Maya's. For a split second, a second too long. Because in that blue gaze... there was still something.

Something Carina refused to analyze. So she looked away and resumed her evening.

She'd laughed a little harder. She'd kissed Jackson with more passion; because it was what she had to do. Because it was her choice.

And yet... A few yards away, in the water... Maya couldn't take her eyes off her.
