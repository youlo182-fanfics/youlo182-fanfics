Carina was sleeping peacefully in her dorm room. She had gone to bed not so long ago when her phone had rung, waking her up.

She had grabbed it when she was already in her deep sleep phase. She hadn't forgotten to put it on silent, there were just certain numbers that could contact her at any time. One of which was this number.

She opened her eyes to see her brother's name on the screen. She immediately picked up.

- "Andrea?" She asked worriedly. "Sono le quattro del mattino..." [It's four o'clock in the morning...]

There had been a blank. She'd heard her brother let out a breath, as if he needed to gather his courage.

He'd probably done something stupid, now he was the age she'd been when they'd arrived in the States. Andrea was a Senior now, he'd been a Senior for two and a half months. It was the end of October.

- "c'è stata una sparatoria" [there's been a shooting] he said in a trembling voice.

She had immediately straightened up in bed. More alert than ever.

- "Sei ferito?" [are you hurt?!] she asked worriedly.

- "no... Non io" [no... Not me] he replied hesitantly.

- "Ma... Mamma?"

- "... No."

Carina had put her hand before her lips. And her fingers had begun to tremble.

She'd opened her mouth, but no sound had come out. Anguish had tightened her throat, knotting her vocal cords like a vise. She had tried to swallow her fear, but her heart had begun to pound against her chest with such force that she had thought for a moment that the man lying next to her could hear her.

Devi chiedere. Devi chiedere. [Ask. You have to ask.]

But the words stuck, as if uttering them would make the horror real.

- "Ma... Maya?" She asked, her voice frail, as if she didn't want to hear that answer.

- "No, Carina, è stato papà an essere colpito." [no, Carina, it was dad who got shot].

The words had struck her mind, but their meaning had taken several seconds to sink into her. Her father. Not Maya. Her father.

A thud had echoed in her ears, as if everything around her had blurred. She wanted to speak, to react, but her body wouldn't respond. Her gaze remained fixed, empty, lost somewhere between senseless relief and brutal panic. Then, suddenly, the air had violently entered her lungs, and pain had exploded inside her. Her heart stopped for a moment and tears began to flow.

Then she'd felt a hand run up her back.

- "darling? What is it?"

- "It's... It's... It's my dad... He, he got… shot."

- "what?!"

- "Lui... Lui è... M-" [he... He is ... M-]

- "no."

Carina had breathed out, but she didn't know if it was the relief of knowing he wasn't dead, or if it was something else.

- "Cosa è successo?" [what happened?]

- "Qualche settimana fa papà ha operato questa donna. Voleva... sperimentare qualcosa. Il marito non voleva, non voleva che sua moglie fosse una cavia da laboratorio... O una cavia... Ma all'inizio lei era d'accordo, poi, visto che il marito era riluttante, si è tirata indietro... E lui... Ha falsificato i documenti... Sai com'è fatto... Voleva disperatamente provare questa tecnica rivoluzionaria... e... E lei è morta. È morta sul tavolo operatorio." [A few weeks ago, Dad operated on this woman. He wanted to... Experiment with something. The husband didn't want to, he didn't want his wife to be a lab rat... Or a guinea pig... But at first she agreed, then as her husband was reluctant, she backed out... And he... He falsified the documents... You know how he is... He was desperate to try this revolutionary technique... and... And she died. She died on the operating table].

Carina couldn't believe what she was hearing. She knew her father had always been stubborn, he'd always done what he wanted, but to the point of causing someone's death?!

"Allora... Ieri sera... Quest'uomo... È tornato a Seattle Pres e... E ha inseguito papà... Ha sparato ad altre persone... Ma ha sparato a papà... è... È ancora in sala operatoria. L'uomo che... Si è sparato quando ha visto papà « finalmente » sul pavimento." [So... So last night... This man he... He came back to Seattle Pres and... And he looked for dad... He shot other people... But he shot dad... He's... He's still in surgery. The man he... He shot himself when he saw Dad « finally » on the floor].

The brunette didn't know what to think. She wondered if it was karma, but she didn't want to have that thought because she thought it was mean, as if she was saying « well done to him ». She didn't answer. She was too shocked.

"Puoi venire?... Almeno per la mamma..." [Can you come?... At least for mom...] Had finally asked her brother.

- "Certo, Andrea, certo che sto arrivando. Sto arrivando. Prendo il primo aereo per Seattle." [of course, Andrea, of course I'm coming. I'm coming. I'm taking the first plane to Seattle].

They were about to hang up, and Carina had called out to her brother one last time.

"Andrea? ... Grazie per avermi chiamato." [Andrea? ... Thank you for calling me].

- "Ci vediamo dopo" [See you later].

They'd hung up and Carina was still in bed. Shocked.

The young man next to her was now sitting up.

- "he's..."

- "no." Replied the brunette.

- "you..."

- "I'm going to Seattle. I have to go to Seattle."

She'd started to get up.

Winston had opened his mouth, then hesitated. Was it up to him? Carina needed support, that was obvious, but was he the one who needed to be there, now? He had glanced quickly at his phone, as if searching for an answer elsewhere, then looked up at her. She was trembling slightly, her gaze lost.

He stood up and followed her. He placed his hands on the Italian's cheeks.

- "hey beautiful. I'm coming with you."

- "you've got class."

- "and so do you."

- "that's my dad."

- "I'm coming with you today and I'll be back tonight, whether you stay or come back with me."

- "thank you Winston." She made, closing her eyes and coming to kiss him.

He had then taken her in his arms. When he'd closed his arms around her, Carina had first felt the warmth of his embrace, but the emotion was too raw, too violent. Her body was rigid, frozen in shock, unable to absorb the comfort he offered. Yet after a few seconds, a slight tremor had shaken her, and she'd let herself go, just for a moment, against his shoulder. Not enough to chase away the pain, but enough to remind herself that she wasn't alone.

- "that's what boyfriends are for, isn't it?"

They had gotten ready in fourth gear. While Carina showered, her boyfriend had been looking at plane tickets. If they didn't hang around too long there was a flight at six forty-five.

She'd kept quiet all the way to the airport, and they'd quickly checked in and made their way to the boarding area.

The terminal was relatively quiet. Usually, even at this hour, it was bustling. But not today. At least that was Carina's impression. In fact, it was just as noisy as usual, and she was just lost in thought.

Barely two years since she and Jackson had broken up. A little over two months ago, she had just started her third year of university to take her Bachelor's Degree in Science.

So much had happened since then. Her relationship with her father had soured, and Carina knew it hurt her mother not to understand, not to get to the bottom of the problem. All she knew was that her husband and daughter were no longer getting along. She thought it was because of the Italian's choice of career.

Was the fact that her daughter had chosen obstetrics for her husband such a disgraceful choice that they had become so angry that they no longer spoke to each other?

Besides, Carina and Jackson's separation was bound to have had something to do with it. Jackson was Vincenzo's pride and joy. He loved his daughter going out with him.

Of course, she didn't know that her husband had actually been blackmailing their daughter.

With everything that had happened in Seattle between, this thing with her father, and losing Maya. Having gone out with Jackson out of spite but also out of blackmail. She found it hard to go back to Seattle. There were too many painful memories.

She'd come back for weekends here and there, but not as many as she could have. Instead, she'd spent her entire first summer between her freshman and sophomore years of college at her grandparents' house. And after that, she'd found herself a little job close to campus to make a bit of money, and working there on weekends and vacations made her inability to return to Seattle more credible.

The brunette had been brought out of her thoughts to board the plane. Once seated, she lost herself again in her thoughts. Winston hadn't wanted to disturb her.

She was always talking to the group. In particular Vic, Andy, Jack, Dean, well, all of them, she even saw Jackson on San Francisco, so she was talking to all of them except her. They interacted on the group chat they always had, but she hadn't seen Maya face-to-face since Thanksgiving weekend two thousand twenty-four. Probably the hardest part of all was that their brother, Mason, and Andrea had become inseparable.

She'd seen Maya before, seeing her working out, without Maya knowing it, or driving past her parents' house, when she knew she'd drop off her laundry there on weekends. But she'd never dared go and see her. The good thing was that the closer the Olympics got, the more Maya had to travel to other cities for competitions, so the brunette took advantage of these moments to come to Seattle and see their friends.

And as she had predicted, « out of sight, out of mind » was anything but the truth. It had been two years since she'd spoken to her directly. She still thought about her just as much, and above all, she still loved her just as much.

But she'd learned to live with her feelings, and sometimes she even daydreamed to herself about « come on less than three years », « come on less than two years, three days and a few hours ».

This countdown? The days before Maya competed in the Olympics and could potentially finally come out to her parents.

She clung to these numbers like a lifeline, a promise that it would all make sense. But tonight, in the suffocating silence of the night, she was no longer sure of anything.

What if she couldn't do it?

The idea had struck her with unexpected brutality, a sudden vertigo that took her breath away. Her throat tightened. She'd never doubted. Never. But now, as the future wavered beneath her feet, she had felt fear creep into her bones for the first time.

But then, Carina was counting the days. She was counting the seconds.

Yet she hadn't stopped herself from living. If for a long time after Jackson she had remained single, during her summer vacations in Sicily and afterwards, she had started dating. Or at least she'd started flirting with people. Boys, but also girls. But they were more flings than real relationships.

And then there was Winston. She'd met him that summer at work, Winston had begun his studies in New York for his science degree, and he'd decided to continue on to San Francisco for Medical School. He was from here, from San Francisco, and on his way to visit the campus, he'd bumped into Carina in the café where she was a waitress.

They had started seeing each other as friends and by the middle of the vacations had become more. They'd been going out for four months. In fact, tonight she was at his place. There had been a party at Winston's fraternity and they'd been there, before going to bed together in the young man's room. Until that phone call.

Landing at almost nine in the morning, Carina and Winston had headed straight for Seattle Pres. Winston had never met Vincenzo before, though he had seen Lucia and Andrea when they had come to visit Carina in San Francisco, just before he started his senior year in high school and she her third year in college.

They had arrived at the hospital and Winston had gone to wait in his waiting room, while Carina had run down the corridors. She had stopped in front of a room with a large bay window.

Inside, Andrea stood at the end of the bed, and next to their father, Lucia sat on a chair, her eyes red.

Vincenzo was intubated, and more than that. Carina could see wires, cannulas and bags of fluid clinging to his body, and he lay there unconscious.

She'd been staring out the window, as if at a painting. As if she couldn't move on.

Her brain seemed incapable of assimilating the scene. She knew the wires, the monitors, she knew exactly what each parameter meant. Yet everything was blurred, unreal. It was as if she were watching the body of a stranger, not her father.

She was pinned down, unable to move. Then, having been there for an eternity, staring at the scene. She felt a hand slide up her back.

- "Carina?"

The brunette had gasped, it was Maggie Pierce, a colleague of her father's. She'd seen her several times before at hospital parties where she'd had to accompany her father, when they played the perfect little tight-knit family.

- "he... Does he..."

- "he's hanging in there. We've done all we can... But now it's up to him to fight..." She replied.

She had watched Carina for a few moments before taking her hand and adding.

"It's good you came... He needed this. He needs his whole family."

The Italian had turned her head towards her. Her eyebrows slightly furrowed. Would her father have said something? Did he, in a way, resent the fact that their relationship had deteriorated? She found it hard not to blame him. She found it hard to think she should forgive him, « because he'd been shot ». After all, if he was sorry, why hadn't she told him? Why not break his blackmail, stop it, instead of continuing to break his daughter.

- "Carina?" Lucia's voice had snapped the Italian out of her thoughts and she'd turned her head, leaving Maggie's eyes, for her mother's.

The doctor had let go of the young woman's hand and the Sicilian had approached her mother, who had risen to the bedroom door.

Carina and Lucia hugged each other, then the brunette beckoned her brother to join them, and they stayed like that for a moment. The three of them embracing.

After a few minutes like that, Carina asked to know more. She wanted to understand what had happened, and when her father had started making increasingly irrational decisions. She'd discovered that since she'd stopped talking to him, or at least having respect and love for him, he'd begun to change his behavior, like a bipolar person. At home, he still seemed pretty much the same, but when he was in surgery, Andrea and Lucia had discovered that for the past two years, things hadn't been going as well at work as he'd let on, or said.

They'd spent a while talking like this, talking their hearts out. But of course, Carina hadn't mentioned the blackmail. She'd just talked about the fact that he no longer respected her for her career choice and for letting Jackson go without a fight.

It had been over two hours since she'd landed from San Francisco and she'd even forgotten about Winston, who was probably sleeping on a chair in the waiting room.

She had remembered him and apologized. She had told her mother and brother that she was going to see Winston and would be back with coffees.

When she returned fifteen minutes later. She had seen her father try to open his eyes as Lucia and Andrea were talking together.

She had stopped in the doorway as he had looked in her direction. He had finally stared at her and she could see his hand open and close in her direction.

She didn't want to do it. She didn't want to offer him this comfort when for two and a half years he'd been destroying her. But he was her father and the eight-year-old Carina in her wanted to go and see her daddy.

She placed the three-coffee holder on the table at the entrance to the room and, without taking her eyes off him, approached the bed.

Her fingers trembled. Her gaze remained fixed on that hand that opened and closed, that gesture so simple, so fragile. An appeal. A silent forgiveness.

She could have stepped back. She should have. But the little girl in her, the one who once ran to him laughing, had taken over. A heartbeat, a jerky breath, and her fingers had slipped into Vincenzo's at last.

Lucia and Andrea had stared at her without saying a word.

Vincenzo had clasped her hand in his, without taking his eyes off her, and with a flicker of Carina's eyelash that had triggered the fall of tears down her cheeks, the heart monitor had begun to sound with a long, shrill beep.

The alarm at the entrance to the room began to sound. Lucia screamed, and before they knew it, they were out of the room as an intern began giving the family dad CPR.

Maggie had arrived. They stood there on the other side of the bay window, like Carina earlier, watching this scene, this tableau vivant, seeing everyone moving around Vincenzo, the three of them feeling as if everyone in that room was moving in slow motion and at the same time surprisingly fast, while they were frozen.

An eternity, everything had lasted an eternity. Then, without understanding why, everyone in the room had stopped moving.

They had seen Maggie reach for the top of her cap and pull it off, closing her eyes. Then she had looked at her watch and they had been able to hear it.

- "time of death, 12:37 p.m."

Her father had died on a Sunday at twelve thirty-seven.

Lucia had collapsed to the floor screaming, barely caught by her son and Carina, she had remained frozen, her eyes on the scene in front of her. Everything seemed unreal, and it was as if she'd left her body.

She could hear Lucia screaming, Andrea murmuring something, but the sounds came to her as if muffled under water. Her own tears were flowing, yet she couldn't feel them. Her body seemed foreign to her, as if she no longer belonged to it.

Her father was dead. That was a fact. A simple fact. Like a clinical sentence she might read in a medical report. And yet, this sentence provoked a feeling in her that she didn't know how to interpret.

Maggie had finally left the room and come to talk to Carina. Not that she wasn't paying attention to Lucia, but the brunette seemed to be the one, at least from the outside, who looked like she was still standing. On the inside, it was chaos.

After she'd left, the nurses had disconnected Vincenzo from all his tubes and electrodes. They had turned off all the machines and asked the three family members if they wanted to come and say goodbye, before the morgue came to collect him.

The three of them stayed for a while, Carina now holding her mother and brother in her arms. Almost unable to cry.

Then, after almost an hour, they were invited to leave the room. They headed for the waiting room, where Winston was still waiting.

And there, when they'd reached the rows of chairs, they'd all been there, everyone who was still in Seattle, Jo, Andy, Ryan, Jack, Mason and her... Maya was there, next to Winston. The Italian's heart had stopped for a second, a beat.

Her friends were there, Maya was there. Two years she hadn't seen her. Two years since she'd heard the sound of her voice live, since she'd had a chat with her alone. Two years soon and yet here Maya was, with her friends, with her boyfriend. Her breathing had stopped too, and before her brain had even had time to think, her legs had sped forward and she'd thrown her arms, not around her boyfriend's neck, or Jo's, Jack's strong, comforting arms, no, she'd come to hug Maya.

She'd felt her body crash against hers, violently, and her face had sunk against her neck, against her hair, and only then had she been able to breathe again, then she'd felt the blonde's arms come around her body in return.

Everything was falling apart inside her, and yet, Maya was there.

The familiar scent of her shampoo, the warmth of her body against hers. She was a bulwark against chaos, a refuge she knew by heart.

Carina didn't know how long she'd stayed like that, her face buried against her, her hands clutching the fabric of her jacket.

But at that moment, nothing else existed. Nothing but them.
