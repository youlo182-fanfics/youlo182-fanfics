"You don't know. You've learned."

The nuance had been slight, but important. The blonde had let a second pass, then nodded.

- "Yes."

There had been nothing awkward about the silence that had followed. It had just been there, steady. Jack had finally given a slight nod toward the station's interior.

- "Come on, I'll show you where to put your stuff." He'd said it as if he, Andy and Maya hadn't been running around these corridors, these rooms for years, getting berated by firefighters.

Maya had retrieved her bag from the floor. Her eyes had slid around her for a second.

The trucks.The walls.The activity.

This time, it wasn't a dream. It wasn't her dream either, not completely. But it wasn't far from it. She had followed Jack.

A little slower than him, but without stopping. Not because she was tired. Not out of pain. But just to anchor in her memory, in her footsteps, those of her first day at Station 19.

- "You know..." he'd said as he walked, bringing her out of that solemn moment.

"If you fall apart today..."

She'd rolled her eyes.

- "Jack..."

- "I'm just saying..." he'd shrugged:

"I'm not carrying your weight alone to the ambulance."

She'd sketched a smile. A little tired - it shouldn't be forgotten that she'd had her week's work cut out for her. And that despite everything... She wasn't the Maya she'd been before either. She was still feeling the symptoms of chemotherapy. The symptoms of a menopause.

- "Don't worry." She replied before pausing:

"I intend to stay on my feet."
