The waiting room in the gynecological oncology department was quiet that morning. The walls were white, almost too clean. A few posters talked about prevention, follow-up and reconstruction after cancer. A television was on in the corner, but the sound was muted.

Maya was sitting on a plastic chair, her hands resting on her thighs. Her gaze was fixed on the floor. Carina was sitting next to her. Their fingers were intertwined.

Since their arrival, the blonde had hardly spoken. She observed the comings and goings in the corridor, the nurses passing by, the files being carried from one room to another.

The smell of antiseptic still reminded her too much of the last time she'd been here less than a fortnight ago, and the Kerry Park outing had seemed far away then. Like, in another time.

The Italian had sensed her tension so she'd gently squeezed his hand.

- "Breathe, amore."

Maya had let out a small breath.

- "I breathe."

She'd looked up at the hallway.

"I hate this place."

Carina had sketched a sad little smile.

- "Me too."

- "Yeah... You still want to work there..."

- "And perform the miracle that Dr. King did... I want to save lives... But me personally, I hate it because I see you here again... It's hard for me to go back through the ER now... I'm always afraid I'll see my mother in that hallway again and immediately understand..."

Maya had run her thumb over the back of Carina's hand.

- "But it's okay now..."

She had sketched a slight smile.

- "It's not for you to console me..."

- "Let me have that role for once..."

A few seconds later, a nurse had emerged from an office almost cutting them off in their conversation.

- "Maya Bishop?"

The blonde had risen. Carina with her. The nurse had smiled at them.

"Dr. King will see you now."

They'd walked down the hall together. Charlotte King's office was at the end. The door was already open. Charlotte was standing by her desk, a file in her hands. She had looked up as they entered.

- "Hello Maya." She said with a slight smile.

- "Hello doctor."

Charlotte had looked at Carina.

- "Good morning, Carina."

- "Good morning."

She had pointed to two chairs.

- "Make yourselves comfortable."

Maya had sat down slowly. Carina had stayed close, sitting beside her. Charlotte had lowered her eyes to the file as she too sat down. Moving her computer mouse to bring up the blonde's computer file.

"How have the last two weeks been?"

Maya had shrugged slightly.

- "I'm tired."

Charlotte had nodded.

- "That's normal. Your body is still recovering from the surgery."

She'd noted something in the paper chart.

"Any significant pain?"

- "No."

Maya had thought for a moment.

"It's stirring a little."

- "That was expected."

Charlotte had closed the paper file to put it to one side.

"I'll take a look at your scar."

Maya had nodded and stood up to move to the examination table.

Charlotte had put on gloves.

"Lift your shirt, please."

The blonde had obeyed. The doctor had observed the scar for a few seconds, then gently placed her fingers around it.

"Does it hurt?" She asked, raising her gaze to the blonde's face to gauge the pain.

- "No."

- "Numb?"

- "Yes..." Then Maya had squinted.

"Is this normal? It's like I'm touching... But that there was a ... a thickness between my fingers and my scar... It's strange."

Charlotte had nodded.

- "It's very common after this type of surgery. Certain nerve endings take time to recover."

She had removed her gloves.

"I'm glad, though. The scar looks beautiful."

Maya had looked up.

- "Beautiful?" Said the blonde, surprised. As she straightened up to get off the examination table.

Charlotte had sketched a small smile.

- "For a laparotomy, yes. It heals very well."

Carina had let out a small breath of relief. Charlotte had resumed her seat behind her desk.

"Now let's talk about what's next."

Maya had felt her shoulders tense slightly. Dr. King had opened the file.

"As we discussed after the procedure, the surgery removed all visible disease."

She had paused.

"That's very good news."

There was silence for a few seconds. Then Charlotte had added calmly:

"But in oncology, we're not yet talking about a cure at this stage."

Maya had looked down. Charlotte had continued:

"At stage III, there's always a risk that some microscopic cancer cells may still be present in the body."

Carina had tightened her grip slightly around Maya's hand. Charlotte had continued - as imperturbable as ever:

"That's precisely why we're implementing adjuvant therapy."

Maya had raised her head.

- "Chemotherapy?"

- "Yes."

Charlotte had activated the file on the computer again, moving the mouse.

- "In your case, it will be oral chemotherapy."

Maya had frowned slightly.

- "Tablets?" She'd asked that, but more as a rhetorical question. She knew what «oral» meant.

She really hated it when doctors talked in «code» all the time. Couldn't they use simple vocabulary? Patients already had to get used to the idea of being sick. Now they had to speak in hieroglyphics. In gibberish. In terms that, when put together, sometimes even gave you a headache.

Yes, well, she could have just said «chemotherapy pills»... Why use terms like «adjuvant» or «oral»? Well, I know what 'oral» means-

- "Exactly."

The doctor had pulled her out of her thoughts. Then she had calmly continued:

"You'll take one tablet a day for twenty-eight-day cycles-or different intakes depending on the cycle. Like I told you two weeks ago. We'll decide before each new cycle."

She had paused.

"At the end of each cycle, you'll have a check-up appointment."

She'd counted down slowly:

"blood test; clinical examination; and side-effect assessment."

Maya had swallowed.

- "But we agree I won't lose my hair? Carina told me not to. And besides..." She'd turned her gaze to her girlfriend, intertwining their fingers a little tighter.

"She loves my neck. But I don't know if she'll ever love it without hair on top..."

Charlotte had shaken her head.

- "No. Indeed."

She had added:

"This treatment is much less aggressive than intravenous chemotherapy."

Carina had made big eyes at her lover. As if to say «I'll always love you, even without hair».

Charlotte had continued:

"You may experience fatigue. Sometimes nausea. But most patients tolerate this protocol well."

Maya had inhaled slowly.

- "How long?" She knew they'd talked about it after the procedure, but she was groggy at the moment.

- "About six months."

Silence had fallen for a few seconds in the room. Maya had whispered:

- "And then what?"

Charlotte had closed the file.

- "If everything goes according to plan, the treatment will be over by then."

She had added calmly:

"Then we'll continue with regular follow-up. Every three months. Then if all goes well, more spaced out. Until it's just once a year. I like to be cautious. And then if everything goes well, it's just a formality. You'll have a normal life."

Carina had nodded. Charlotte had continued:

"About the surgical menopause..."

Maya had looked up.

"Since your ovaries have been removed, your body no longer produces estrogen naturally."

She'd rested her hands on the desk.

"We're going to start hormone replacement therapy."

Carina had then asked:

- "At the same time as the chemotherapy?"

Charlotte had shaken her head.

- "No."

She had calmly explained:

"We'll start the adjuvant chemotherapy first."

She'd looked at Maya.

"Once we've assessed how your body tolerates it - usually after the first few cycles - we'll introduce hormone therapy."

Maya had nodded. Charlotte had added:

"This will help limit many of the effects of early menopause."

She had listed gently:

"Hot flashes; sleep disturbances; bone loss; and some long-term cardiovascular effects."

Carina listened attentively. Charlotte had concluded:

"We'll adapt the treatment gradually, depending on your recovery."

There was silence in the room. Then Dr. King had asked softly:

"Do you have any questions?"

Maya had thought for a few seconds. Then she'd whispered:

- "Will I... ever be normal again?"

Charlotte had taken a moment before answering. Then she'd said simply:

- "Your body has been through something very difficult."

She'd looked in the direction of the scar under Maya's shirt.

"But it's healing."

She'd sketched a slight smile.

"And so are you."

Silence had filled the room. And for the first time since they'd entered the ward... Maya had breathed a little more freely.

Two weeks later, the sun was slowly creeping into the apartment. Morning light streamed through the curtains and drew pale lines on the living room floor.

Maya was sitting at the kitchen table. In front of her was a glass of water and a small white box. She had been looking at it for several minutes already. Without opening it.

Carina was standing by the worktop. She pretended to be busy with the coffee, but in reality her gaze kept returning to the table. To Maya. To the box. Finally, the blonde let out a small breath.

- "This is ridiculous..."

Carina had looked up.

- "Cosa?"

Maya had pointed to the box.

- "I faced a four-hour operation... I had an emergency blood transfusion..."

She had sketched a tired smile.

"And now I'm stuck in front of a pill."

Carina had approached slowly and sat down opposite her.

- "It's not just a pill."

Maya had looked up.

- "No..."

She'd shrugged slightly.

"It's the sequel."

Silence had fallen between them again. The blonde had finally taken the box in her hands. She had turned it over. Observed it. As if still looking for a reason not to open it.

"One tablet a day..." She almost whispered.

"For six months."

The Sicilian had reached out and gently covered her lover's hand.

- "One day at a time."

Maya had opened the box. Opening it from the side with the leaflet.

- "Why do they always open these boxes upside down?! They could put a little «open here» so we don't have to slog through the instructions, which are longer than the Old Testament..."

Humor - again. But for once, Carina agreed. She had smiled. She really had.

- "you have to open it from the end of the word... That is... If I'm not mistaken."

- "We'll do the test next month..."

Then finally, there it was - in her hand. Simple. Almost banal. She'd pushed the tablet with the tip of her finger and let it drop into her palm.

It was small. Far too small to represent all it meant.

"It's strange..."

She'd observed it - like an alien object.

"I feel like this tiny thing is going to decide the next six months of my life."

Carina had sketched a small smile.

- "No."

Maya had looked up at her.

"You decide."

The blonde had blown softly through her nose.

- "You know you're unbearably positive?"

- "It's my charm."

A small smile had appeared on Maya's lips.

- "Trust me... There are an awful lot of things that make up your charm... And many of them are visible to the naked eye..." Had flirted the American before refocusing on her palm.

Then she took the tablet from between her fingers. Her gaze was lost for a moment in the glass of water. She inhaled. Then exhaled.

"Well..."

She'd looked up at Carina.

"If I have to fight a little longer..."

She'd sketched a small smile.

"Might as well start now."

The Italian had squeezed her hand lightly and the blonde had brought the tablet to her lips. She swallowed. A simple gesture. A gesture lasting only a few seconds. Then she'd taken a sip of water and the silence had remained in the kitchen. Just a few seconds. And Maya had finally put the glass back down.

"Voilà..."

She'd shrugged slightly.

"First battle won."

Carina had stood up. She'd rounded the table and approached her. Then she'd placed her hands on either side of her face.

- "I'm proud of you."

Maya had rolled her eyes slightly.

- "I just swallowed a pill..."

Carina had shaken her head gently.

- "No."

She'd kissed her forehead.

"You're still fighting."

Then she'd sketched a smile.

"Besides... I think you swallowed it with a lot of class... You were sublime."

The blonde had rolled her eyes with a slight laugh, then her gaze had softened and she'd whispered:

- "You know what?"

- "What?"

Maya had taken her hand.

- "I think I'm going to need you here..."

She'd sketched a small smile.

"Every morning."

Carina had intertwined their fingers.

- "That was already the plan, bambina."

The sun continued to fill the kitchen and for the first time, this tablet was no longer just a treatment. It was a promise that she would stay.

Three days later, the apartment was quiet. The rain fell softly against the windows - one of those fine, constant showers that seemed never to leave Seattle. Maya was sitting on the sofa. A blanket over her legs. A book open in her hands.

She wasn't really reading. Her eyes passed over the lines without holding them. For a few minutes, something had been wrong. Not so much pain as... a strange sensation.

As if her stomach had suddenly become too small. She frowned slightly.

Strange...

She'd turned a page and suddenly the sensation had risen.

Brutal. A wave.

- "Oh..."

The book had slipped from her fingers. Her hand had instinctively gone to her mouth. Nausea had hit her all at once, violent, unexpected. Her stomach had contracted.

"Baby..."

Her voice had come out weak. The brunette was in the kitchen.

She'd come almost immediately.

- "Amore?"

She'd stopped when she saw Maya's face. Pale. Too pale.

"Che succede?" [What's going on?]

Maya had shaken her head slightly.

- "I think..."

She'd swallowed, with difficulty.

"I'm going to throw up."

Carina hadn't wasted a second.

- "Okay."

She'd knelt in front of her.

"Breathe slowly."

But Maya had already put her feet on the ground. She'd stood up too quickly. The room had spun.

- "Wowww..."

The Sicilian had immediately caught her by the waist.

- "Easy, easy, amore..."

Maya had clamped her hand to her mouth.

- "Bathroom..."

They had crossed the apartment almost running. Carina was supporting her with one arm. As soon as they'd reached the bathroom, Maya had leaned over the sink - the toilet being too far an option at the moment.

Her stomach had contracted violently. And she'd thrown up. Once. Then a second. Her body had started to shake slightly and the brunette had tied her hair back quickly to get it out of her face.

- "It's okay... I'm here..."

She'd run a hand down her back. Slow movements. Reassuring. The crisis had passed as quickly as it had arrived. Maya had remained leaning over the sink, breathing hard. She turned on the tap and rinsed out her mouth. Then she looked up at the mirror. She looked exhausted.

- "Great..."

She'd dropped her head against the cool plane of the vanity.

- "I love this new chapter in my life..."

Carina had sketched a sad little smile.

- "It's probably the chemo."

Maya had closed her eyes.

- "Seriously?"

- "Nausea is common."

Carina had taken a glass and filled it with water.

- "Drink a little."

Maya had taken a small sip, but her stomach was still protesting.

"It'll pass..."

Carina had slid a hand across the back of her neck.

"Dr. King warned us."

Maya had sighed.

- "Yes..."

She'd let out a tired little laugh.

"But I was secretly hoping to be one of the patients with nothing."

Carina had shrugged.

- "Three days without symptoms was good enough."

Maya had leaned her head against the brunette's shoulder. Her eyes almost closing.

- "You know what the worst part is?"

- "What?"

- "I'd almost forgotten I was sick."

There had been silence for a few seconds. Then Carina had kissed her temple.

- "Then we'll pretend this nausea was just a parenthesis."

Maya had murmured:

- "A six-month parenthesis..."

Carina had smiled gently.

- "A parenthesis that will end at some point..."

Maya had closed her eyes. She'd let herself rest against her. Tired. But still on her feet.

- "The only good thing I can see right now... I'll never know morning sickness... Because frankly... No thanks."

A few days later, the apartment smelled of garlic and olive oil. Carina had been in the kitchen for almost an hour.

She had applied herself. More than usual. Because Maya had had less appetite for a few days. Nothing dramatic - but she was eating less again. And the Italian refused to let that become a habit.

So she'd prepared a simple dish. One of Maya's favorites. Pasta. Nothing complicated. Just fresh pasta, homemade tomato sauce, a little basil and grated Parmesan. The smell had filled the whole apartment.

- "Bambina?"

Her voice had carried across the living room.

"A tavola..." [At the table]

Maya had appeared a few seconds later. She was still walking a little slowly - her abdomen sometimes tugged - but she was doing much better than at first. She had settled down at the table. Carina had placed the plate in front of her with a small, satisfied smile.

- "Ecco fatto!" [Voilà!]

She had sat down opposite.

"Medical prescription."

Maya had raised an eyebrow.

- "Oh, yes?"

- "Yes."

Carina had taken on a very serious air.

"Doctor DeLuca strongly recommends pasta."

Maya had let out a small laugh.

- "DeLuca-Bishop..."

- "We're not married yet... Not even engaged!" Replied the outraged brunette.

Maya had raised her eyes to the ceiling as if to say, 'You're playing with words,» then picked up the fork.

The first bite had disappeared into her mouth and immediately... her face had changed. She froze. Her eyebrows had furrowed. And her girlfriend had immediately noticed.

"Cosa?"

Maya had swallowed hard, then put the fork down.

- "It's..."

She had hesitated. She'd taken another small bite. And this time she'd winced.

"Oh..."

Carina had straightened up.

- "What's wrong?"

Maya had shaken her head.

- "I don't know..."

She'd looked down at her plate.

"It tastes... weird, doesn't it?"

Carina had frowned.

- "Weird how?" The brunette asked, a little surprised.

Maya had been thinking.

- "Metallic... Are you sure the tomato sauce was still...? Good?"

She had made a little grimace.

- "Yes it was still good. And on top of that, it was in jars.... Do you think I'd give you something... Expired?!" Carina said, a little offended.

- "It's like licking an iron spoon..." Maya replied.

Carina understood immediately.

- "Aaaaahhh... okay... Chemo..."

Maya had pushed the plate slightly.

- "This is vile."

She'd taken another small bite - as if to check. The grimace had been even worse.

"Oh my god."

She'd taken a sip of water.

"This is horrible."

Carina had stood up.

- "Wait..."

She'd picked up a fork and tasted the sauce straight from the plate. She'd chewed for a few seconds. Then she'd looked up.

"It's good though..."

Maya had sighed.

- "I suspect..."

She'd run a hand over her face.

"That's the problem."

There was silence for a few seconds. Carina had picked up the plate again and brought it gently closer to Maya.

- "Try a little more."

The blonde had obeyed. A small bite. She had closed her eyes as she chewed. Then she'd shaken her head.

- "Impossible!"

Her voice had grown brittle and her eyes had filled with tears.

"It was one of my favorite dishes..."

Carina had approached her. She had crouched down beside the chair.

- "It's not the dish."

She'd put her hand on her thigh.

"It's the chemo."

Maya had dropped her head back against the backrest.

- "Great."

She'd huffed.

"Am I going to have to survive six months with a taste of metal in my mouth?"

Carina had sketched a small smile.

- "Not necessarily."

Maya had opened one eye.

- "No?"

- "We'll cheat."

The blonde had raised her head.

- "What do you mean?"

Carina had shrugged.

- "We're going to find out what your body will accept."

She'd started counting on her fingers.

"Cold foods. Acidic. Lemon. Ginger."

Maya was watching her.

- "When did you study this?"

Carina had assumed an innocent air.

- "Between an oncology chapter and one from the Military School..."

Maya had sketched a small smile. Then she'd pushed the plate away gently.

- "Sorry..."

Carina had shaken her head.

- "No."

She'd kissed her knee.

"Your body is fighting. So you have every right."

She'd gotten to her feet.

"And I'm going to figure out how to feed it during the war."

Maya had let out a tired little laugh.

- "You're taking this very seriously."

Carina had retrieved the plate.

- "Obviously."

She'd leaned toward her.

- "You're my favorite patient."

Maya had looked up at her.

- "You know I love you, Doctor?"

Carina had smiled.

- "Yes."

Then she'd added softly:

"And you're going to eat something tonight. Even if I have to test twenty recipes."

Maya had sighed. But a small smile still hung on her lips.

Because despite the chemo. Despite the metallic taste. Despite the fatigue. She knew she wasn't alone in this battle.

A few days later, the apartment was quiet. It was gray outside. One of those silent afternoons when the light seemed unwilling to enter the rooms.

Carina was sitting on the sofa with a book open in her lap. She'd only been reading for a few minutes, but her gaze kept returning to the kitchen. Maya was there. She was standing in front of the worktop, busy cutting an apple. At least... she was trying. The knife advanced slowly into the flesh of the fruit. Then it stopped.

Carina looked up. Maya had stopped moving. The blonde looked at the apple as if she'd forgotten what she was doing. Then suddenly... the blonde's shoulders began to tremble. The first tear fell onto the worktop. Carina had put the book down immediately.

"Amore?" she asked worriedly.

Maya had shaken her head.

- "No..." Her voice had broken.

"No, it's all right..."

But her hands were trembling now. A second tear. Then a third. Carina had risen and crossed the living room quickly.

- "Maya..."

The blonde had put down the knife. She had turned to her. And this time the tears were really flowing.

- "I don't know why I'm crying."

Her voice trembled.

"There's no reason."

Carina had approached gently.

- "Hey..."

She'd put her hands on her arms.

"What's going on?"

Maya had shaken her head again.

- "Nothing."

She'd wiped her cheeks in annoyance.

"This is stupid."

But the tears continued.

"I cut up an apple and start crying like a stupida."

Carina had understood. Not immediately. But soon enough. She'd slipped her arms around Maya and pulled her against her. The blonde had resisted for a second. Then she'd let herself fall against her.

"I hate this..."

Her voice was muffled against Carina's shoulder.

"I can't even control my emotions."

Carina had slowly run her hand through her hair. Slow gestures. Soothing.

- "It's not you."

Maya had sniffed.

- "Yes, it is."

- "No."

Carina had rested her forehead against hers.

- "Your body is going through a hormonal tsunami."

Maya had let out a shaky little laugh.

- "These six months are really going to be the most interminable of my life..."

She'd closed her eyes.

"Wasn't I supposed to stop having these... «hormonal»?"

Carina had sketched a sweet little smile.

- "You know how common they are after a hysterectomy?"

Maya had looked up at her.

- "I don't think I like it when you have all the answers... You're too realistic when I just want to cry and complain."

- "But you can still complain and cry if you feel the need to bambina."

She'd shrugged slightly.

"And it's totally human. The chemo, the fatigue, the brutal menopause..."

"All at the same time."

Maya had sighed.

- "So I'm going to cry in front of apples now?"

Carina had pretended to think.

- "Maybe over yoghurt too."

Maya had let out a little laugh. A real one this time.

- "Great."

Then her face had tightened again.

"I feel like I'm not me anymore..."

Carina had immediately taken her face in her hands.

- "You're still you."

But Maya had whispered:

- "Sometimes I don't recognize myself anymore."

There was silence for a few seconds. Then Carina had gently kissed her forehead.

- "You know what?"

- "What?"

- "I don't see anyone but the woman who's made my heart beat since I was seventeen.... And if you have to weather a storm..."

She'd hugged Maya a little tighter.

"Then we'll go through it together."

Maya had dropped her head against her shoulder. Her fingers had clung to Carina's T-shirt.

- "You're annoying with your knowledge... But I love you..."

Carina had smiled softly.

- "Perfect... Because I love you too. Even if I am annoying..."

Silence had returned to the kitchen. The apple was still waiting on the worktop. But for the moment... it didn't really matter. Because Maya was still crying a little and Carina was holding her. And sometimes... that was all we needed.
