The notebooks.

As if someone had dropped them one by one. As if someone had lost the strength to hold them.

No. The problem was all the others.

Lucia's words.

Maya's look in the hospital room. The way she'd said:

« I know how much you love me. »

Her handwriting.

«Mi ha fatto scrivere una lettera per i genitori di Maya.» [He made me write a letter to Maya's parents].

Carina had stopped breathing. The page shook in her hand. She turned her head to the other notebooks.

All those secrets.All those pieces of her life.All those pieces of pain - that she'd kept locked away for years. Under control. Always under control. With Maya. With her friends. With her mother. Even with Diane, her psychologist. With everyone.

She knew everything.

"You know everything now..."

And Carina had always wanted to spare her that. She always had. Always. Always. But now... it was too late. She'd dropped her head against the edge of the bed.

Perche? Perché? Perché ho scritto quei diari? Che stupido. Perché? Perché l'ho fatto!? [Why? Why did I do it? Why I wrote those diaries. Stupid. Why did I do it? Why did I do it?!?]

But she knew why. Because trauma victims had a tendency to block out bad memories.

Trauma. And she didn't want to forget. She'd never wanted to forget. Because forgetting meant forgiving him.

So the tears continued to flow. And for the first time in years... Carina DeLuca no longer had any control over history. Over her story. Because Maya knew. She knew everything.
