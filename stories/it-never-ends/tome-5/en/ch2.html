The block doors had barely closed when silence invaded the corridor. A strange silence. Thick. As if the world held its breath.

Carina stood motionless for a few seconds in front of the automatic doors. Her hand was still suspended in the air, exactly where Maya's had been a few moments earlier.

Lucia had approached gently behind her.

- "Tesoro..."

Carina hadn't replied. Her eyes were still fixed on the white doors.

A few minutes had passed. Maybe two. Maybe five. Carina couldn't say. Then the sound of a swinging door echoed behind them.

Charlotte King had just appeared in the corridor, already dressed in her dark blue scrub for the OR. The same blue net covered her hair and a mask still hung around her neck.

Carina straightened up immediately.

- "Doctor King!"

Charlotte had stopped in front of them. Her gaze was calm, focused.

- "The team is almost ready."

Carina had swallowed.

- "How long...?"

The question had been asked almost in a breath, and Charlotte had answered simply:

- "About three hours."

Three hours.

Carina had nodded, as if her brain were trying to figure out what that really meant.

Three hours.

Three hours in which she could do nothing.

A nurse had then appeared at the end of the corridor. At the doors leading to the blocks. The doors that had stolen her girlfriend.

- "Doctor King?"

Charlotte had turned to her.

- "Yes?"

- "OR two is ready."

The surgeon had nodded.

- "Coming."

She'd looked back at Carina one last time. Her gaze had grown soft, reassuring.

"We'll take good care of her. Promise."

She'd sketched a smile.

"And she'll take care of herself... Finally." She added, placing her hand on the brunette's shoulder.

Then she put on her mask and headed for the OR doors. A few seconds later, they closed behind her. And this time... there was no one left to enter.

Lucia had gently guided Carina towards the waiting room seats - or rather, the corridor waiting seats. Where the doors leading to the blocks were visible.

The young woman had dropped into the chair, as if her legs had suddenly stopped working.

The corridor was almost empty. Only a few dim lights illuminated the walls.

The minutes had begun to pass. Slowly, very slowly. Carina stared at the clock on the wall. The seconds seemed to stretch on forever. Lucia glanced at her daughter. Her hands were clasped together. Her fingers trembling slightly.

Without saying a word, Lucia had slipped her hand into hers. Just as she had done when Carina was a child. When she was afraid of the dark. The brunette had looked up at her and her lips had trembled.

- "Non posso farci niente." [I can't do anything.]

Her voice had broken, so her mother had squeezed her hand gently.

- "Lo so tesoro mio..." [I know my treasure...]

Carina had shaken her head.

- "Lo detesto." [I hate it.]

Lucia hadn't answered. She had simply continued to hold her hand.

The first hour had just passed by a few minutes now, and behind those white doors Maya was already on the operating table. And the minutes continued to tick by.

Half an hour.Then an hour.

The first hour had finally passed and no one had risen. No one spoke again. The corridor remained almost empty, crossed only by the occasional hurried nurse, or the creak of a metal cart.

Carina kept staring at the clock. The seconds. The minutes. Each time the hand moved forward, she felt as if her heart was taking another step towards the unknown.

Lucia, for her part, had finally looked away. She knew hospitals too well to remain hypnotized by a clock.

She looked up at the doors to the operating rooms. The same doors she'd walked through hundreds of times in her career - these, or others - block doors. But never had she been on this side. Never like this. All her life, she'd helped women bring children into the world. Hundreds of them. Perhaps thousands. She'd held trembling hands. Encouraged breaths. Welcomed the first cries.

Birth. The beginning of life. And today... Maya was behind those doors for the exact opposite. Lucia felt her throat tighten, because she'd just realized something she hadn't yet dared formulate. Maya would never know this. She would never. Never would she feel a child move under her hands - in her belly. Never would she feel her belly round. Never would she push in a birthing room.

Lucia had closed her eyes for a moment, not to cry, but just to breathe. And when she opened them again, she turned her head towards her daughter. Carina hadn't moved, but her gaze had changed. It was no longer fixed on the clock. It was elsewhere, lost in thought. Lucia knew that look. Her daughter was thinking. She was trying to understand.

- "A cosa stai pensando?" [What are you thinking?] Lucia had whispered.

It took the brunette a few seconds to reply.

- "Sto cercando di ricordarmi." [I'm trying to remember.]

- "Di cosa?" [Of what?]

Carina had swallowed.

- "I miei corsi." [My lessons.]

Her voice was low. Too quiet.

"L'anestesia... l'intubazione... la preparazione del campo operatorio..." [Anesthesia... intubation... preparation of the operating field...]

Lucia had watched her without interrupting. Carina spoke as if reciting a lesson.

"Prima lo metteranno sotto anestesia... poi gli libereranno le vie respiratorie..." [First they'll put her to sleep... then they'll secure the airways...]

She had swallowed her saliva.

"Poi inizieranno l'intervento." [Then they'll start the operation.]

Lucia understood immediately what she was doing. Her daughter was trying to turn this expectation into something rational. Something she could understand, control. But her hands were still trembling.

- "Tesoro..." murmured Lucia softly.

Carina had shaken her head.

- "So come funziona. In teoria." [I know how it goes. In theory.]

She had inhaled slowly.

"So cosa stanno facendo." [I know what they do.]

But after a second, her voice had broken in spite of herself.

"Ma non so se sta bene." [But I don't know if she's all right.]

Silence had returned. Lucia had squeezed her hand a little tighter. And the minutes had continued to pass. An hour and a half. Then soon two hours. And behind the doors of the operating theatre, the operation continued.

Two hours had just passed. The hand of the clock continued its relentless course. The Sicilian woman had stopped talking a while ago. She remained seated, her back slightly bent forward, her elbows resting on her knees. Her hands were clasped. Her fingers were still trembling.

Lucia hadn't said anything either. She knew that some expectations couldn't be met with words.

The minutes stretched on. Ten past two. Two fifteen. Two hours twenty. Then, finally, Carina stood up. The movement had been sudden. Almost too quick. Lucia had looked up at her.

- "Tesoro?"

Carina had run a nervous hand through her hair.

- "Vado... vado a prendere un caffè per noi." [I... I'll get us a coffee.]

Her voice was strained.

"Torno subito." [I'll be right back.]

Lucia had nodded gently.

- "Okay."

Carina had left the corridor without further ado. Her footsteps echoed lightly on the hospital floor. She had crossed another corridor. Then a third. And finally she'd arrived near the small break room where the coffee machines and vending machines were located.

She slowed down. And stopped dead. The room was full. Two children were sitting at a table laughing at a tablet. A woman was leafing through a magazine. A couple were chatting quietly by the coffee machine. Someone had probably just told a joke, because a small burst of laughter crossed the room.

Life went on. Business as usual. As if nothing important was happening.

Carina had remained motionless on the threshold. For a second, she had had the strange impression of having entered another world. A world where people breathed normally. Where people laughed. Where no one waited behind white doors to see if their loved one would survive.

Her throat tightened. She had finally made her way to the coffee machine. Her hands were shaking so badly she'd had to press the button twice. Then the second machine.

The machines had begun to rumble. The coffee poured slowly into the paper cups. Carina stared at the black liquid as if it were the most important thing in the world. Then she reached for the cups. But instead of drinking - instead of going back to her mother, she'd just stood there. Unmoving. Her gaze lost. Because at the same time, a thought had crossed her mind. Behind those doors, Maya was probably still asleep. Or maybe the operation was more complicated than expected. Or worse, maybe...

Carina had closed her eyes abruptly. No. She refused to think about that. She had inhaled deeply, then turned on her heels. Then she'd headed back down the corridor to the OR.

When she returned, Lucia was still sitting in the same place. It was as if she hadn't moved an inch. The brunette sat back down beside her and handed her one of the two cups. The coffee was still hot in her hands.

Lucia glanced at the clock. Half past two. Thirty minutes to go. If all went according to plan... The operation should be over soon.

Carina's coffee had gone cold in her hands. She had only taken two sips. The cup now rested between her fingers like a forgotten object.

Lucia looked up at the clock. Three hours. The hand had just crossed the number. The Italian mother felt her breath catch. Three hours. Charlotte had said around three. Carina had understood it immediately too. She sat up slightly in her chair.

Her eyes rested on the doors of the block.

White.Motionless.Silent.

The seconds had begun to tick away. Ten seconds. Twenty. Thirty.

Every sound in the corridor suddenly seemed amplified. The creak of a cart. The beep of a monitor in a nearby room. The distant murmur of two nurses.

But the OR doors remained closed. Carina had swallowed.

- "Sono già tre ore." [It's been three hours.]

Lucia hadn't answered right away. She knew the blocks. She knew that surgeries often went over schedule. Still, knowing didn't make the wait any easier.

- "A volte le operazioni richiedono un po' più di tempo." [Sometimes surgeries take a little longer.] she finally murmured - as if that would change anything.

Carina had nodded. But her gaze never left the doors. The minutes had begun to stretch again. Five past three. Ten past three. And still nothing.

The future doctor stood up. She started to take a few steps down the corridor. Then she stopped. Then she started walking again.

Her mother watched her. Without interrupting. She knew that sitting still became impossible when anxiety took up too much space. Carina had stopped in front of the block's doors.

As if standing closer would make a difference. As if she could hear what was going on behind them. But there was nothing. Only silence. She had started back in the other direction, then suddenly the automatic doors had opened and the Italian had immediately turned around.

Her heart had leapt into her chest. But it wasn't Dr. King. It was simply a stretcher crossing the corridor. A sleeping patient. Two nurses. They had passed without even looking at her. And the doors had closed. Then silence returned.

Carina felt her stomach tighten. She had finally made a U-turn and returned to sit next to Lucia.

"Dovrebbe essere finita..." [It should be over...]

Her voice was almost a whisper. Lucia had placed her hand gently on her arm.

- "Non necessariamente." [Not necessarily.]

Carina had shaken her head. But her fingers had tightened around the paper cup she still held. Which she held like an anchor. As if this simple object kept her on her feet.

The minutes continued to tick by. Three fifteen. Three twenty. Three twenty-five. Then, at the end of the corridor, a familiar silhouette appeared.

Carina straightened up immediately. Doctor Lucy Field - Maya's gynecologist. Carina had stood up before she could think.

- "Doctor Field!"

Lucy had stopped when she saw her.

- "Carina."

The young woman had felt her heart quicken.

- "Did they call you into surgery?"

Lucy had frowned slightly.

- "No."

Carina had turned whiter, so Lucy had immediately added:

"But I can go and see what's going on."

Carina had nodded quickly.

- "Please."

Lucy had laid a reassuring hand on her shoulder.

- "I'll be right back."

Then she'd disappeared behind the block doors - after swiping her badge on the reader. The silence had become overwhelming again.

The brunette was now staring at the clock. Every second seemed to last an eternity. Lucia had said nothing. She had simply kept her hand in her daughter's - again. The coffee now on the floor.

The minutes had continued to pass. One minute. Then two. Then five. Then ten. Fifteen minutes later, the OR doors opened again. Lucy Field had returned to the hallway and Carina had stood up immediately.

- "Well?"

Lucy had sketched a tired little smile.

- "The operation took a little longer than expected."

Carina had held her breath. Then Dr. Field had added softly:

"But Maya's a fighter."

The Italian's heart had finally started beating again.

"Dr. King is almost finished."

Then Lucy had laid a hand on her shoulder.

"It should be over soon."

OB-GYN had then continued down the corridor. Carina had remained motionless for a few seconds. Her heart was still beating too fast.

Lucia had glanced at the clock. Three thirty. Just a little longer. Just a few more minutes. And the operation should be over.

However, the minutes had started ticking again. Three thirty. Three thirty-five. And Carina hadn't managed to sit down again. She had remained standing by the block doors, arms crossed against her chest, as if trying to keep her heart inside.

Her mother remained seated. But her gaze never left her daughter. Carina stared at the doors. Without blinking. As if she could open them by sheer force of will.

Three forty. Three forty-five.

The corridor was silent again. Then, suddenly, the sound of hurried footsteps echoed at the end of the corridor. A nurse was running in. He was pushing a metal cart.

Carina had barely looked at first. But something had caught her eye. Pouches. Blood bags. Red. Lined up on the cart.

Her heart had stopped. The nurse had slowed as he approached the OR doors. The automatic doors opened. Then a nurse had appeared on the other side.

- "Is this for block two?!"

- "Yes!" The nurse had replied immediately.

Block two.

The world had stopped. Carina had felt her stomach twist violently.

Block two. Maya's block.

She hadn't even realized she'd taken a step forward. Then another.

- "No..."

The word had come out on its own. Barely audible and Lucia had stood up immediately.

- "Carina-"

But the young woman had already stopped hearing her. Her eyes were fixed on the blood bags the nurse was making disappear behind the doors.

The doors had closed. And the corridor was silent again. But in Carina's head, only one thought was running.

And again.And again.And again.

Hanno bisogno di sangue. [They need blood.]

Her legs had suddenly seemed to give way. She had put her hand on the wall next to the door so as not to fall, and her mother had arrived at her side immediately. Catching her too.

- "Tesoro!"

The medical student had shaken her head.

- "Era per il secondo blocco." [It was for block two.]

Her voice trembled.

"Era per il secondo blocco." [It was for block two.]

Lucia had placed her hands on her shoulders.

- "Questo non significa necessariamente che-" [It doesn't necessarily mean-]

- "Sì!"

Carina had looked up at her. Her gaze was filled with raw fear.

"Questo vuol dire che sta avendo un'emorragia." [It means she's hemorrhaging.]

Her breathing had quickened.

"Questo significa che c'è un intoppo." [It means there's a complication.]

She'd felt her head wobble.

"Questo vuol dire che non sta bene." [It means she's not well.]

Lucia had felt her own heart clench. Because her daughter was right. But she couldn't tell her that.

- "Durante gli interventi chirurgici capita spesso di dover ricorrere alle trasfusioni." [Transfusions often happen during surgeries.]

Carina had shaken her head.

- "Non così!" [Not like that!]

Her voice had broken.

"Non è urgente." [Not in a hurry.]

She had turned her head toward the block doors. Her eyes were now glistening with tears.

"Sta morendo lì dentro." [She's dying in there.]

- "No!"

Lucia's voice had been firm. She had caught her daughter's face between her hands.

"Mi senti? No!" [Can you hear me? No!]

But Carina was trembling. Lucia had placed her forehead against hers.

"Maya è forte." [Maya est forte.]

Carina had closed her eyes. A tear rolled down her cheek. And behind the white doors of the operating room, the surgery continued.

Se muore... Allora ci saranno due tombe da scavare. [If she dies... Then there will be two graves to dig].
