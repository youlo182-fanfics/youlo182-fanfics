The doors to the operating rooms remained closed for several minutes, and Lucia finally sat her daughter down.

Once settled, Carina didn't even dare to move again. Her eyes had been fixed on those same white doors for so long that she felt they were part of the décor.

Lucia continued to hold her hand. Neither of them dared speak again. Then finally, after several dozen minutes, the doors opened.

Carina stood up immediately. Her heart was beating so fast it felt as if it would burst in her chest. Charlotte King had come out. She was still in her scrubs.

The blue cap still covered her hair and her mask hung around her neck. But that wasn't what the Italian was looking at. It was her face. She knew that surgeons learned to control their facial expressions. But she also knew that some looks were impossible to hide.

Carina felt her stomach knot.

- "Doctor King..." She said in a trembling voice.

"How is she?"

Charlotte had stopped in front of them. Her eyes rested first on Carina. Then on Lucia. She had slowly removed her cap, and the gesture had seemed to last an eternity. Then she had spoken.

- "The surgery's over."

The brunette's heart had missed a beat.

"She's alive."

And then the Sicilian's legs had suddenly given way. Lucia had just had time to hold her back - the doctor had also reached out a hand toward her. A sob escaped Carina. But Charlotte hadn't finished, and her gaze had become more serious.

"However... the end of the operation was more complicated than expected."

The silence had fallen immediately. Carina had looked up at her.

- "What does that mean...?"

Charlotte had taken a slow breath.

- "The disease had spread further than the tests showed."

Carina had felt her throat tighten. Dr. King had continued softly:

"We had to remove more tissue than expected."

Lucia had closed her eyes briefly and Charlotte had resumed:

"The hysterectomy was indeed total."

Carina hadn't reacted. Not yet. The onco-gynecologist was still talking.

"The uterus."

She had paused.

"Both ovaries."

Another pause.

"The fallopian tubes."

Carina felt the words drop like stones. But Charlotte clearly hadn't finished.

"We also removed several infiltrating lesions."

She'd looked Carina straight in the eye.

"The operation was long because some of the lesions were close to the intestine."

Lucia had swallowed and Charlotte had added calmly:

"But we managed to remove all the visible disease."

Charlotte had nodded.

- "Yes."

Then her voice had softened:

"Now the priority is for her to recover."

The brunette was still trembling.

- "Can I see her?"

The surgeon had shaken her head gently.

- "Not yet. She's in recovery."

Then she'd added more gently:

"She's still sleeping."

Carina had closed her eyes. The tears were still flowing. But this time, she was breathing. Maya was alive.

The recovery room was bathed in soft light. Not totally dark. Not totally lit. Just enough to keep an eye on the patients. The monitors beeped regularly. A calm rhythm. Reassuring.

Carina had been sitting beside the bed for almost an hour now. Charlotte had finally agreed to let her in for a few minutes - and she'd probably left it there. Perhaps on purpose...

« She's still asleep. »

That was what she'd told her. So Carina waited. Her fingers rested gently on her girlfriend's hand. The blonde's skin was warm. Alive. And that was all that mattered.

For a long moment, nothing had happened. Then Maya's fingers had moved slightly and the Italian had straightened up immediately.

- "Bambina..."

Her voice had been a whisper. The Olympic champion's eyelids had quivered. Once. Then a second. And finally her eyes had opened. It took them a few seconds to adjust to the light. Then they looked for something. For someone.

Carina.

When their eyes had finally met, Maya's lips had sketched a very slight smile. A tired smile.

- "Hey..."

Her voice was hoarse, barely audible. And Carina had felt the tears rise immediately.

- "Hey."

She'd squeezed her hand gently:

"You're awake."

Maya had tried to speak again, but her throat was dry. Carina had grabbed the small wet stick lying on the table and gently moistened her lips. The blonde had sighed lightly.

- "I'm..."

She'd searched for her words.

"Alive?"

Carina had let out a shaky little laugh.

- "Yes."

A tear had rolled down her cheek.

"You're alive."

- "Okay..." Maya said before clicking her tongue against the roof of her mouth. Still thirsty.

"Because you look like an angel."

Then her eyes closed for a moment. As if she were savoring this simple piece of information.

Then they'd opened again.

"Told you."

Carina had frowned slightly.

- "Cosa?"

Maya had murmured weakly:

- "That I'd come back."

Carina had rested her forehead against her hand. Her shoulders trembled slightly.

- "Sì."

Her voice was broken.

"You came back."

The Italian had tried to suppress her urge to cry. And she'd lifted her head.

A soft silence had settled between them. Then the blonde had breathed a little more deeply. And her face had changed slightly. The pain was beginning to reawaken and her girlfriend had noticed it immediately.

"Does it hurt?"

Maya had shrugged one shoulder ever so slightly.

- "A little."

- "I know Maya Bishop's «a little»." Said the Italian, pragmatically.

Carina had pressed the call button and a nurse had arrived a few seconds later.

She checked the vitals. Then adjusted the painkiller drip. Maya's face relaxed gently and the nurse smiled.

- "The operation went well."

She had looked at Carina.

"But she's going to need some rest."

Then she'd gone back and silence had returned. Maya was still looking at Carina. Her fingers had tightened slightly around hers.

- "You've been crying." It wasn't a question. Nor a statement. Nor an observation. Perhaps a fact.

Carina had sniffed slightly.

- "What do you think?"

A tired little smile had appeared on Maya's lips. Then her eyes had grown more serious.

- "Baby..."

The young woman had raised her head.

- "Yes?"

Maya had looked at her for a long time. As if she wanted to make sure she was really there.

- "I'm sorry."

Carina had immediately shaken her head.

- "No."

She'd put her hand against her cheek:

"You have no right to apologize."

Maya had whispered:

- "Yes I do. For everything."

Carina knew she wasn't just talking about the operation. But she hadn't said anything. Not yet. She'd simply kissed her forehead gently.

Her voice was soft.

"Right now... you rest."

Maya's eyes were already beginning to close again. The anesthesia was still there. So was the fatigue. And just before going back to sleep, she'd whispered:

- "Stay with me."

Carina had squeezed her hand.

- "Always."

And Maya had gone back to sleep.

Morning light filtered through the blinds of the hospital room. Pale light - calm light - reflected against the pastel-colored walls of the oncology ward.

Maya opened her eyes slowly. This time, her head was clearer. Less heavy. The monitor's regular beeps were still there. The white ceiling too. The hospital smell as unpleasant as ever. And a strange sensation in her body. A heaviness. A tension in her abdomen.

She'd moved slightly and the pain came immediately. Not violent pain - but deep, heavy. Her breath had caught for a moment and a hand had immediately squeezed hers.

"Hey." Carina said. Still there, sitting beside the bed.

Her hair was tied up in a hurry. Her eyes were rimmed. She probably hadn't slept. Maya had turned her head slightly toward her.

- "You're still here."

Carina had sketched a tired little smile.

- "I told you I'd stay."

Maya had inhaled softly. Then her gaze had slid to her own body. The drip. The wires. The bandage she could imagine under her gown, on her belly.

Memories of the day before had slowly returned. The diagnosis. The signature. The operating room. Her brows had furrowed slightly.

- "Baby..."

- "Yes my love?"

The blonde had seemed to be searching for her words. Then she'd finally whispered:

- "They've... they've, taken everything away."

It wasn't a question. It was a realization. Carina had felt her heart clench. She hadn't answered right away, and the blonde hadn't looked away. She'd been waiting for an answer.

- "The uterus."

She had paused. Just as Charlotte had done with her. She had to give her lover time to take in every word that was about to leave her lips.

"Ovaries."

Another pause, but for Maya, the wait was unbearable.

- "Everything?"

Carina had squeezed her hand. She'd swallowed before answering.

- "Yes."

The word had been almost inaudible. Maya had looked away and up at the ceiling. For a long time. A very long time. Her eyes were shining but she wasn't crying yet. Then she'd whispered:

- "Then I'll really never be able to get pregnant."

The sentence had come out softly. Like a scientific fact. As if she were announcing a diagnosis to someone else, and Carina had felt the tears rise immediately.

- "Bambina..."

- "I knew it."

Her voice had begun to tremble more.

"I knew it was going to happen."

"But knowing... and..."

She hadn't even managed to finish her sentence, and then Carina had put her hand on her cheek.

- "Look at me."

The blonde had reopened her eyes and the brunette was now weeping openly.

"And that's all that matters."

Maya had whispered weakly:

- "I'm alive for you."

Carina had shaken her head immediately.

- "For us."

"Can you feel that?"

"That's because you're here."

Maya's eyes had filled with new tears.

- "I wanted... to give you..."

Her voice had broken. The Sicilian knew exactly what she was going to say.

"A baby."

Carina had shaken her head.

- "You've already given me everything I want."

Maya had breathed softly.

- "What?"

Carina had kissed her fingers.

- "Your life."

There had been a silence, then she'd added softly:

"And we still have the embryos."

Maya's eyes had blinked. As if her brain were reconnecting the information.

The stimulation.The puncture.The frozen embryos.

Carina had smiled through her tears.

"See?"

She'd rested her forehead against Maya's.

"We haven't lost anything, Bambina. Today, we won. We've won another battle toward the end of this war."

Maya had closed her eyes and a tear had slipped. But this time, she was smiling - very slightly - but she was smiling.

- "Future Doctor DeLuca-Bishop..."

Carina had sniffed.

- "Exactly!"

Maya had murmured weakly:

- "You'll never let go...?"

- "Never. I want to call myself that. Despite our fathers. Despite it being their name. I want to call myself that."

And Maya had clenched her fingers then.

- "So, one day. We'll call ourselves that."

Carina had kissed her forehead gently.

- "And it will be forever."

A few hours later, the room was quiet. Maya was awake now. Tired, but conscious. Carina was still sitting by the bed. Her hand gently encircled that of her lover. Lucia had gone to take care of her, despite her light sleep.

Someone had knocked gently on the door, and then they had seen Dr. King enter, without even waiting for the invitation. She wouldn't risk interrupting anything.

She'd changed out of her scrubs. She wore a white blouse, civilian clothes underneath and a folder under her arm. Her gaze was calm, but serious.

- "Hello, Maya... Hello Carina."

The blonde had turned her head slightly.

- "Doctor King." The American said, while the Italian merely nodded.

Charlotte approached the bed. She had placed the file on the shelf.

- "How are you feeling?"

Maya had shrugged a shoulder slightly.

- "Like someone decided to rearrange my internal organs."

The oncologist had sketched a small smile.

- "That's pretty much what happened..."

Carina had squeezed Maya's hand gently. Her humor, which had once charmed her.

Made her laugh, tended to hurt her now. But with any luck, that was in the past. Charlotte had taken the folder and leaned lightly against the table.

"I'll explain exactly what we found and what we had to do."

The silence in the room had now become heavy. The blonde had slowly nodded.

- "Okay."

The doctor had begun calmly.

- "The tests already showed a progression of the disease. But during the operation, we found that some of the lesions were more extensive than expected."

Carina had felt her fingers tighten slightly on those of her lover. Charlotte, for her part, had continued, as imperturbable as ever:

"We removed the uterus."

She paused briefly. As if to gauge the Olympic champion's reaction.

"Both ovaries and the fallopian tubes."

Maya had closed her eyes briefly. She knew it - but hearing it out loud again, was still different. Charlotte had then continued:

"Several lesions had also infiltrated the pelvic peritoneum. We were able to remove them."

She had turned the chart slightly towards Maya.

"Some areas were close to the bowel. Fortunately, we didn't need a bowel resection."

"But the surgery took longer to make sure we removed all the visible disease."

- "And...

And she hemorrhaged... No ? Yesterday... Yesterday I saw a nurse running towards block two with blood bags..." The Italian asked.

Carina's gaze had immediately frozen. Charlotte had maintained that clinical, almost unnerving calm.

She paused.

"We controlled the bleeding. But Maya lost enough blood to require a transfusion."

Silence had fallen. Heavy. Dense.

"Multiple units."

But in stage III, there is a risk that some microscopic cancer cells may still be present.

So we're going to start adjuvant treatment to reduce the risk of recurrence."

Adjuvant treatment... What's that again... ?

Charlotte had read her gaze.

"Medicated chemotherapy." she said, as if she'd read her eyes.

One tablet a day, organized in twenty-eight-day cycles - not all cycles are alike. Some cycles you'll have to take the treatment for a fortnight, then nothing more. Another cycle will be every other week... And so on. We adjust with each new cycle. And we'll do a check-up at the end of each cycle."

There may be some side effects. But it's a protocol that most patients tolerate well."

"Treatment will be tailored to your medical situation."

Without ovaries, the disease should remain inactive. But with hormone therapy, it can slightly reactivate the residues we may have missed..."
