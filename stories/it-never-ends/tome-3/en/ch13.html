The California sun bathed the campus steps, illuminating the golden cobblestones in front of the main amphitheater. Carina had been the last to emerge, her canvas bag slung over her shoulder, her index cards still in her hand.

It had been an elective, an art history course. She'd taken it purely for pleasure, almost out of nostalgia, as it was mainly about Italy. It had, in fact, been the very first classroom she'd entered, four years earlier, her heart full of anger and her soul laden with secrets.

Today, she had closed her notebook with a calm gesture.

Today, she had walked out free.

She had inhaled deeply, feeling the warm wind on her face. A light breeze in her hair. Her phone had vibrated in her back pocket. She pulled it out, frowning. A notification.

« Final results - Bachelor of Science + promotional ranking ».

Her thumb froze for a moment, then she clicked.

There was silence, her ears closed to the outside world, then her heart missed a beat.

« Valedictorian. Mention of excellence. »

Which meant she'd officially been admitted to UWSOM.

Indeed she'd passed her MCAT for medical school entrance and had been accepted to Washington University School Of Medicine on the condition that she passed her Bachelor of Science. Which she had.

Carina had reread the screen several times. There it was. In black letters on a white background. The fruit of her efforts. Her sleepless nights. Of her years of pain, silence and hard work.

She'd done it.

And the first thought that entered her mind... Was... Maya.

Sarebbe stata orgogliosa di me, vero? [She would have been proud of me, wouldn't she?]

She had stood staring at her phone for a long moment, her lips parted, her thumb hovering over the « call » button on Maya's contact sheet. But she hadn't pressed it.

Not yet, anyway.

Instead, she'd dialed her mother's number.

- "Mamma... sono stata rapita." ["Mamma... I've been taken."]

After the call with her mother, which she'd continued until she returned to her student room, she'd typed a message, just one, and sent it to Graziella:

Carina: Ho appena ricevuto i risultati! Sono il primo della classe. Mi iscrivo ufficialmente all'UWMOS. [I've just received the results! I'm top of my class. I'm officially returning to UWMOS].

The next morning, Andrea met her in front of the student residence. He had parked his car on the other side of the courtyard, and waited stretching, his Stanford cap screwed on his head.

- "do you want me to take the suitcases or the boxes?" he challenged, catching sight of her.

- "The boxes, please. The suitcases are already in the trunk." she replied with a smile.

They had gone up and down the stairs two, three times. There wasn't much left, just one last box, sitting in the center of the room.

Carina had entered her room one last time. She had looked at the empty walls, the unmade bed, the little shelf that had held her medical books, and the corner where she had cried for months after being forced to leave everything behind.

She bent down, grabbed the last box, hugged it to her, and backed up to the door. Before closing it, she took one last look around the room.

It had come full circle.

She was no longer the broken girl who'd been forced to come here.

She had become the one who left by choice.

She pulled open the door and the latch clicked softly.

Downstairs, Andrea was waiting for her, leaning against his car, bottle of water in hand.

- "Well? Ready?" he asked with a smile.

Carina had taken a step towards him, the keys to her apartment still in her hand.

- "I'm ready to live my life. To start this new adventure."

Andrea had burst out laughing.

- "I was talking about the twenty-hour drive back to Seattle..."

She'd looked up at him, a softer, deeper smile at the corner of her lips.

Then she'd planted her gaze in his and answered, simply:

- "back home."

Maya had woken up suddenly, short of breath, her stomach twisted. The pain had gripped her at once, acidic, brutal, radiating from her lower abdomen to her spine.

She had sat up slowly in bed, leaning forward, her arms hugging her belly as if to prevent her insides from tearing open. She had closed her eyes, breathed in and out, tried to remember all the breathing exercises her coach, Robert Sullivan, had shown her when she injured her thigh. Robert Sullivan who had followed her to Chula Vista.

But it made no difference.

She was used to it. She'd always had painful, unpredictable, sometimes crippling periods. But this time, it was something else.

More violent. Deeper. Almost unbearable.

And she had no desire to use Carina's technique. Not today. She wasn't supposed to think about her. She shouldn't watch that video and lose herself in the past, when this day was supposed to be about her future.

She finally got up, slowly, gropingly.

She had prepared a hot water bottle and placed it on her lower abdomen, curled up on her bed, jaws clenched, eyes fixed on the white ceiling of the Olympic room.

Not today. Not today, damn it.

She'd muttered through gritted teeth:

- "you've worked too hard to be eaten by a temperamental uterus, Bishop."

An hour later, she'd slipped on her Team USA tracksuit, tied her hair back into a high-tail elastic, camouflaged the dark circles under her eyes with a hint of concealer. She'd slipped a painkiller into her jacket pocket, just in case. And she smiled in the mirror.

A tense smile. A war smile.

She walked towards the changing room with the gait of a wounded lioness, her step determined, her gaze empty.

The locker room was quiet at this hour. A few relay teammates were laughing further on, already made up, already ready to become national heroines.

Maya had settled down alone, on a bench near the lockers. She had closed her eyes for a moment, her headphones in her ears.

But the pain was still there, dull, throbbing, twisting her lower abdomen at regular intervals.

She pressed a hand against her abs.

Hang in there. Just a few hours. Then you can collapse.

A figure had approached. White sneakers, high socks.

The voice of Shantel, her fifteen-hundred-meter teammate, had broken the silence:

- "hey... What's up? You don't look... very Bishopian this morning."

Maya had slowly raised her head. She had sketched a smile. Weak, but present.

- "I didn't work ten years to let a cramp steal my medal."

Shantel had smiled, a little worried, but admiring.

- "You're going to get it, that fucking medal. And then you'll get to roll around in a ball with pizza and Netflix for two days."

Maya had nodded, forced another smile.

- "If I win, you better order me the pizza. I might not even have the strength to walk anymore."

Shantel had laughed, patted her gently on the shoulder.

- "then I guess it's safe to say I'll have to order it from you!"

Then she'd walked away, a smile on her face.

Maya had found herself alone. Again.

She'd lowered her head to her shorts, slowly tugged at the waist to replace the hot water bottle discreetly wedged against her belly.

Her gaze caught on her shoes. Her laces. Black, thin, perfectly tightened.

Every detail counted.

She put both hands on her thighs. Firm. Stable.

Then she'd whispered, to herself alone:

- "you can do this. You've been through worse. Today, you run. For yourself. For what you're worth. For what you've survived."

Come on... Come on, Maya. Today's your big day. You've been waiting for this... your whole life. Your whole life. Sixteen years you've been running... Today's the day to give it your all. The doctor said it was just a painful period. More painful than average, but just painful periods, nothing more.

Los Angeles, Olympic Stadium Twenty-seventh of July two thousand and eight, five forty-five. A few minutes before the ten-thousand-meter final.

Carina was alone in the upper bleachers, one row back. High enough to see the whole track. Low enough to see Maya's face as she crossed the line.

She wore a beige cap and large sunglasses, not to hide herself, no. Not really. But to keep this moment to herself. Like a precious secret.

Down below, she'd seen them. Andy, Vic, Jack, Ryan, Jo, Travis, Taryn... They were all there, even Mark and Jackson. Grouped together, in red, white and blue scarves, shouting slogans, waving flags. Andy even carried a « Run Bishop Run! » sign that she'd obviously made by hand.

Carina had smiled, moved. They'd never stopped being there for her. Even once they'd all gone their separate ways after high school.

Her phone had vibrated. The « Forever Young » group chat was already coming to life.

Andy: Seriously, you should have come, Carina. It's crazy here!

Jack: There's sun, hot dogs and Maya Bishop in Warrior Princess mode.

Taryn: Is there a seat next to me if you ever want to teleport?

Amelia: I wore lip gloss on purpose to interview her after the medal. Amelia: (and to make my lips slide over Maya's!)

Carina breathed in softly before answering.

Carina: I'd love to... but I'm working at Seattle Grace this week, just to help my mom out a bit. Carina: give her a kiss for me if you see her first. And... good luck to Maya.

She had closed the application. Gently. Her heart was already pounding too hard.

Because she was lying.

She wasn't in Seattle. She wasn't at Seattle Grace.

She wasn't a thousand miles from Maya.

She was there. Right there. In the bleachers. Waiting for her to arrive, finally. Sitting in a bleacher heated by the California sun, surrounded by strangers who had no idea her heart was about to explode.

And then... The music had changed. The crowd held its breath.

The runners had begun to enter the track.

And Maya had appeared. At last.

Carina had stopped breathing for a second. Maybe two. The world had slowed down. Her gaze fixed on a figure she would have recognized in a thousand. Her posture, her gait, the way she tucked her hair, still a little short in places, behind her ear.

There she was.

Sublime. More beautiful than she remembered. Or maybe it was lack that made her even more dazzling.

Or love.

Or both.

She was wearing the colors of the American team. Red, white and blue. A fitted tank top, tight shorts. Her name emblazoned on her chest. Her gaze fixed straight ahead.

« BISHOP » and « 1717 ».

But before long, Carina felt something. A twinge in her stomach. A silent alarm.

Maya's features were drawn. Her skin paler. Tired eyes.

She was smiling, of course. But it wasn't the real smile.

Not the one that had melted Carina's heart for years.

And above all... there was that gesture.

Discreet.

Her hand that had rested for a second on her belly, like a reflex.

Carina swallowed. She knew her. She knew. She could see. Something was wrong. And suddenly, the lump in her throat had settled for good.

Correrà in quel modo? Con quel dolore negli occhi? [She's going to run like this? with this pain in her eyes?]

Carina had placed a hand on her chest, as if to calm her racing heart. She curled up a little more in her seat, her eyes riveted on the starting line.

She couldn't help her. She couldn't get off, couldn't run with her, couldn't hold her hand. Let alone take her pain as she had so often done.

But she'd be there. Just there. To love her in silence. Down to the last metre.

Los Angeles, Olympic Stadium Ten-thousand-meter final, July twenty-seventh, two thousand and twenty-eight, five past six.

The stadium's silence was but a mirage.

It was already vibrating under the electricity of expectation, under the murmurs of spectators, under the weight of cameras pointed at the starting line.

Maya Bishop had stepped forward.

Her hands rested on her hips. Her gaze had swept the track one last time. She didn't smile. Not this time. She was focused.

She closed her eyes. Inhaled deeply. Exhaled slowly.

You've done this a thousand times before. It's only a race. Just one more.

But it wasn't true. It wasn't « just a race ».

It was the race. The race of a lifetime.

Sixteen years, Maya... Don't screw it up today...

Since she was six. Since her first cloth bib. Since the first plastic medal her father hung proudly on the fridge. Every stride. Every failure. Every pain. Every wake-up call at five in the morning, rain, shine, snow. Thirty degrees or minus five. Everything had led her here.

And today, she was bleeding. Hurt. Too much pain. Her belly was a silent inferno. An inner storm she could no longer control. But she hadn't let on. Not to her coach. Not to her teammates. Not to her opponents. And certainly not to herself.

A hand rested on her shoulder.

- "ready?"

It was one of her competitors but at the same time one of her teammates, a sprinter, confident, radiant.

Maya had smiled. Just a little.

- "More than ever." She replied before they got into position. She mustn't let it be known that she was in trouble. That she was suffering. She might have been her teammate, but right now she was mostly her competitor.

The pistol had gone off.

And she was gone. She was gone.

From the very first meters, she'd sensed it wasn't going to be easy. Her muscles were heavy. Her breath was short. But she'd found a rhythm. A refuge. A bubble.

For the first few minutes, she'd just kept up. Keep up. Gauging. But by the fifth lap, something inside her had lit up.

Not pain. No.

Fury. The rage to live. The certainty that she wouldn't leave here without that medal around her neck.

Lap after lap. Breath after breath.

She'd passed.

One.

Then two.

Then three.

The crowd roared. But Maya heard nothing. Nothing but the sound of her heart. And that little voice in her head, familiar and distant.

Run, baby Bear. Run to me.

Carina.

She couldn't see her. But she felt her. Like an invisible presence at the end of the finish line.

With three laps to go, her body had wobbled. Her vision had blurred for a fraction of a second. But she'd bitten the inside of her cheek. She'd swallowed the scream.

Not now, she wouldn't.

With two laps to go, she was in the lead. And no one was following her anymore.

The last lap... had been a mirage. She couldn't feel her legs. She couldn't feel her belly. She couldn't feel anything. But one of her competitors was gaining on her. She was coming up behind her.

The blonde flew, and then saw the line. She concentrated with all her might, her body already on fire, and crossed it. Seconds before the next.

A flutter of eyelashes. A breath. A victory.

The stadium exploded. Shouts, applause, flags. Flashbulbs. Cameras.

Maya had collapsed on the runway, on her knees, panting, arms trembling.

Her name rang out over the loudspeakers. Her face had appeared large on the stadium screens.

« MAYA BISHOP! MAYA BISHOP IS GOLD MEDALIST! GOLD MEDALIST FOR THE TEN-THOUSAND-METER RACE! »

And then, only then, had she cried.

Not from pain. Not from exhaustion.

But joy.

Pure. Immense. Devouring.

She'd cried when she was twenty-one, almost twenty-two, her face raised to the California sky, her arms raised as if to embrace the world.

And she'd cried all the more when she'd seen the time displayed on the screen. Where her face showed her in close-up. She was a gold medallist and had broken her own record.

Carina, in the stands, had brought her hands to her mouth. Tears rolled noiselessly down her cheeks. She was no longer a woman. She was a wave of pride.

Her heart beat in time with Maya's. She could see her.

She could see her. Her triumph. Her grace. Her invisible pain.

And then Maya had stood up. She had turned her head toward the bleachers. She'd seen Andy. Vic. Travis. Jack. Taryn. Dean... All of them, waving their arms, jumping up and down, crying with joy. And as he reached the end of her friends' line of presence, a sparkle passed through her eyes. A fleeting sparkle. A crack.

She'd seen everyone who loved her. And yet, something was missing. Someone was missing.

And Carina understood.

She understood that even after the gold... even after the glory... Maya was still looking for her. Her gaze swept the rows. But she couldn't see her.

Lei pensa che io non sia qui... Certo che pensa che io non sia qui... Ma se... Se fossi venuto prima... Per vederla prima... Allora forse... Forse avrei potuto alleviare il suo dolore... Crede davvero che io non sia qui per lei... Che non sono qui per l'amore della mia vita... [She thinks I'm not here... Of course she thinks I'm not here... But if... If I'd come sooner... Earlier to see her... Then... Then maybe I could have taken away her pain... She really thinks I'm not here for her... That I'm not here for the love of my life...]

And her heart had exploded again.

But this time, it wasn't from pain. It was love. Maya was looking for her.

The stadium was still buzzing with adrenalin.

Maya had barely had time to remove her top to remain in her bra before the journalists rushed towards her. Microphones tense. Cameras on. Overexcited voices.

- "Maya! How does it feel to win gold at home, here in Los Angeles?!"

- "what was your state of mind in the last round?!"

- "are you thinking about Brisbane two-thousand-thirty-two? Or even a career in other types of racing?"

She had smiled. Sincere but calm. Not exhilarated. Just... there.

- "It's a dream. A child's dream. And today, it came true. I don't realize it yet. But... I'm proud."

Her coach, Robert, came up behind her, eyes shining.

- "she gave it her all. Even injured, she would have fought. Maya runs with her heart. She always has."

Then it had been her parents. Lane, arms already open, had hugged her so tightly she'd lost her breath. Katherine's eyes were flushed. Mase too, for once.

Andy had caught her between two reporters. She looked proud, overexcited. But above all, deeply moved.

- "You're incredible, you know that?! And Carina even wished you good luck on the group chat. I don't know if you saw."

Maya had looked up, just for a second. Just a second.

- "No... I didn't have time to look..."

Andy had scrutinized her, for a split second, then simply smiled.

- "bah I'm telling you. She's been thinking about you."

Maya hadn't answered. Because in this whirlwind of light, microphones, tears and gold, a single silence resounded.

Carina wasn't there.

After the medal ceremony, the photos, the shouts, she had finally been allowed to return to the checkroom.

Alone. Alone to digest that the goal of the last sixteen years had come to an end.

There, Maya had sat on the varnished wooden bench, her back against the wall, her hands resting on her knees.

She breathed. A long breath.

The medal rested on her chest, against her suit, still damp with sweat and emotion. She grasped it between her fingers. Turned it over. Looked at it as if it were a fragile thing.

She'd won. Olympic champion. A dream come true.

She had closed her eyes. And in the darkness of her eyelids, a face had appeared.

Carina.

Another dream. A dream never fulfilled. A dream she'd had to deny, that she'd torn from her heart to run straight, to stay focused on her finish line. And she had done so for five years.

She had seen her smile. She'd seen her in the stands. But it was an illusion. Because she wasn't there.

She hadn't congratulated her. She hadn't called out her name. She hadn't run to her. Not today. Not like in dreams.

Then Maya had opened her eyes again. She rested the medal against her chest. She'd exhaled again. And she'd forced herself to smile. She had sacrificed so much, she couldn't let her absence destroy everything.

That dream, at least, I fulfilled.

She got up to go to the shower. A moment to herself. A well-deserved moment.

And she was out of the locker room. Under the spotlight. Under the applause. But inside her, something deeply silent was still whispering:

You could have been there. And I would have run for you.

The bleachers had gradually emptied, but the crowd gathered around Maya never seemed to go away.

She stood behind a long barrier in the colors of the U.S. Olympic Committee, surrounded by flags and cheers. A huge line stretched out in front of her, made up of fans of all ages, brandishing T-shirts, caps, photos, souvenirs. Everyone wanted a signature. A photo. A moment with the new athletics legend.

Maya smiled. Endlessly. She wrote her name, signed, took selfies, thanked. She was attentive, gentle, warm. She laughed sometimes, but never too loudly. And with every smile she offered, a part of her... seemed elsewhere.

In the crowd, not far away, Carina was staring at her. Her fingers were clenched on a « Women Athletism JO 2028 LA » T-shirt she wanted signed. Trying to pass herself off as an ordinary fan. Wanting to surprise her. Her heart was beating too fast. Much too fast.

She'd seen her as a child, then on the big screens. But to see her there... up close. It was too much.

Maya was sublime. Tanned by the California sun. Dazzling under the flashbulbs. Elegant even in a USA Track & Field track suit.

È più bella che mai... O forse è solo la mancanza a parlare... [She's more beautiful than ever... Or maybe it's just the lack that speaks...]

Maya had looked for her after the race, scanning the stands.

Not for a second had the Italian thought she'd been seen. But now that Maya was just a few yards away, she couldn't help hoping. Hope for what, exactly? A glance? A sign? For her to feel her presence?

She'd wanted to tell her how proud she was. To shout that she was there. That she'd always been there, somewhere in the silences.

But Maya didn't look up. She remained focused on the crowd, on the smiles around her.

So Carina stayed back. Invisible. She took out her phone to take a photo. Just one. To keep a trace, a memory, a proof of her coming. But she'd taken the photo, and the image that followed the split second when the screen went black as she took the shot had taken her breath away. It had broken her.

Another young woman had approached Maya. Brunette. Slender. An athletic uniform still slung over her shoulders, her hair tied in a tight braid.

A girl from the American relay team.

She came up behind Maya, saying nothing. Just a tender smile at the corner of her lips. She had slid her hand down the champion's back, like a familiar, gentle gesture. Then she leaned over and placed a kiss on her cheek, almost at the corner of her lips. Then she applied gentle pressure to the blonde's side. Her lips had rested close to the Olympic champion's ear and she had whispered something and Maya had smiled. A genuine smile. Not forced. Not a facade. A tender smile. And a look. Her gaze, hers.

Carina's heart had broken. She hadn't screamed. She hadn't moved. She just stood there, petrified.

Maya ha qualcuno. [Maya has someone].

It wasn't a guess. It wasn't a fear. It was obvious.

The hand on the back. The kiss. The smile.

And Carina had finally turned away when the woman's hand left Maya's body and she winked at her.

The Sicilian was breathless. Her throat was tight as ever. She had turned and fled, her phone still in her hand, forgotten. She had pushed her way through the crowd, becoming invisible, a ghost in a jubilant world.

She wasn't crying. Not yet. Not there. Not in front of them.

As for Maya, she felt a shiver run down her spine. Like a breath. She looked up. Her pupils had scanned the crowd.

Something... Her gaze had stopped on an empty space. Her eyebrows had furrowed. A figure she'd thought she'd glimpsed. A lock of hair. A strange sensation. As if her heart was calling to her in reverse.

But it was too late.

Carina was already gone. Gone without a sound. And with her, something had gone.
