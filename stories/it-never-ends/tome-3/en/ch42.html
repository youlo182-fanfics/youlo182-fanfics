A few days later, the sun hadn't yet risen when Maya opened her eyes. The room was bathed in the bluish gloom of dawn, and only Carina's quiet, barely audible breathing reminded her that she was not alone in this bed. She stretched in silence, then leaned forward slightly to observe her companion's relaxed features. Even asleep, Carina seemed to radiate the natural gentleness that made her heart skip a beat.

Maya smiled silently.

It was the day of her last EMT shift before graduation. The fifth. Another moment too long without her lover: twenty-four hours. Even though she'd put on this uniform many times before, there was a special kind of emotion in the air. A promise of fulfillment.

She gently got out of bed so as not to wake Carina, grabbed her sweater and tiptoed out of the room to the bathroom. But no sooner had she set foot in the kitchen than she realized she wasn't the only one already awake.

Carina was there. In her pyjamas, wrapped up in a zipped jacket too big for her, her hair up in a loose bun. She held Maya's mug in her hands, the one she'd given her at the very start of her training. The one where she could write her words of love. The smell of fresh coffee wafted through the air.

The blonde had frowned and the brunette had looked up at her, a gentle smile at the corner of her lips.

- "did you really think I was going to let you go without coffee this morning?"

Maya had approached, touched.

- "You were still asleep, I wanted to leave you sleep..."

- "impossible to sleep. Not today."

She had handed her the mug delicately. Maya had seized it, and immediately turned it over to see what love note her lover had written this morning.

« Last stretch. I'm so proud of you. Come back to me in one piece, my Supergirl. ♡♡♡♡ - C. »

Maya had felt her throat tighten. She hadn't asked for anything. And Carina, as always, had guessed. That need for discreet encouragement. That mixture of pride and pressure.

She had looked up at her, moved.

- "I love you..." she breathed, resting her forehead against hers.

Carina had closed her eyes, savoring the warmth of the moment.

- "I love you even more. And tomorrow, when you come home, I promise a miniature party. Champagne, pizza and... maybe a massage if you're nice..."

- "You mean if I survive?"

Carina had opened her eyes, falsely threatening.

- "that's not an option. You survive, punto!"

They'd stood there for a while, silent, holding each other, looking at each other, saying a thousand things without a word.

Then Maya had finally left. Mug in hand. Heart swelling. And in the doorway, Carina had stood for a moment, watching her descend the steps, shoulders straight, head held high. Her champion. Her lover. Her future heroine.

The sky was gray, cottony. Typical of a Seattle winter morning.

Maya had scarcely stepped through the doors of the intervention center before eyes were focused on her. It wasn't hostility. Rather... a form of recognition. And curiosity. A stocky man in a black T-shirt and fatigues approached her with a ready smile on his lips.

- "You must be Bishop."

- "Maya, yes." she replied, extending her hand.

- "Captain Hendricks. Welcome to 82. We heard we were getting a... celebrity."

Maya had raised an eyebrow, amused.

- "An EMT trainee, you mean."

- "of course. But also an Olympic medalist. It's not every day you get that combo."

No sooner had she set her things down than a young EMT - younger than her - approached, phone in hand, eyes shining with enthusiasm.

- "is it true you were on the track team in Los Angeles?"

- "yes..."

- "I was at EMT school at the time. I saw you live. You were incredible. Can I... can I take a picture with you?"

Maya had smiled, a little embarrassed, but nodded. One photo, then another, then an autograph scribbled on an inventory notebook... By nine o'clock, she already felt like she'd been more of a star than EMT. But she remained humble. Present. Disciplined.

And thankfully, at ten thirty-seven, the radio had finally crackled.

« Company 82, medical response - man unconscious, possible cardiac arrest. Five hundred and seven, Fifth Avenue. »

Maya had grabbed her procedure bag as a reflex. One of the paramedics had seen the anticipation in her eyes and then nodded positively and Maya had followed the team without another word. As soon as she was finally seated in the ambulance, her eyes lit up. She was no longer a « celebrity ». She was back to being what she wanted to be: Useful. Present. Focused.

Arriving at the scene, the man, in his sixties, lay on the icy sidewalk, pale and motionless. A passer-by was kneeling beside him, still performing a clumsy cardiac massage. Maya knelt down without hesitation.

- "I'll take it from here! Keep talking to the victim if you can."

She had put her hands down, aligned her shoulders and started the compressions. Her rhythm was perfect. Firm. Regular. Her posture impeccable. No sooner had she started than Carter, the paramedic, exchanged a surprised glance with one of his colleagues.

A minute later, the defibrillator was in place. Maya had already untucked the patient's shirt, placed the electrodes, checked breathing.

- "we shock on my cue. Okay?"

- "okay." confirmed one of the EMTs.

A shock. Tension in the air. Then the monitor came to life.

- "sinus rhythm regained" breathed one of the EMTs.

The man had coughed, weakly.

Maya had smiled, kneeling, one hand still on his shoulder.

- "You're safe now. We're going to get you to the hospital. You're going to be okay."

The patient had barely opened his eyes. Just enough to see his blond angel, focused, reassuring.

Back at the company, the garage door had opened. Hendricks had approached and patted Maya twice on the shoulder as she put away her equipment.

- "Not bad, Bishop. Heard you impressed in the field."

She'd looked up.

- "thank you, captain."

- "No, really. You stepped up right away. You didn't wait to be told what to do. You were precise, calm, quick..." made Carter come up beside them.

- "I've been training for a while," Maya replied, modestly.

- "It's not training, it's instinct. You've got it. And I won't lie to you: I've come across some « famous » people. But right now, I want you on my team. I don't care about the name. The composure? Determination? That's what we're looking for," said the captain.

Maya had smiled, touched in spite of herself.

And in her pocket, she had felt the familiar shape of her phone vibrate. She didn't need to pull it out to know what she was reading: a message from Carina. Something simple, sweet.

And she had promised herself that at the end of this shift, it would be in her arms that she would celebrate her success. Because the most beautiful trophy... was her.

The calm of the night was just an illusion. Maya had dozed for barely twenty minutes on a spare bed in the break room. Her t-shirt slightly wrinkled, a plaid over her legs. She had turned to the window, eyelids heavy, when the radio had begun to crackle again:

« Company 82, response - suspected stab wound. two hundred and twenty-eight, Queen Anne Avenue. Floor three, apartment three hundred and five. »

She had leapt out of bed as if she'd never slept a wink. No moans. No complaints. Just the efficiency of a woman who knew she was there for one thing: to save lives.

Putting on her jacket, she climbed into the back of the ambulance with the team. The January chill was biting, but the adrenalin was defrosting her skin.

The apartment smelled of blood and fear. A young woman in her early twenties held her right flank, trembling. An argument with an ex, a bad reaction, a kitchen knife... Words floated in the air without logic.

But Maya only listened to the vital signs.

She had knelt down, freed the blood-soaked tissue, compressed it with precision. Her gaze remained riveted on the victim, whispering reassurances as she guided her colleagues in a low voice. Once again, she had taken the reins as if the operation were her own. Not as someone who was there to learn, but as someone who showed she already knew how to do everything.

"She's in shock. Low blood pressure, tachycardia. She needs to be evacuated. Now."

Just minutes later, they were leaving the building, siren blaring, and in the ambulance, Maya was holding this shaking girl's hand, telling her she'd be okay. And she believed it. Every intervention, every second, she believed it.

It was much later when the team had returned to Company 82, and Maya had been back in the break room, this time leaning against the wall, a bottle of water in her hand, her white shirt stained at the shoulder. She was about to take a quick shower. A cold one, to keep her awake, as fatigue was beginning to pull at the back of her neck. And yet, her fingers naturally reached for her phone.

A message was already waiting for her. A message she'd received only a few minutes ago, even though it was past three.

Cucciola ❤️: Mi manchi tanto... I can't sleep without you. I clutched your pillow all night. But it's not the same. Cucciola ❤️: You are my home, Maya. Come back to me soon.

A sigh had escaped the blonde, soft and painful. She replied immediately.

Baby Bear ❤️: I can't manage without you either. Baby Bear ❤️: I've been running all night between calls, but it's always you I'm looking for in the shadows. Baby Bear ❤️: Tomorrow morning. First train to you. I take off my shoes and slide against you. You'll sleep on my heart. I promise.

And she'd added a photo of herself at that very moment: in her stained shirt, her hair no longer as neatly plastered as it had been that morning and her eyes tired, but full of love.

Seconds later, the answer had arrived:

Cucciola ❤️: You're the most beautiful thing I've ever seen. Cucciola ❤️: And tomorrow morning, I want you naked, warm, in my arms. And I don't want to share you with anyone else for at least twenty-four hours. Not even Andy and Ryan.

Maya had smiled. A real, long smile. The kind that comes from deep in the belly. And she'd rested her head against the wall. Closing her eyes. Putting her hand on her sternum, feeling the two steel plates she'd gotten for Christmas from her lover. Thinking of tomorrow. To Carina. To the promise of a morning in her arms.

The Italian had woken up early. Too early for someone without an alarm, on a Saturday morning. But her body knew: Maya would be home in a few minutes. A smile had crept onto her lips even before she opened her eyes.

She'd gotten up, made two coffees - one in a classic mug, the other in a mug, one she'd given Maya for Christmas, one with a little inscription in gold felt-tip that she renewed when it faded and that she'd found at the Christmas market when they'd strolled with Chiara and Graziella: « Per sempre tua ♡♡ »

She'd double-checked the plaid on the sofa, Maya's pyjamas folded neatly on the bed, just in case she wanted to change right away. But she knew. Her lover was going to want to slide right up against her, naked.

When the sound of the lock clicked, Carina's heart missed a beat.

She'd crossed the corridor barefoot, her girlfriend's slightly baggy T-shirt falling over one shoulder, her hair still a mess. And when she'd opened the door, she'd almost cried. Maya was there. Dressed in her navy-blue sweater, her sports bag slung over her shoulder, her features drawn by the sleepless night... but her gaze, oh her gaze... shone as bright as the first morning. Carina had said nothing. She had opened her arms and Maya had rushed in.

First a kiss. Then a sigh. And then this embrace, long, silent, where no words could say better than the mad beats of their reunited hearts.

- "I missed you..." murmured Maya at last against her temple.

- "I don't know how I'm going to survive our respective shifts for the rest of our lives..."

They hadn't changed room. Maya had dropped her things in the hall, then her shoes, then her jeans... She had followed Carina to the bedroom - forgetting even the coffees already made, she had let herself be undressed in silence, and had slipped into the sheets still warm from the night.

The Italian had wrapped her in her arms, curled up behind her, her hand resting right there, on her belly, her legs intertwined with hers.

"You're freezing..." she breathed, pressing her forehead against the nape of her neck.

- "I'm going to warm up..." murmured Maya. "Here, with you."

A soft silence had settled in. The kind of silence that breathes with two. Then Carina had slipped a kiss into her hair.

- "You'll be graduating soon. It's all over soon. You've done it, Maya Bishop. You're going to be a full-fledged paramedic and soon the future will open its arms to you, you're only a few months away from your biggest dream."

Maya had closed her eyes, her heart swelling with emotion. She hadn't realized it yet. But in this room, in these arms, it was truer than any degree. She had yawned and as if her thoughts were escaping her mind she had whispered:

- "I'm living my greatest dream right now..."

The Italian had understood, she was her greatest dream. She had felt a tear roll down her cheek, probably linked to fatigue and also to love, and she had tightened her embrace, bringing her hand up from her belly to Maya's heart.

The American had felt her lover's slightly shaky breathing, the one that announced her emotion. She had turned her head slightly, seeking Carina's lips.

"And now... I want to sleep. Naked. Glued to you. And I want you to never let me do another twenty-four-hour shift without you at the end."

Carina had smiled a huge smile.

- "Done deal."

And in that silent promise, Maya had fallen asleep, her breath peaceful, her heart warm.

The ceremony room was packed. Families, friends, loved ones of the graduates formed a tide of smiles, drawn telephones, bouquets of flowers and awkwardly colored signs. And in a corner, standing by the aisle, Carina hardly blinked.

She was looking for her. And suddenly, at last, she'd caught sight of her.

Maya had just entered single file with the other graduates, in the official EMT uniform: perfectly pressed sky-blue shirt, dark tie knotted to perfection, matching pants, shiny braid on the shoulder. Her nameplate gleamed in the light from the ceiling.

Carina felt her heart miss a beat.

- "Santo cielo..." she hissed softly, more to herself than to Andy at her side.

"It's illegal to look this sexy in uniform, isn't it?"

Andy had chuckled softly, arms crossed.

- "You ain't seen nothing yet. Wait till she passes firefighter and wears class A. She'll kill you."

Carina hadn't answered. She couldn't take her eyes off her. Maya's stride was straight, her head held high. An aura of calm, pride and determination radiated around her. She was sublime. And she wore the uniform as if she'd been born for it.

When her name was called, there was thunderous applause. Carina, however, hadn't just applauded. She'd let out a high-pitched, hearty Italian squeal that several heads had turned to identify.

But Maya knew exactly where it was coming from.

She had turned her head briefly in their direction. And when their eyes met, there was that smile. That smile that said it all. The one that said « I did it », but also « I did it for me. And for you. »

After the ceremony, on the school forecourt, the Italian had approached her before she'd even had time to remove her tie. She'd wrapped her arms around her neck, brushed the knot with a finger and whispered against her mouth:

- "you look dangerously hot in that uniform, baby bear."

Maya had laughed, her voice still full of emotion.

- "You should see the firefighters's, the class A. With the jacket, the medals, the cap... It's even sexier. I can't wait to show it to you."

Carina had planted a quick kiss on her lips, then a more tender one on her neck.

- "I hear... Andy warned me too... but be careful, bambina. I could have a heart attack before you even become a firefighter."

- "and I could die happy..."

Carina had slipped her hand into hers.

- "Are you coming? Let's celebrate. I'll buy you anything you want. Pizza, wine, dessert, massages, my whole life."

- "I'm not asking for that much..." murmured Maya with a playful smile.

"Just one night in your arms. And maybe you'll put the tie back on me... a little later... but not necessarily around my neck..."

The look Carina had given her, at that moment, was worth all the graduations in the world.

An impromptu party was taking place at the home of Maya, Andy and Ryan. As soon as they arrived, the blonde had been greeted by a salvo of applause and enthusiastic shouts.

- "there's our graduate!" shouted Taryn, jumping into her arms. "EMT Bishop, that's a wrap!"

- "she's officially authorized to save your lives, too!" added Jack with a wink.

- "And to wear the uniform without making everyone drop dead..." chuckled Carina softly, loud enough for only Maya to hear.

The blonde hadn't even taken the time to change. She'd stayed in uniform, because Carina had expressly asked her to. She had whispered in her companion's ear, even before leaving the ceremony, that she wanted this evening in her arms... but also in this outfit.

So Maya kept it. Tie neatly knotted, sleeves rolled up, look quietly proud. And throughout the evening, Carina hadn't stopped looking at her.

There had been laughter, wine, pizza, toasts to the new graduate's health. Andy had recounted anecdotes from her early days, Jack had done the same before imitating Maya's military step in the barracks corridors - the same one she'd been practicing for a week, non-stop, before graduation « to make sure she didn't miss out on the stage ». Everyone laughed, toasted, ate and celebrated.

But Maya only wanted one thing. To feel Carina's hand in hers. To meet her gaze in the crowd. To read that discreet pride, that silent adoration that was always there, more vibrant than any speech. Later, when everyone had begun to leave or settle into quieter corners, the blonde had approached the brunette, who had remained in the living room, a glass in hand.

- "Are you going to stare at me like that all evening?" she had whispered.

Carina had raised an eyebrow, teasing.

- "Are you planning to look at me like that all evening?"

- "Maybe I've got something better to offer you..."

- "Mmh... I'm listening."

Maya had held out her hand, gently, inviting her to follow. Without a word, Carina had grasped it, and they'd gone off to Maya's room, « their room » - as the American liked to call it.

As soon as the door had closed, the EMT had swung the Sicilian against her.

- "you know what I liked best about this day?"

- "tell me..."

- "the look on your face. When I went upstairs to get my diploma."

Carina had smiled softly, then undone Maya's tie, slowly, without taking it from her. A caress. A tribute.

- "And me, you know what I liked best?"

- "Mmh?"

- "That moment. The one when I found you again... Just for me. Where I can touch you, hold you. And tell myself that I love you... and that you're here, whole, beautiful, shining, and so mine."

Their foreheads had brushed. Their breaths had mingled. There had been no rush. Only a precious slowness, a silent stripping, a kiss on the hollow of the neck, a sigh against the mouth, a soft embrace in the sheets of a dwelling full of love.

That evening, Maya Bishop wasn't just graduating. She was loved. Adored. Surrounded. And when she'd fallen asleep in Carina's arms, the tie undone around their joined wrists, she'd thought yes. Maybe this was, after all, the best diploma of her life.
