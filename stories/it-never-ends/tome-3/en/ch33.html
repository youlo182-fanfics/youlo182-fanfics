The sky was still shrouded in mist when Maya arrived in front of the ambulance company located in a slightly secluded neighborhood north of Seattle. The unassuming building bore a simple red-and-white logo, a stylized stretcher crossed with a snake - the emblem of emergency medical services. The smell of coffee and wet tires wafted through the crisp morning air.

She had her bag over her shoulder and her tumbler in her hand. A black mug, as always, and this morning Carina had written on it in white: « Sei la più Bella, la più forte, la migliore. » Three little hearts were lined up at the bottom, clumsily traced, as always. Maya had smiled as she read it in the car, and the note had warmed her even more than the coffee itself.

Inside, three other students were already waiting in a small sitting area: Kelsey - obviously - and two others, Sam and Devon. Both looked nervous, but excited. Kelsey, for her part, had given Maya a look that was a little too strong, but said nothing.

The room was modest: an aged leather sofa, a few armchairs, a coffee machine that spat more than it worked, a foosball table in the corner and walls covered with team photos, badges and newspaper clippings. A few paramedics came and went, preparing vehicles or scribbling notes on laminated sheets.

Then a door opened. A burly man in his forties, with a snake tattoo on his forearm, graying hair at his temples, had entered with a redheaded woman with a confident smile and military posture.

- "so you're today's recruits?" he announced in a deep but not unpleasant voice.

"Welcome to the real world. No simulators here. You see something, you remember. You hear a piece of advice, you engrave it. You put your hands on it, you learn."

The redhead had taken over.

- "We're here to show you. But no touching without the approval of real paramedics you'll be teaming up with. We won't send you to the worst cases in the first hour, but be ready for anything."

She had scanned the room with her eyes before ticking and lowering her eyes to her clipboard.

"Maya Bishop?" she then asked, consulting an index card.

- "Yes." the blonde answered, straight, professional.

- "Like the Maya Bishop of the Olympics?" she added with a curious little smile.

The blonde had smiled politely.

- "yes, but here, I'm just... Maya."

- "Yeah, well, the break room's about to become a selfie museum. Get ready." Laughed the man.

And indeed... Maya's first hour with the company hadn't exactly gone as she'd imagined. No sooner had introductions been made than two paramedics had asked her for a photo. Then three. Then five. They told her about her run in the Olympics. Her medal. Her TV appearance, her cross-country tour. She'd signed two caps and a mug. And someone had even brought her an old box of cereal that must have been sitting in a cupboard for at least a year, a brand she'd been the face of.

She lent herself to the game with a smile - she didn't want to seem rude, but inwardly, she was dying to be talked to about emergency care, not timing records.

It was almost ten o'clock when she was finally able to take a breather. She settled down in the relaxation area with her phone, then opened the message from Carina that had just arrived.

Bébé ❤️ : Have you ever saved anyone? Or melt the whole company with your smile?

Maya had smiled, then typed quickly.

Baby Bear ❤️: I spent the first hour taking selfies, signing caps and talking about the Olympics. I'd have preferred to talk about interventions, but I didn't want to be cold... Baby Bear ❤️ : But otherwise, it's been fascinating. I can't wait to finally get into an ambulance. And to be with you in the morning.

Baby ❤️: You can be polite AND set your limits, amore. They'll soon realize you're more than just a familiar face. You're brilliant. And you belong. Baby ❤️: I can't wait for you to wake me up in the morning...

She looked up, met the gaze of a paramedic who gave her a knowing wink, then grabbed her tumbler.

She raised it to her lips. The coffee was lukewarm now, but the words on it remained the same.

« The most beautiful, the strongest, the best. »

She'd inhaled deeply. She was going to prove to them that it wasn't just a title.

A little later, Maya was sitting on a slightly rickety metal bench, her back to the wall, her tumbler which was now filled with a coffee far less good than her lover's in one hand, her phone in the other. Her legs slightly outstretched, she quietly answered Carina's messages between glances towards the hither and thither of the hangar.

Bébé ❤️ : You're already changing lives, amore. Even if you can't see it yet.

She had smiled and was about to reply that at the moment she felt more like a trophy than a future ambulance driver, because she had the feeling that they wanted to keep her inside, when a deep, gruff voice echoed through the room.

- "gold medal, blondie, with me."

Maya had looked up, a little surprised. A sharp-eyed, square-shouldered man in his fifties packed into a navy-blue EMT jacket was staring at her and had run a hand through his hair. He held a tablet in his other hand while chewing gum with a perfectly assumed nonchalance. Next to him, a second ambulance driver - younger, dark-haired, focused on the radio he was adjusting on his belt - had nodded in the direction of the garage entrance.

Sam, who was finishing a bag of chips at the other end of the corridor, had frowned.

- "Both of us?" he asked, pointing at Maya, then at his own chest.

- "yeah. The rest of the team is already out or on break. You're coming with us. Just to observe and ask questions if we have time."

Maya was up in seconds, her heart beating faster, that adrenaline rush she hadn't felt since the competition. Sam had glanced at her, a mixture of apprehension and excitement, then they'd followed in their mentors' footsteps.

In the hangar, the ambulance was waiting for them, engine on, flashing lights off. Maya climbed into the back with Sam, while the two professionals took the front seat. No sooner had the engine rumbled louder than the radio spat out a call:

- "code 2, residential, acute chest pain, female fifty-six, conscious, breathing unstable."

The vehicle had sped off down the still damp late-morning streets. One of the EMTs had risen from the passenger seat to come around to the back. Maya stared at the precise movements of the young EMT next to her, who was already preparing gloves, checking electrodes, stowing equipment in silent but efficient order.

The driver, the one who had called her « gold medal », had gruffly said, without turning around:

- "No heroism, young people. You observe, you learn. If someone asks, you answer. If not, you make yourself small. Got it?"

- "Yes, sir." they replied in unison.

- "And don't call me sir. This is Lou. This is Ben. You're allowed to breathe, not pass out."

Maya had nodded seriously. Her gaze had shifted to the street that flashed by behind the glass, the flashing light activating as the address approached.

She could feel the excitement, but also the concentration. It was real this time.

Not a simulation. Not a practice run.

Just... a life. And maybe a gesture, a word, a reflex, that could make all the difference.

She had inhaled deeply, remembering the words written on her tumbler.

« The most beautiful, the strongest, the best. »

No need to be heroic. Just present. Attentive. Efficient. Humane.

The ambulance had screeched to a halt. Lou had jumped from the driver's seat, then gone to open the rear doors.

"Come on, rookies. Welcome to the real world."

The front door of the house had opened on a pallid-faced, sweaty woman clutching the jamb as if trying to hold in the air that was escaping her.

- "Hello ma'am, my name's Ben, this is Lou. You called about chest pain, right?" the younger of the two paramedics asked calmly.

- "Y-yes..." she breathed, clearly on the verge of discomfort.

Maya followed closely behind Sam, both gloved, masked and standing slightly back, as expected. Their job here was to observe. Not to act. But the blonde had already taken mental notes as she analyzed the scene: the patient's position, pallor, excessive sweating, forward-leaning posture - everything pointed to serious chest pain.

Lou had gently taken the patient's arm to settle her on the folding wheeled chair they'd brought along.

- "We're going to sit you down and take you to the ambulance, okay? Breathe easy, we'll take care of you."

His way of talking to patients was less gruff, and it had surprised the two youngsters.

- "I've been in pain... since this morning..." she murmured.

"And it's not getting any better..."

Ben had already equipped her with the saturometer and started fitting the electrodes for an EKG while Lou pushed her towards the sidewalk. Maya, for her part, was watching with precision as Ben adjusted the electrodes without disturbing the patient's breathing.

Sam had whispered to Maya:

- "do you think it's a heart attack?"

Maya had replied in a low voice:

- "possible. She's sweating, she has dyspnea, persistent pain since this morning... but I haven't seen the ECG results yet. And she's conscious, so no loss of consciousness. I would have thought acute coronary syndrome."

Sam had looked at her for a moment, mouth ajar, as if she were an alien.

- "you learned all this in five days?"

- "my best friend's a paramedic, I nicked her books to read when I have free time and then... I had a good medical teacher at home, my girlfriend's a future doctor." she breathed with a smile.

- "ah yeah, I understand, you're well surrounded. Even if it is a bit weird to read books on first aid as a hobby."

Once in the ambulance, Ben had shown Lou the EKG screen with a nod.

- "we're leaving right now. Let Mercy West know."

Lou had nodded, returned to the wheel, and the siren had gone off as the ambulance sped away. Ben had set up an IV line, calmly slipping words to the patient as he worked.

Maya, for her part, was concentrating on every gesture: the compresses opened in a single movement, the disinfection, the tourniquet, the needle, the attachment of the catheter, the recording of the vitals.

It was a millimetric ballet, precise, tense but calm. A dance of sure movements.

Before they arrived at the hospital, Ben had glanced at Maya.

- "You've got a sniper's eye. I'm not even sure I saw you blink. You retain everything, that's good."

Maya had smiled beneath her mask.

- "I try."

A few minutes later, the ambulance had pulled into the emergency arrival area. Ben and Lou had relayed the information to the care team clearly, efficiently, and without a word too many.

When they got back into the ambulance, Lou said:

- "not bad for an Olympic champion and a weakling. We've had trainees faint at the first drop of blood."

- "I'd rather run than fall," Maya had replied, a smile in her eyes.

They had returned to the center. On the rest bench, Maya had picked up her phone again. A notification from Carina had already appeared:

Baby ❤️: what's up? You've disappeared. Did you save the world?

She had typed a quick reply.

Baby Bear ❤️: not yet. But I'm finally off to work.

A few seconds later, Carina's reply was in:

Baby ❤️: as soon as they see what my lover is capable of, they won't be able to do without you. I'm sure by the end of the night, they'll want to hire you before you even finish school. Being a firefighter is sexy, but I wouldn't mind you doing something less dangerous and just as heroic.

Maya had smiled, moved.

She'd just experienced her first real mission in the field. She hadn't made a diagnosis, nor saved a life - not yet - but she'd felt the intensity, the responsibility, the tension... and a silent certainty: she wanted to do this.

Not for the glory. To help. To serve. To be useful. And most of all... to deserve the words she read on her mug every day. Like today's.

« Sei la più bella, la più forte, la migliore. »

Several hours later, the Seattle sky had clouded over, and a fine rain was falling on the ambulance's windshield, forming a translucent veil. Inside the vehicle stopped in the middle of a clogged artery, Lou tapped nervously on the steering wheel.

- "this can't be true..."

Next to him, Maya was looking at the GPS map on the dashboard. They were less than three blocks from the scene. The radio had just crackled again.

- "central to unit 6. Patient in confirmed anaphylactic shock. Difficulty breathing, loss of consciousness. Status evolving. Need team on scene immediately."

Lou had grunted.

- "Ben's already gone down the other side to try to clear a path, but it's clogged everywhere. And the kid's alone with her big sister at the apartment, so they're afraid she's going to stop.... Wait..." He turned to Maya.

"Gold medal... this is your moment of glory. Do you think you'd get an EpiPen shot? It's in the leg."

- "yes chief."

- "okay, you take this bag, there's an EpiPen shot, a kit, a portable radio and you run. Three blocks. Building nineteen. Second floor. Apartment 2B. Can you?"

Maya had already grabbed the emergency bag, the portable radio, and the adrenaline that went with it.

- "I'm already gone." Lou didn't know her yet, but she had that look. The one that meant she was ready, determined and going to be the best.

She'd jumped out of the ambulance, her bag flapping against her lower back, her safety shoes - much heavier than her runners - every time she hit the puddles on the sidewalk. Rain pelted her white shirt and blue coat borrowed from the ambulance company; but she didn't slow down. Three blocks was a walk in the park - or rather, a survival sprint.

Each step struck the asphalt with relentless precision. She avoided passers-by, rounded a bike, accelerated as she climbed the sidewalk. Her breath was steady, regular. This was no longer a race. It was a mission.

Arriving in front of the building, she had jostled the door open on the fly, climbed the two flights of stairs three at a time, and knocked on the door of apartment 2B. A terrified, red-eyed teenager had immediately opened it.

- "she... she's not breathing properly! She's in the living room!"

Maya had entered without waiting.

The little girl was on the floor, wheezing, her throat already swollen, her skin dotted with red patches. Her eyes were glassy.

- "I'm Maya, I'm going to help you, okay?"

She'd already opened the bag. In less than three seconds, the EpiPen was drawn. She had gently spread the little girl's leg and planted the needle in her thigh.

One... two... three... four... five... she mentally counted, maintaining the pressure.

But the little girl didn't react. Maya had slipped two fingers under her jaw. She must have been so relieved to finally be taken care of, that she had let go.

"Minchia, no pulse," she breathed.

It hadn't really been on the agenda, but she hadn't hesitated for a second. She had placed her hands in the center of the chest, arms outstretched, shoulders over wrists, and started compressions.

"One, two, three, four..."

She counted aloud. To synchronize. To not let panic seep in. To keep her alive.

She'd looked up at the older sister, the one who'd opened the door for her.

"Can you rummage in the bag and see if there's a sachet with « mouth to mouth » written on it, please?"

The girl had searched, hands trembling, and found the sachet at the bottom of the bag.

- "t-that?"

- "yes, open it for me, please."

She'd complied and when she'd handed it to Maya, she'd removed her hands from the little girl's chest, placed the leaf over the little girl's mouth and breathed in two breaths, holding her head back, before returning to place her hands on her chest.

The radio on her belt had finally crackled.

- "Maya, we're two minutes out. Are you on site? Over." Ben asked through the radio.

- "come press the green button, please." She told the older sister, who immediately complied.

"Cardiac arrest confirmed. Epi injected. Starting compressions. Over. You can let go..." She said without stopping her movements.

- "Hold on, we're coming. Over."

But she wasn't thinking about holding on. She was thinking about holding on to the life under her hands. The life of this unlucky kid. That of the big sister in tears who had gone to take refuge again in the doorframe.

Her hands compressed relentlessly, at a steady, precise rhythm. Not too strong. Not too weak. She'd done it on mannequins. But this was real. A supple chest, a living body... or almost.

And then, a gasp.

The little girl had coughed weakly. Maya stopped the compressions and took her pulse again. Slight. Weak. But present.

When Lou and Ben entered, oxygen bag in hand, the scene spoke for itself.

Ben had crouched down beside the girl.

- "You saved her life." he breathed to Maya as he plugged in the mask.

Lou had nodded, hand on her shoulder.

- "I've rarely seen a beginner stay so lucid under pressure. You may have won a gold medal. But today you've earned a uniform."

Maya, panting, her gaze still fixed on the little girl, had felt a mixture of adrenaline and relief wash over her. No screams. No tears. Just a burning, vibrating certainty throughout her body.

She'd been there. And she'd done the right thing. She wasn't even sure she'd been there the whole time. It had been as if her body had known what to do.

The ambulance had stopped dead in front of the Seattle Grace Hospital emergency entrance. Maya had barely waited for it to come to a complete stop before getting out and opening the rear doors, helping Lou and Ben to pull out the stretcher on which the little girl was slowly regaining consciousness under the oxygen mask.

No sooner had her feet touched the floor than Maya saw her.

Carina.

Right there, a few steps away from her, heading for the doors of the emergency room too, on her way to work. Her long hair hastily tied back. Her body in a coat tied all the way up, her school bag slung over her shoulder and thankfully, the rain had stopped for a lull of only a few minutes. Carina was now frozen, her eyes riveted on her girlfriend.

On her... soaked, cheeks flushed, still panting.

Maya had slowed down, hesitated. Then she had turned her head towards Lou.

- "Can I...? Just two minutes?"

Lou, who had already seen more dedication and composure in her in a few hours than some had seen in years, had nodded.

- "After what you just did, Bishop? You got three."

Maya had smiled slightly, then darted toward the Italian. The Italian hadn't moved a millimeter. She'd let her come right up to her, eyes wide with both relief and wonder.

And when Maya had gotten close enough, Carina had immediately raised her hands to her wet cheeks.

- "you're soaked... And all red. What have you done?!" she murmured, half worried, half dazzled.

Maya had inhaled, still under the effect of adrenaline. She'd spoken quickly, her eyes shining.

- "We were stuck with the ambulance. Traffic jams. Huge! Dispatch called back, it was a life-threatening emergency. Lou told me to run. I took off. Three blocks; in those shoes! But she was a kid in anaphylactic shock. I injected the EpiPen. And... she stopped breathing. So I did CPR until they came. And she came back. Carina. She breathed."

She had paused, barely, to catch her breath.

Carina hadn't said anything, not right away.

Her thumbs had passed gently - half over Maya's temples half over her cheeks, soothing, tender. She'd moved closer, almost glued her gaze to the blonde's with silent intensity.

And then, at last, she had breathed:

- "this morning, I wrote on your tumbler that you were the most beautiful... the strongest... the best."

She'd paused, then sketched a smile that warmed more than any afternoon sun.

"I was right. You really are... the best, Bambina."

Maya had felt something pass through her. A soft, deep warmth. A mixture of admiration and pure love in Carina's eyes. And there, in the light rain that had just begun again, in front of that building, she had known. She knew she'd made the right choice. That she was moving in the right direction.

She had never been so proud. Not even at the Games. Because now, she wasn't just a champion. She was useful. She was saving lives. She'd saved at least one.

And Carina's gaze... was her podium.

The Italian had leaned over and kissed her gently on the forehead.

"I'm so proud of you."

Maya had closed her eyes, frozen under this contact, her girlfriend's lips on her skin like a balm, a seal. She'd simply nodded, the words stuck in her throat.

The Sicilian held the blonde's face and stepped back before dropping her eyes to the white shirt under the blue jacket. This one was glued to Maya's body, almost transparent.

"E questo per finire, visto che non posso godermelo?" [And this is to finish me off when I can't enjoy it?] She'd said this in a sensual, almost absent voice.

The blonde had blushed and the Italian had approached again, once more placing her lips on her face. From her forehead, down the bridge of her nose, then onto her cheek, turning the blonde's head towards the ambulance.

Lou had glanced up from the emergency entrance and given Maya a little wave.

She'd straightened up a little reluctantly.

- "I have to go back."

- "Go, Supergirl..." murmured Carina with a tender smile, taking a step back.

Maya had trotted away, but just before passing through the rear doors of the ambulance, she had turned one last time.

Carina was still there. Looking at her as if she were glowing. And Maya had felt herself grow wings.

They had returned to the ambulance station a little after four o'clock, having been recontacted just minutes after leaving Seattle Grace. Maya, still soaking wet, her hair sticking to her forehead, hadn't had a second to change since her sprint earlier. She'd helped put everything back in order in the ambulance, cleaning equipment, checking stocks, as if her body didn't want to slow down yet.

But Lou had looked up as she entered the garage.

- "Bishop. You're going to go change now. I don't want you to catch pneumonia."

He hadn't raised his voice, but his tone left no room for argument.

Maya had looked up at him, surprised not to hear the famous « gold medal » he'd been using since morning.

She had nodded.

- "Yes, Chief."

She'd retrieved her bag from the changing room, gone into the shower quickly and changed into clean clothes: new pants and a new shirt, she'd been so far-sighted, she had two spare outfits. When she returned to the break room, all eyes were on her.

And suddenly, a round of applause broke out.

Maya froze in the doorway.

Kelsey was clapping her hands, a sincere smile on her lips. Sam had raised his coffee like a silent toast. Even Ben had sent her a knowing wink from the corner of the room, a half-empty cup in his hand.

Lou, seated at the back of the room, had crossed his arms and added in a neutral tone but with a hint of pride:

- "I told them. You were alone with the kid for over five minutes. Perfect cardiac massage, EpiPen at the right moment. She's breathing thanks to you."

An almost respectful silence followed her words. Maya hadn't said anything right away. She had just sketched a small, humble smile and lowered her eyes slightly.

- "Thank you..." she'd breathed.

"But it was the job."

Lou had nodded. Then he'd stood up.

- "Exactly. But you only have four days of school..."

The alarm had sounded.

"And now we're going back." He added.

This time Lou had asked Devon to come with them.

- "Did your clothes dry during your shower?" asked the young man.

- "I had two spare outfits besides the one I was wearing."

- "You think of everything, don't you?" He said, closing the ambulance doors.

The late afternoon had been quieter. They'd had a few non-emergency transports, then an alert for a fall in a supermarket. Lou had pointed to Maya and motioned for her to ride in front with him, and for Ben to ride behind with Devon.

This time, she hadn't been in the front line.

But Lou had been talking to her all along.

- "you see the old lady over there, she slipped trying to retrieve a cereal box. What we're looking for here are mostly signs of head trauma or fractured femoral necks. Falls in the elderly can be catastrophic if not properly managed."

He then let her take the vitals, under his direct supervision. He showed her how to position the victim without forcing her.

"No need to run yet, Bishop. Just watch, learn. Endurance, you've already got it."

She'd smiled softly. Not « gold medal ». Bishop.

She'd heard the nuance. She was no longer the curious celebrity come to do her immersion. She was one of them. And she'd just earned something priceless: respect.

It was almost eleven. The EMT company break room was plunged into semi-darkness. Only a few subdued lights illuminated the room, giving the place an almost warm atmosphere despite the accumulated fatigue and wrinkled shirts.

Maya was slumped in one of the armchairs, legs folded under her, a light blanket thrown over her lap. Her hair tied back, a few strands not yet caught in its elastic band, she stared at her phone screen, a half-smile on her lips, her cheeks still rosy from the day's hectic hours. Despite the first two rather quiet hours, she had finally gone out several times. The boys, who had been more in demand at first, had taken a back seat after the little girl's episode.

Bébé ❤️ : Your bed is much too big tonight. And your sheets are looking for you.

Maya had smiled as she typed her reply, her fingers quick despite her fatigue:

Baby Bear ❤️: Just the sheets? Or do you miss me a little too?

Carina's response was swift:

Baby ❤️: I miss you more than anything, amore. The sheets want you, yes, but me... I'm dying to hold you close to me.

The blonde bit her lip, touched, and wrote softly:

Baby Bear ❤️ : I wish I could be there. To hold you in my arms, to listen to you talk about your day. Kiss you till you fall asleep.

A small suspension point appeared, indicating that the Sicilian was writing. Then:

Baby ❤️: It's torture not having you with me tonight.

I want to show you how much I love you. How proud I am of you.

Maya had raised her head for a moment, as if to breathe easier, as if these words had made her heart swell with emotion.

She typed slowly, carefully. But another message had appeared in the meantime:

Baby ❤️: especially since I can't stop thinking about you in this soaking wet, see-through shirt...

She smiled, but didn't stop.

Baby Bear ❤️: I saw it in your eyes, you know. When I got out of the ambulance and you were there. It was stronger than anything I've ever felt.

She paused, then added:

Baby bear ❤️ : Your look... it was love, but also pride. And that... made me feel proud of myself in a way I'd never felt before. Not even at the Games. Not even on the podium.

It took Carina a few seconds to answer. Then:

Baby ❤️ : Because what you did today... it wasn't for a medal. It was about saving a life. And you did it, Bambina. You did it with your heart, with your instinct, with everything you are.

A small sigh escaped Maya's lips. She had pulled the blanket tighter around her.

Baby Bear ❤️: I'd give anything for you to be here. Just you against me. Your hand in my hair. Your breath on my neck.

Baby ❤️: And my lips on your skin. And my arms around you. And my "I love you" in your ear.

Maya had slid back a little further into the armchair, her gaze riveted to the screen, as if every word from the Italian brought her closer to her.

Then a new notification appeared.

Baby ❤️: I'd give anything to be in your arms.

The light had come on and an alarm had sounded.

- "Bishop, it's ours." Lou shouted as she walked past, knowing she had just a minute before she had to go.

Baby Bear ❤️: I've got to go to intervention, my love.

Baby ❤️: Be careful. I love you so much, baby bear.

Baby bear ❤️: Ti amo più di quanto tu possa immaginare, cucciola. [I love you more than you can imagine, cucciola].

Maya had rested her phone on her chest, her eyes closed for a moment. The armchair creaked softly beneath her, the room remained quiet despite the alarm, and in this pause in the middle of the night... she had felt surrounded, despite the absence.

She missed her. But she knew that in that lack, there was also all the love in the world. Devon had walked past her, while her eyes were closed:

- "are you coming?!"

- "yes! Of course!" She said, getting up and running towards the hangar where the ambulances were.

The night was just beginning; she still had nine hours to go. But one thing was certain: this was what she was made for. To run to the rescue of others.
