On the Friday after Thanksgiving, it had rained all morning. One of those cold, unobtrusive November showers, dripping silently down the windows. Lucia had lit a few candles in the living room. Carina had waited for Andrea to leave. He'd waved to her from the hallway, his bag slung over his shoulder, on his way to meet Mason, and then some friends in town. He hadn't planned to return until the next day.

For her part, Carina hadn't made any plans for the weekend. She just wanted to enjoy her mother. And to be honest, she'd heard from Taryn that Maya would be staying in San Diego for Thanksgiving, and hadn't felt like going to see their friends and pretending, for an entire evening, that she and Maya spoke regularly.

She didn't know if it was her past year of therapy, but what had become second nature to her was getting harder and harder to keep up. Pretending to talk to Maya. Lying to their friends. It was hard to hide the truth in a new world where she was doing everything to reach that truth and stop lying all the time.

She had to become the Carina she'd been before the blackmail. An improved version, even. A version in which she didn't fear judgment. A world where she could assert herself frankly, clearly.

Then silence filled the house. And Carina knew it was time.

Lucia was in the kitchen, tidying up the previous day's leftovers. Grey light filtered through the blinds, bathing the room in a soft, almost unreal clarity.

Carina had entered, her hands slightly clammy. She closed the door behind her and approached the kitchen island. Her mother had turned around when she heard her footsteps.

- "Maya?"

The tears had come this time. Carina had sketched a sad, wavering smile.

Lucia had looked at her, her eyes misty.

A nod. Slow. But she hadn't said anything.

"You know tesoro... Mothers know and see everything... It's not « new » to me that you love Maya. I've always known."

Lucia had deliberately used the present tense for the verb to love and not the past tense. She wasn't fooled. She knew her daughter still loved her.

Then Carina's words, finally:

She had lowered her head, her hands trembling.

Lucia had nodded slightly, a gesture of encouragement, without pressing her.

She'd swallowed, then looked at her mother, straight in the eyes.

A shiver ran down her spine. She looked down, then forced herself to continue.

Lucia hadn't said anything. But her fingers had tightened slightly on those of her daughter.

She was safe and sound..."

She'd looked up at her mother, her cheeks soaked.

"I had to break her heart, Mom. And I broke mine too. Because I loved her. I still do. And I couldn't even tell her why. I had to pretend I didn't feel anything. That it had never happened. That the situation wasn't slowly killing me, that it wasn't making me disappear... That I'd fallen in love with Jackson..."

Lucia had placed a hand against her mouth. A silent, wrenching pain. And Carina had continued, her voice cracking:

"He told me I'd go to Berkeley. That I would date Jackson. That I'd be normal. And... he slapped me... All because I wanted to go back to my room because he'd taken away everything that made me me... Me."

A sob had escaped her.

"I finally went up to my room, Mom. And I went to bed hoping I wouldn't wake up. Because he'd just trampled everything. My love. My freedom. My life..."

Lucia's breath had stopped. She knew there was more. Her cheeks drowned in tears.

"Because he'd suggested it was better, if I didn't.... If I didn't wake up."

She'd broken down, then. In tears. Shoulders shaking. Breathless.

Lucia had stood up. Slowly. Silently. And she had taken her daughter in her arms. Hugged her. Tightly. As if to make up for all those whole years of silence, of silent suffering, of suppressed pain.

- "I'm here, tesoro mio... I'm here."

Carina had clung to her, face buried against her neck, hands trembling.

"I didn't know... I didn't see it. And, and, and I'm sorry. God, I'm so sorry..."

She'd kissed her hair, her temples, her forehead.

"What he did to you, what he said to you... It's unforgivable. And I'm sorry I didn't see anything. I'm sorry I didn't understand. I'm sorry I couldn't protect you, my daughter, my baby..."

Lucia had kissed her temple.

A silence. Then Lucia had added, in a breath:

"And I love Maya."

Carina had nodded, tears welling up again, her face still hidden against her.

Lucia didn't have all the answers. But she was there. And now she knew.
