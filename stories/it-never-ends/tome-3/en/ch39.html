Time flew by.

Ever since they had found each other, everything seemed to move at breakneck speed. Days passed in the blink of an eye, nights faded into crumpled sheets and breakfasts came and went as if someone had pressed the fast-forward button. And before they knew it, before they had time to catch their breath, Thanksgiving was already here.

They'd been together for almost two months now. Officially. Two months of loving each other without hiding, without doubt, without restraint. Two months that had flown by faster than the four and a half years of silence and distance that had separated them. And yet, in each of those almost sixty short days, they had experienced more love than in anything they had known before.

That morning, they had woken up a little earlier than usual, excitement in their bellies, like children before a big day. Their respective brothers were arriving for Thanksgiving. Andrea had just returned from Stanford, and Mason from art school in Ohio. Two brothers they hadn't seen in months - and above all, the only ones who had always known, from the start, that there was a « them », even when they were still a whispered secret, or in a teenager's bedroom with a closed door.

They were in a hurry. Not just because they were happy to find them. But because this time, they could go looking for them together. Hand in hand. No hiding. Two loving sisters. Two proud lovers.

Their flights landed only minutes apart. Carina had donned a long beige coat with turned-up collar, and Maya a thin leather jacket open over a midnight-blue sweater. As they left their apartment, they gave each other a knowing glance, the kind that said, « We're going to relive a piece of history, but this time for the better. »

And in the car, their fingers intertwined on the gearshift, they turned up the radio a little louder than usual. Because it was a special day. Because they were going to be reunited with those who had first witnessed their love. Because at last, they could be themselves one hundred percent in front of them - not just sexfriends. And they'd just be happy for them.

The Seattle airport was buzzing like a beehive. Suitcases rolled in every direction, loudspeakers announced arrivals over and over, and the smell of hot coffee wafted through the terminal air.

Carina walked at a brisk pace, Maya right beside her, their hands intertwined, clasped as if by evidence. It was crazy, this sensation... To have her there, by her side, with no need to hide anything. To be there to welcome their brothers, together. Like a couple. Their couple.

The screens showed the simultaneous arrival of Mason and Andrea's flights within minutes. They had stopped just in front of the domestic arrival gate, where the endless stream of tired but happy faces passed through the security barrier.

The airport concourse echoed with the sound of suitcases, reunions and announcements over the microphone, but to Maya and Carina, everything seemed a blur as excitement knotted their stomachs.

Almost two months. It had been almost two months since they had found each other. And yet... those eight weeks had flown by at breakneck speed, as if time, too, wanted to make up for everything they'd missed. It was as if the years of distance, the wounds, the silences, had been absorbed into this living, vibrant present.

And now it was Thanksgiving.

And they were finally going to see their brothers again.

Mason, for Maya. Andrea, for Carina. Best friends for a few years now, they had been, long before anyone else, the silent witnesses of this love story between their sisters. Back when Maya and Carina were dating in secret, they were the ones who knew. The ones they had trusted.

And today, they were going to find them. Together. Officially together.

Their flight arrived within minutes of each other. Andrea was returning from Stanford, a university sweater on his back, a bag slung over his shoulder and his hair a little disheveled, as if he'd run all the way to the gate. Neat, upright, with that bright light in his eyes that betrayed his intelligence as much as his benevolence. He'd spotted his sister immediately in the crowd and his smile had widened.

Mason, on the other hand, had just arrived from art school in Ohio. His hair was almost as long as Maya's, falling in soft locks around his face. He dragged a battered suitcase behind him, a canvas bag over his shoulder, and wore a khaki-green jacket in faded colors, covered with small traces of paint. His bohemian style contrasted with Andrea's more academic look, but his eyes lit up in the same way when he caught sight of the two women.

Carina had let go of Maya's hand just long enough to run into Andrea's arms, giggling like a little girl as she felt her brother lift her off the ground. Mase, for his part, had surrounded Maya in a strong, silent embrace, his cheek pressed against her temple, an embrace more in the image of their family, which wasn't the most demonstrative.

Then their eyes had met, and Mason and Andrea had hugged as if they'd parted the day before.

And somehow, for Maya and Carina, it was a bit like that. The return of that discreet but precious core, that little circle of trust that, even in the shadows, had never ceased to exist.

- "so you two..." said Mason, raising his eyebrows.

- "you two FINALLY!" corrected Andrea.

Both boys had burst out laughing, while Maya and Carina had rolled their eyes at the same time.

- "you knew very well we'd end up together!" muttered Maya, falsely pouting.

- "We knew it..." nodded Mason. "We were just waiting for you to realize it too."

- "And now here you are, together, at the airport, looking for us..." quipped Andrea, an expectant smile on her lips. "And it feels really good to see you like this."

Carina had slipped her hand into Maya's again, their fingers searching for each other as if by reflex.

And for a moment, there in the middle of the hall, surrounded by hurrying families and clattering suitcases, there were four of them. Just four. But it was a world unto themselves.

The boys had rushed to the back of the car, hugs quickly dispatched, replaced by a deluge of endless talk.

- "so you saw Sam again?" asked Mason, shooting Andrea a knowing look.

- "We're officially dating, what do you think?" replied Andrea, puffing out her chest slightly.

- "I think you're on the verge of marriage."

A few streets over, Maya had stopped in front of Lucia's house. Carina had detached herself, then leaned over the handbrake. Her fingers had brushed her girlfriend's cheek with discreet tenderness. She had come close to kiss her, a slow, loving, almost intimate kiss despite the two chatterboxes at the back. Then, her lips barely detached from the blonde's, she'd whispered:

"See you later... I miss you already."

A heart in suspension. Breath held.

- "How long are we apart? Thirty minutes?" muttered Andrea in a mock groan. "You're unbearably cheesy."

Carina had glanced over her shoulder at them, falsely threatening.

They got out of the car and Maya drove off with Mason, heading for the Bishop home, only ten houses away.

Lucia, for her part, had been busy in the kitchen since dawn. Her table was magnificent. Everything had been thought of: the turkey roasted to perfection, the fresh cranberries, the homemade mashed potatoes, the pumpkin pie, the little Italian dishes revisited in a Thanksgiving version... It was the first time she had welcomed « real » Americans into her home. The first time she'd opened her home to Maya's parents.

She wanted everything to be perfect.

And if she'd stopped for a minute, she might have noticed that her hands were trembling slightly.

Carina, putting the bags on the floor, had seen her. She had joined her in the kitchen and slipped her arms around her mother.

/Conversation in Italian/

- "Mamma... you're perfect. Calmati un po'." [Calm down a little].

Lucia had looked at her with emotion.

- "I want everything to be perfect for her. For her famiglia. I want them to see how much I love their daughter."

And it was.

In the Bishop's hushed living room, the sweet smell of caramelized sweet potatoes with marshmallows still wafted through the air, a remnant of the dish Katherine had finished wrapping. Maya sat cross-legged on the sofa, her hands folded between her knees, watching her father set his watch, ready to leave. He was wearing his favorite shirt, the one he reserved for family meals, and looked a little stiffer than usual. She knew it wasn't the collar that was squeezing him.

- "Thanks, Dad." she said in a soft voice, breaking the silence.

Lane had turned his eyes to her, surprised by the tone, but hadn't said anything.

"Thank you for agreeing to come to the DeLuca house this noon. I know it's not... your idea of the perfect Thanksgiving meal, and that's just it, it means even more to me."

He'd looked away for a moment, hands on hips. He'd nodded slowly, not smiling.

Lane had sighed.

She'd looked at her with a soft gleam in her eyes, no anger, just a tender truth.

He had smiled discreetly, his eyes slightly crinkled.

She'd stood up slowly, walked over to him and put her hand on his arm.

- "Then accept that I don't like it either... in a... « classic » way."

He'd nodded gently. And this small gesture, this silence between them, was perhaps the beginning of something.

Half an hour later, they had arrived on foot in front of the DeLuca house. Katherine held a steaming plate of sweet potatoes au gratin with marshmallows in her arms, all smiles, while Maya proudly carried the pecan pie she'd made the day before with Carina straight to her parents' house.

In fact, it was the Italian who had opened the door. And even though it had only been thirty minutes since they'd parted, her eyes lit up as if she'd just seen the love of her life again after an eternity. Maya couldn't help but smile. And Lane, in the background, was forced to realize that this love, this way of looking at each other, was nothing different from a « classic couple ». Nothing less pure.

Lucia, nervous, had greeted them with a slightly trembling warmth. But very quickly, Katherine had taken the lead, complimenting the decor, the scent wafting in the air, the attention to every detail.

- "oh my god, Lucia, that tablecloth is gorgeous. And the little wreaths of leaves? Did you make those? It's beautiful!"

Lucia, touched, had blushed slightly and nodded.

While the Bishops settled into the living room for the aperitif, the boys - Mason and Andrea - had slipped away to Andrea's room, two glasses of soda in hand and a console controller under their arm.

Seeing them disappear, Carina teasingly called out:

Laughter erupted around the living room. Except for one. Lane, a little stiff on the sofa, hadn't cracked a smile.

So she'd slipped her hand into Maya's, next to her on the sofa, and offered him a smile that said, « but never mind, because we're together... And that's enough. »

- "Qualunque cosa tu decida di fare... sarò sempre orgogliosa di te." [Whatever you decide to do... I'll always be proud of you.]

"You're already part of this family, Maya. Like a daughter. And... maybe one day... my daughter's wife."

A suspended beat.

Of course the word had already floated into the air, sometimes in their jokes, sometimes in the silences between two « I love you's ». But this... This was real. Concrete. Said out loud, with all the weight of what it meant.

And that day, around that table, in that house where two worlds met, we weren't just talking turkey and traditions.

It was about the future. About roots. And of a love that was gradually taking root in all directions.

Andrea.

"Ma ora l'hai," Andrea concluded, "Ce l'hai davvero. E nessuno potrà più togliertela." [But now you have it. You've really got it. And no one will ever be able to take it away from you again].

A shiver had run through Carina, and she'd placed a discreet kiss on her brother's temple.

- "Ti voglio bene, fratellino mio." [I love you, little brother].

- "Anch'io." [Me too.]

And while the parents took their leave with hugs, thanks and reassuring smiles, while Lucia saw them off with that benevolent warmth that had become her signature, Carina and Andrea had remained there. Brothers and sisters in the moment. Reunited, too. Healed, in their own way.

Then Carina joined Maya, naturally. Like returning home. And that evening, more than ever, everything was in its place.
