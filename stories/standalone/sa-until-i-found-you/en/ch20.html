Carina was discharged from hospital on July 9th. She was still doing physiotherapy on her elbow, but overall she was already feeling better.

She was back in her new room. The one she didn't know yet.

She was a little disgusted because having blown up her phone, which in reality she hadn't, she'd lost all her passwords. She'd had to recreate quite a few accounts because the passwords she now had all referred to Maya in one way or another. The only one that was rather old was her Meta account, where her father had deleted her friendship with the blonde, as well as photos of the skateboarder. She also no longer had access to her Google photo account, which contained hundreds and hundreds of photos of the two lovers.

She had never been here before, not that she knew of, and yet she recognized her belongings. She recognized the faces on the photos, even those of her two new friends, but despite everything, despite the fact that she could now see her room, she still had a feeling of lack, of emptiness, as if something was missing even here, like the one in her brain. Perhaps she was mistaken and the empty spaces on her mirror had never been filled.

She had taken her camera and tried to turn it on, but it had no battery. She put it on charge before opening her wardrobe. She looked at all the clothes in it. She finally opted for a pair of shorts and a tank top and headed for the shower, a real shower at last. Even if she didn't recognize the place, it wasn't a hospital shower and that made all the difference.

She had undressed, there were no more bruises on her body, but on the other hand she had a ten-centimeters scar on her left elbow, she had a scar under her chin and her hair had started to grow back in the two places where she had been shaved for her two operations.

She'd looked at her body, she was pretty, she thought she was rather pretty, and she couldn't help wondering why she hadn't managed to find a girlfriend. She wondered if it was because she was looking too «straight».

She'd taken a shower and stayed a little longer. Then she got dressed and went to see her brother.

The Italian had left the house and started walking through the neighborhood. She'd walked for hours, so much so that she'd come to the Venice canals. She had taken a few shots, several. She had taken some with her phone and with her camera. She'd been surprised by the quality of the photos on her new phone. She understood why she'd hardly taken her camera for the last eight months, if her phone took such good photos. If her old phone took photos as good as this one.

She had taken a photo and posted it on her Instagram.
Maya had immediately received the notification. Her heart began to race. She clicked on it and saw that the brunette had posted a photo of the Venice canals. It wasn't very far from home, she was at the skatepark and it added five to ten minutes to the journey. Besides, whether Carina was still there was less certain. Perhaps she'd been there earlier and was only posting the photo now. In fact, the Italian's father had unsubscribed her from her profile, but he hadn't thought of removing the blonde's invitation from his daughter's account; he thought it was done automatically when he stopped following Maya's account from that of Carina.

She had kept her finger on the screen for a long time, just to look at the photo. Carina was as good as ever.
She hadn't seen her for a month and a day now, and she hadn't seen her eyes for two months and six days. They'd spent more time apart than together. If only the Italian could post a photo of her.

Since last week, Carina would have had to come for two and a half weeks to the Bishops' as they were going on vacation and the blonde was going to be left alone. She had to train before leaving for the Olympics.

She had put her phone in her bag, which she had placed on their bench, on her and Carina's bench. She was helping Alberto get sponsored.

The Italian continued her walk. She'd spent so much time at the hospital, she didn't feel like going home and locking herself in.

She continued her stroll along Venice Beach. She stopped at the skatepark and started watching the skateboarders on their boards. She got a little closer and took out her camera. She'd taken a few photos and then picked up her phone again to take more shots with it. She'd posted another story and a few miles away, Maya had felt her watch vibrate.

«Carina DeLuca has updated her story»

She had wrinkled to her bag and picked up her phone to click on the notification. The Italian was in Venice Beach, at the skatepark where they'd often been.

Maya picked up her board and threw it in the direction of the bowl, almost injuring someone.

-"What is it?" Alberto asked as he joined her and retrieved her board.

Maya was crouched on the ground with her head in her hands.

Alberto didn't know how to react. Maya had always been a strong girl. She'd never even cried over a skateboard fall, not even when she'd split her chin open, and now, to Carina, she was in pieces.

She'd been on edge for two months, but now she was cracking up. He'd come and put one of his arms around her shoulders and Maya, who wasn't tactile, had done something surprising. She hugged her best friend and cried.

While Carina was still in Venice Beach and Maya at the skatepark in Alberto's arms, Jack and Jo had arrived to see their friends.

-"What's... What's going on?" Jo had crouched down and ran her hand down Maya's back.

-"Carina posted a story... She's... She's in Venice"

Maya was ashamed. She'd broken down in front of everyone. She usually broke down in her room, alone, out of sight; but this time, it had been stronger than she could bear. And on top of that, she couldn't stop crying.

Carina had then gone for a walk on the beach, heading back up to Santa Monica. She then walked back up to her house. She felt like she had to make up for more than two months of walking.

On Thursday, two days later, Carina had received a message from Pam suggesting that they go for an afternoon stroll in Santa Monica. She accepted and decided to bring her camera. She'd wanted to propose to Taryn and Jo, but she hesitated, not knowing if it was a date or not. She didn't know if she wanted it to be a date or not.

So she'd gone for a walk with Pam, but for the moment she didn't know what she wanted to do. It was hard to know what to do when she'd forgotten part of her life. It wasn't a question of whether or not she was being kept in the dark, but of who she'd become in the last few months. She might not have changed, but all the same, she wanted her memories back, if only to remember classes, her high school, Jo and Taryn. Everything that made up the Carina of her sixteen years.

It had been eight days since she had been discharged from hospital. This morning she'd been awakened by the doorbell. She'd come downstairs, her parents were working this morning and Andrea was the kind of person who slept like the dead.

She opened the door and her first sight was a huge bouquet of roses. The same red roses she'd received at the hospital. The bouquet had been moved to the side and a man in a sky-blue cap had looked at her.

- "uh... I have a delivery for Carina DeLuca".

- "y-yes it is... That's me."

- "okay... Here..." She had taken the bouquet in one hand and he had handed her a tablet.

"Could you sign here please?"

She signed with her finger and went home with the bouquet, which she held up to her nose. She had closed her eyes and inhaled the scent of the flowers. Who could have sent her a bouquet? Who could have sent her a bouquet of red roses?

She had counted the number of flowers and there were seventeen. She saw that there was a card in a small envelope.

She placed the bouquet on the table, took the note and opened it.

«Happy birthday Carina. ♡ »

She didn't recognize the handwriting. Who could have sent her these flowers? What did the heart mean?

She had taken a vase and put them in it before depositing the flowers in her room, where she had gone back upstairs.

She had taken one of the roses and sat down on the bed with the card. She'd brought the card to her nose, she didn't know why she'd done it, but she'd smelled something when she'd received the bouquet. The card smelled of a perfume she didn't know, but had immediately liked. She didn't know if the scent had any significance, but she couldn't help but place the card against her nose again and again.

She couldn't help wondering if the bouquet was from Pam, her parents, her friends, someone else? And she couldn't help wondering if it was someone's perfume, or one that the florist had added to the card.

She wouldn't broach the subject with Pam, except in the event of her being the first to bring it up, as these flowers resembled the ones she'd received when she was in a coma, but she didn't yet know her new friend. It might be strange to have received roses from her, given that for the moment they were getting to know each other, especially red roses, a symbol of love. It would be strange to ask, because if they weren't from her, Pam might feel embarrassed not to have given her a present for her birthday. So many possibilities and no answer. Besides, she didn't know if she wanted them to be from her. For the moment, she was still at a loss.

She had turned on her computer to try and get more answers. Maybe her photos would speak to her in a way no one else could.

She'd been waiting for it to light up, more than two months switched off. At least two months, she thought. In fact, it had only been a little over a month since her father had emptied the folder entitled Maya, which contained a whole bunch of photos of the blonde.

She brought the card up to her nose again. She didn't have a password, fortunately, because otherwise, as she'd changed most of her passwords to a link with Maya, she wouldn't have been able to start it.

She'd tried putting the folder in the trash, but without the password she couldn't move it. For the moment it was stuck there, and if she never recovered her memory, she'd have no information about what was in it.

They'd been renamed for the simple reason that Vincenzo had removed all the ones in which Maya appeared, and so there were gaps in the numbers. He'd thought of everything.

Why had she stopped taking photos at the skatepark in February? What made her stop taking pictures of this girl? Was she perhaps surprised? After all, there were photos of the blonde with two men, Alberto whom she knew and another. And obviously portraits of other skateboarders, but the blonde really seemed to be a source of inspiration for her photos. She'd handed over a photo of herself in portrait mode where she could only see her face, and held the card up to her nose. She'd wondered if she was the girl Jo and Taryn had told her she had a crush on, «a blonde», that was not surprising but this blonde was really very beautiful.

She had spent part of her morning looking at her photos. She'd been cut off by a message.

Pam: Hi beautiful! Happy birthday to you! Shall we do something?

She had looked at the photo of the blonde on the screen, if this blonde was «the blonde» she didn't stand a chance, it was too late, «the blonde» was in Senior and so she was in her last year of high school. She wouldn't see her again in the fall.

The Italian had spent the day with her new friend, and in the evening had eaten at a restaurant with her parents, her brother, Vic and Andy, who would be sleeping at her place tonight, as well as Jo and Taryn. Vincenzo hadn't wanted Jack and Alberto to come, as he didn't want the conversation to drift onto skateboarding and finally Maya. Carina still hadn't recovered her memory, and for him, that was fine.

Maya was due to leave for Paris on July twenty-fifth. She had the Olympic Games at noon on the twenty-eighth for the women's street event.

Between Carina's birthday and her departure for the Olympic Games, she had gone skateboarding in Venice Beach. She'd emptied her bottle of water as she'd been there since early morning, so she'd come to one of the stores on Venice Boardwalk to buy herself a bottle. She got out, put one foot on her board and drank half the bottle in one go.

She had put her head back on straight and lowered her arm. Her hand had dropped the bottle to the ground. A few feet away from her, Carina was with a young woman she didn't know. A woman black woman. They were eating an ice cream. Carina had tapped her «friend"» on the arm and crushed the scoop of ice cream on her upper lip and under her nose.
From where she stood, Maya could hear that laughter she hadn't heard for over two and a half months now.

The woman with her «girlfriend» had grabbed the Italian's neck and jaw and wiped her lip and the underside of her nose against Carina's cheek, making her laugh even harder.

Maya was torn between a multitude of feelings. Carina was fine, she was alive, she was able to laugh out loud, she was beautiful, she was more beautiful than ever. She was wearing a cap, not her own, Maya's, but she'd kept a little habit she'd acquired from hanging out with her. She wore it backwards. She'd thought that with the flowers she should have sent her a cap.

But apart from seeing her at last, eyes open, laughing, breathing, talking, there was a feeling that was even stronger, a predominant feeling, Carina seemed to have forgotten her, well that wasn't great news, she already knew that, no Carina didn't «seem» to have forgotten her, she had forgotten her and on the other hand she seemed to be in a relationship with someone else. She had long told herself that even if Carina had forgotten her, they were soul-mates and that deep down, the Italian must have felt that something was missing. But on the face of it, Carina didn't seem to be missing anything.
