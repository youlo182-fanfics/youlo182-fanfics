She'd been very lonely.

At her age, her parents were parents, her former classmates were all parents or at least couples, her firemen were practically all with someone else. 

She had put her hand on her heart and felt panic overwhelm her. Even now she was alone, she was having a panic attack and she was alone, no one could calm her down. 

She had turned around and stooped to sit down, letting herself fall along the single-bowl vanity in her bathroom. She sat there for a while until her panic attack passed.

She then went to bed. She'd had trouble falling asleep. 

The next day, when she woke up, she had found her panic attack, her existential crisis of the previous day, ridiculous. 

She got up and went for a run. Sport had never let her down. 

A few days later, while this idea that had seemed absurd the next morning was running on a loop in the corner of her mind, she had gone out on an intervention with her team. They arrived at the scene of a house fire. Maya was dispatching firefighters when she saw a man run out of the house carrying two children, drop them on the grass in front of the house, and then run back inside before any firefighter could catch up with him. 

The blonde had asked her firefighters to search the house for the man, while others had gone to check on the children. 

They eventually found the man and came out with him, carrying his wife while another firefighter carried a wheelchair. 

He had gone to look for his wife and children, as his wife was unable to run out of the house by her own. One of his firefighters unfolded the wheelchair and the father of the family was able to put his wife in it. 

The man and woman hugged for a long time, but he was in tears. He had been so afraid of losing his wife. The two children had joined their parents and the four of them had hugged for a long time. 

Maya had looked at them and swallowed. Even growing up, she'd never known this kind of love. 

The man wasn't afraid to show his tears, to show his love. 

The captain had finally gone to see them to find out what had happened, and she had been able to witness their love up close. She envied them. She could see how much this man loved this woman or their children and then she'd felt that pain in her chest again, but this wasn't the time, she couldn't have another crisis now. 

When she'd gone home that evening, she'd started to cry. It wasn't like her to cry, but she'd been thinking about that family and all that love. 

She was thirty-four and in less than a year she would be considered geriatric, if she wanted to have a child. 

She'd never thought about having children because she'd never had parental love, but after what she'd witnessed today, she told herself that this kind of love didn't only exist in the movies. 

She knew she couldn't find love just because she wanted to, she couldn't fall in love or make someone fall in love with her just because she felt ready, but having a child was something that was within her grasp. She wouldn't be the first woman to make a baby on her own. 

She hadn't been able to sleep all night, she'd thought about it for hours, at four in the morning she'd even decided to research IVF specialists. 

Her mind was made up.

By eight o'clock she'd gone for a shower; today she wasn't going for a run. She got ready and went to the station afterwards. She'd have plenty of time to run at Station 38, on the treadmill.
