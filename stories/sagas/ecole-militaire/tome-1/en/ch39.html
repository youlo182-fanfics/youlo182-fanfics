It had been three weeks since Maya returned home. She still hadn't given Carina any sign of life.
All she feared was that Carina would show up on her spring break.

After all, she didn't know if Maya was going to enlist or not, so if she came, she would have no idea if she would be there or not.

She continued to receive letters from Carina.
She was crying as she wrote the letters, Maya could tell by the state in which she was receiving the letters, words that were illegible because of the tears that had flowed over them.

Carina sometimes acted as if Maya was talking back to her. She would tell her about her day. And sometimes she would beg her to answer.
Maya read her beloved's letters. Yes, she loved her, but she could not force herself to contact her, even if it tore her heart. I love you' she thought at the end of each letter. She was no longer the same. She was no longer that carefree young girl. She was nothing, she thought, without him. She missed Mason and she refused to be happy without him. Why would she deserve it.

Carina on the other hand was again withdrawn. She was going to class. That's all she had left. Her classes, her letters and the memory of Maya.
Adisson sometimes tried to revive her but it only worked for a short time.

Obviously she had hesitated to go to Arizona to confront Maya but would she be there? would she receive her? So many questions and so few answers.
She sometimes talked with Katherine but she didn't ask about the beautiful blonde who had broken her heart. She didn't want to put Kathy in a bad position. She had told her that she would give her back the money for the rings, but she had refused. She felt guilty, guilty for not changing her own children's minds. She had lost a son, she didn't want to lose a daughter and that's what Carina meant to her.

It had been seven and a half months since Carina had heard from Maya and 11 since she had seen her. Katherine had invited her to spend a week in Arizona at the beginning of the summer. She would go just before her birthday, just before returning to Sicily.

She would arrive on Monday, June 30 and leave on July 6.
Obviously Katherine had warned her daughter. The latter had gone camping in the Grand Canyon valleys. It was out of the question for her to see Carina. She hadn't written her a letter in almost eight months, at least not sent one, and she hadn't seen her for eleven months.

Katherine had come to pick up Carina at the airport. The escalator she had used many times brought a tear to her eye, she had been looking for her, consciously or unconsciously, but she wasn't there. Her favorite pair of blue eyes was not there.

Instead, she found the comforting arms of her ex-mother-in-law.

-"how... Are you okay, honey? "

-"It's okay. She... She won't be there? "

- "no, I'm sorry"

They were now in the car.

-"I just want to ask you a question and then I won't ask about Maya" just saying her name out loud caused her voice to go off the rails, painfully and uncontrollably.

- "tell me, I'll see if I can answer you"

- "she... Did she... Enlisted?"

-"I think I can answer that. No. I don't think she expected that... And the death of..." Her voice broke "Mason's death in that surprise attack had a lot to do with it too and you..." But she stopped

Carina wanted her to continue her sentence but she had said she would only ask one question.

- "I got Mason's room ready for you."

-"Can I... Sleep in HER room? It was kind of mine at one time".

- "I think you can"

They had arrived at the base. Carina climbed the stairs she knew by heart. She put her hand on the handle but froze for a moment. She had passed this door so many times.

The room had not changed. The pictures of Carina were still plastered on the walls. She didn't know if the room was occupied. But it must have been, since Maya had not enlisted.

She passed her hand on the quilt of the bed. Then approached the desk, a T-shirt was placed on the back of the seat of the latter. She grabbed the fabric between her hands, pressed it and brought it against her face.
Maya must have worn it recently, she could smell her. A tear ran down the cheek of the beautiful Italian.

She then tossed it onto the bed. It would be her blanket for the next six nights.

She went downstairs to see Katherine, who offered to let her sleep in for a while. She had promised Maya that she would put the Italian to sleep when she arrived.

To go to bed in Maya's sheets? She didn't hesitate. She undressed, remaining in only a t-shirt and boxers, and slipped into the sheets of the one she still loved.

The sheets hadn't been changed and Maya's scent was all over the bed, on the pillows, on the comforter. She took the t-shirt and put it over her face. It had been eleven months since she had slept so well. As if they had just disappeared in a snap of the fingers.

At 7 p.m. she woke up and before going downstairs she put on a pair of pajama bottoms.
She went to kiss Lane and sat on the couch with them.

They talked for a while before going to the table. They talked about the classes Carina was taking, about her life in Bologna, about her roommate, about Mason. They even reminisced with Maya as if nothing had happened, as if she was about to walk through the door of the dining room.

The week went by quickly. Carina spent time with her ex-in-laws. She cooked for them. She went to the mall with Kathy. She even saw Vic and Lucas.

The two lovers had told Carina that they both wanted to become firefighters. Being pure military was not for them and they did not want to live apart. This was without counting on Carina's feelings, she didn't want to live separated from a certain blonde either.

Sunday had arrived, however, and at 4 p.m. the beautiful brunette had her plane to Catania.
At 1:30 pm she kissed Lane and Kathy on the porch before going to Vic and Lucas' car who had offered to drop her off at the airport.

She got into the car and after one last look at the house she might never see again, they drove off.

Lane and his wife entered the dwelling and there she was, hidden behind the living room curtains looking out the window without being visible from the outside. A tear on her cheek. She had entered through the door adjacent to the kitchen. Shortly after Carina walked through the front door.

She took her pillow and brought it to her nose. He smelled Carina and in a split second she heard her laugh again, felt the warmth of her skin, saw her beautiful hazel eyes full of life, her smile so angelic. Shivers ran through her, the same ones she had felt a quarter of an hour ago when she saw Carina under the stoop, then Carina getting into the car.

Then she put it down, she could not abandon herself in her memories. Carina was her past despite the pictures on her walls that proved otherwise. She just couldn't get them off. She had tried several times but without success.

Her eyes wandered through her room until they came to rest on a box on her desk.

She opened it. Inside were letters, a small box, scrambled sheets, notebooks.

She didn't know if she should dive in.
Numbers were written on each notebook. She started with the first one.

They were there, all there, Carina's secret thoughts. Since that day in November when she left, Carina had been writing, pages and pages of it, every day. Sometimes she wrote about how much she missed Maya, sometimes she was angry, sometimes she wrote about her nightmares.

Maya had been reading for two hours, skipping pages, but she would come back to it later. She looked at her alarm clock. 4 p.m., Carina must have been taking off. She looked out the window, knowing full well that the base was not below any air corridors. But it didn't matter. After ten minutes she settled back on the bed. She removed the six identical notebooks and took the sheets underneath, essays, mostly from this year, very few from senior year, all of them more or less about Maya, various aspects. The dates showed how Carina was suffering more and more. However, she always came up with a logical reasoning that earned her excellent grades.

Then letters always numbered. Obviously these had started a few weeks before her arrival, so she must not have sent them. Considering the quantity, there must have been some this week.

Then she saw one different from the others, probably the last one she had written.

She decided to start with this one.

'The last one' was written on the back of the envelope where normally a number would be.

Seeing that word, 'the last one', brought another tear to Maya's eye. It was the end, and Carina had put a note on it. Never again would she hear from Carina, never again would she read that she loved her, never again would she be able to read something new when she was done with the notebooks, the essays and the letters.
