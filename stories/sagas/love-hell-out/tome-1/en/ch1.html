My name is Maya Bishop, and I've waited years for this day. Today I'm finally a Battalion Chief, it hasn't been an easy road, it's been more of a bumpy ride.

For a long time, I was a firefighter at Station Nineteen, with my family and friends. Then I became a lieutenant, I was with my best friend's almost-fiancé, whom I lost in the process, then who forgave me, then whom I lost again the day I took her father's place. She resented me for a long time, then we finally found each other again and her father lost his life saving them from a fire, saving my firemen from a fire. He died while I was in charge, and yet, despite the fact that I let him go up there, she forgave me and I found her for good.

During an intervention several months later, I made a decision that cost me my job. I was relieved of my duties pending « mytrial ». In the meantime, we've inherited a misogynistic prick who calls me « mypretty » - I'd so much rather Andy had taken the job...

But that's another story. A few months later I still hadn't heard from my trial, almost a year. Almost a year to wait until one morning Beckett, the misogynistic prick, told us that McCallister had gone into early retirement, we understood him well, it was a forced retirement, but in short, a woman had come to replace him, Natacha Ross.

It took a while, but one morning she came to see me at the barracks and told me I wouldn't be going back to Station 19 as captain. I was devastated. That, too, was one of the worst days of my life. My career as captain of Station 19 was over.

After a year of  « temporarily replacing me » Ross couldn't ask Beckett to leave just like that, just because she'd decided to end my year of torture.

And not only was this one of the worst days of my life, because in addition to learning that I wouldn't be returning to Station 19, I also learned that I'd be taking command of Station 23. I know what you're thinking, what am I complaining about? I'd just learned that I was going to be captain again.

To give you an idea, Station 23 and ours did an intervention together a few weeks ago in a neighborhood where there was a gas leak so impressive that we experienced several earthquakes in Seattle as well as explosions. It wasn't a pretty sight, and we lost one of my family members, Dean, who left behind a little heart, an adorable little girl named Pruitt Arike Miller, Pruitt in honor of our former captain, the one who perished under my command.

But that's another story. The captain of the twenty-three, Captain Akino, also perished that day, and Maddox has been temporarily replacing him ever since. I'm still wondering how that man ever made lieutenant. This is the image of the twenty-three, a man who thinks that women belong in the kitchen, not at work, and certainly not in a fire station.

Twenty-three barracks has no women and I'm sent there. It's a macho barracks. The whole barracks smells like feet. I'm not a fragile little flower, but I have my limits. Lucas Ripley, our former Fire Chief, the best Fire Chief in the world, former fiancé of Vic, a friend, a sister, a colleague of mine from nineteen, who also died in the line of duty.

I realize that I'm in a very dangerous business. I feel like everyone around me ends up dead. But I'm a firefighter, I know the risks, but still, I have the feeling that I've already talked about too many people who left too soon... But I digress.

So Natacha Ross came and gave me the good news one morning that I was going to be captain again, but this news, which was one of the best pieces of news of my life, was followed by two of the worst. I'm no longer captain of the 19 and I'm off to the 23. Some misogynistic prick was going to keep my barracks, Pruitt Herrera's barracks. My home.

I held on. I stayed at Station 23 far too long for my liking. I did manage to get three girls on board in the two years I spent there, and without wishing to boast, the day Natacha Ross came to give me the terrible news that Station 23 was closing due to budget cuts, I was rather proud to be the Station's last female captain. It didn't feel feet anymore, I had female firefighters, and I'd managed to educate Neanderthals.

But the station where I was captain was about to close. Beckett was still in charge of the 19 and I was a « homeless » captain, or rather Station-less. Natacha had obviously explained to my firefighters, my lieutenants, that she was going to try to offer most of them a reassignment, but that unfortunately some of them were going to be thanked. She then interviewed me and made me a more than surprising proposal.

Really surprising.

Natacha Ross had offered me a position as Battalion Chief in North Seattle. An area I know absolutely nothing about. But a position as Battalion Chief. She hadn't seen me as Captain of Station 19, but she had, in her words, been impressed by what I'd done with Station 23 in a year and a half.

So here I am, Maya Bishop, thirty-five years old, single, and Battalion Chief of an unknown sector.

And I'm standing in front of my mirror in a suit that looks exactly like the one I wore  « yesterday » , when I was a captain, a captain without barracks, except for a few details. I've swapped the silver « accessories » for gold and red ones, retracing my history...

-"and above all to talk to myself".

Maya had huffed as she looked in the mirror. She had to go for it. She couldn't just stand there. She couldn't stay in front of her mirror forever.

She wanted to move on to Station 19; it would have been so easy to start with the station where she'd grown up. Through friends, family, people who loved her. But Station 19 wasn't in her area.

She had gone to one of the barracks in her area to introduce herself, then another, then another.

I feel like I've spent my day touring fire stations and introducing myself. There were some firefighters I knew. Firefighters with whom I'd already worked, fire stations with whom I'd already worked, like the one at the tower, when, without telling anyone, I had problems with my hearing. Fortunately, it was temporary. It feels strange to have been passed over as Battalion to some of the captains who have already given me orders. It's so strange, but I'd like to think that if Chief Ross made me as a Battalion and not one of them and me in one of their places, it's because she must have thought I was the right person to do it.

It feels like a never-ending day, and yet it's not that late. It's only two o'clock. I've still got a few more stations to go and I'll end this day at station 88, which will be the last one, and then I won't even be able to go with my friends to celebrate because they're on duty. We've already celebrated the fact that I'm going to pass Battalion.

It's Saturday, so not Wednesday, but would it really be wrong if I decided to go out tonight rather than on a Wednesday? After all, it's not obligatory to have girls and guys in my bed only on Wednesdays. When I was « with » Jack we didn't care what day it was and yet I don't consider Jack my ex, ex. It was more like that. But anyway, Jack's in the past, he's my friend again, as is Andy, fortunately, and all the other firefighter in 19. I think Pruitt's death, then Dean's, brought us closer together, almost like before.

But anyway, as I was saying, tonight I couldn't go and see them, it's not as if I could go and squat them at the barracks. So maybe I'll go to Joe's. Or maybe I'll stay at work, I'd like to know what's waiting for me there, maybe I'll read some reports. Okay, I've got some strange hobbies. Maybe I'll pick up some reports and take them home and read them over Asian or Italian food, a quiet evening.

It all happened very quickly, too quickly.

About ten days ago, Fire Chief Ross announced the closure of Station 23. That we'd be closing the following Wednesday, and last Friday I learned that I'd be passing Battalion, so I only had eight days to get used to it. I had hoped, I had imagined passing Battalion before I was forty, but I didn't really imagine it possible, especially when I was dismissed.

And now I'm thirty-five and battalion. Would it be too much to think that I could potentially be Fire Chief before I'm forty? I've already given up hope of being Seattle's first female fire chief, but hey, I'm not going to complain.

Sorry, I think all the time, my brain goes three thousand miles an hour. It can't keep its mouth shut and neither can I. As soon as I reach one level, I'm already thinking about the next. And fire chief is next. I know I'm thirty-five and I'm not getting any younger, and I'm not going to get any younger.

I'd have to think one day about starting a family, marrying a man, but let's be honest, I couldn't marry a firefighter, I'd have to find a man from a different sector because my future husband would never accept that I am his superior and the men in Seattle, well in the fire department who are in a higher position than me, are old, ugly and already married. I just don't have to worry about kids, I don't want any, so I wouldn't be pressed for time and my biological clock. I just need to find someone who will spend their old age with me if I ever retire. If I make it that far.

In short, it's not my priority right now. I know I should be happier to have passed battalion, but Station 19 and its area were my whole life and now I'm like in another city. I'm -

Maya's cell phone had rung while she was driving to another barracks.

/Telephone conversation/

- "cap-Battalion Chief Bishop, may I help you?"

- "battalion, h-here it is, Captain Khalidi, there... there... There's been a... A problem"

-"I'm listening"

-"you... You can come to the... The training center..."

-"I'm on my way"

/End telephone conversation/

Maya had switched on her company car's flashing beacon and alarm and turned down the middle of the street, heading straight for the training center.

My God, what's going on now? It's my first day and I feel like there's already a big mess. A day of respite. I know that's what the job calls for, but still. Just one day, it's my first day.

It hadn't taken Maya long to arrive at the training center. She got out of the car and went to see Andy. Today, her friends, her old station was at the training center with Station 88, the one she was supposed to come and see at the end of the day.

-"what's going on Herrera? What's the matter?"

- "I-"

-"Andy?" Maya asked worriedly, placing her hand on her best friend's shoulder.

"Where's Captain Khalidi? Where's Beckett?"

-"there... there... There's been an incident. We were doing a training simulator. Lieutenant Cooper was leading his team into the simulator. We were having a little competition because this is Beckett's old barracks... And... In the final round in the simulator, Lieutenant Cooper and Probie James took on Ben and me in two-on-two. We had just one mannequin in the simulator to save, with the winning team being the one that retrieved it. James and Cooper found the dummy, but an unexpected explosion knocked them down before they could retrieve it. James found Cooper, who was unconscious, and called for help. Beckett ordered Ben and me to get them out, which they did with the help of our two teams. James was given oxygen as he had lost his mask in the explosion, and Cooper was taken to hospital with a head injury. They just left with their captain and Beckett...

Before left us the captain asked me to manage so we could close the center, put out the fire and wait for you to explain what had happened."

- "he... Does Beckett he..."

-"I think... We all think so... But he... He seemed really upset... And Cooper he told us about his girlfriend, he's going to propose to her, they have a little boy a year and a half old, Beckett had been Cooper's first captain when he was a probie, he seemed really affected. He was in hospital for the birth of his son, and they're friends... He was a different man today, but... But yes, I think... That he'd been drinking and that it was an oversight... Want to take a walk around the center before the Fire Investigation Unit arrives? You were the best of us at checking details like fire starts and so on".

- "I'd like to, please"

Andy also seemed affected, like all the firefighters on 19 and 88 she was seeing at the moment.

And indeed, on entering the center, on the way to the blast area, several things had jumped out at her. Several mistakes had been made.

At least once a year, two stations met here. Firefighters from both stations came here to test their mettle and practice essential firefighting skills.

My God, how can Ross still let that man run my... No, it's not my firehouse anymore, but how can she still let him run a firehouse. As much as I still resent Sullivan, he was right when he ratted him out for alcoholism. She just didn't test him on the right day, he got lucky, but if this Lieutenant Cooper has any after-effects, there could be serious consequences. I almost lost my job forever for saving a little boy's life, but to have endangered the lives of twenty Seattle firefighters, I hope it doesn't end there. Yes, even Battalion, I still resent the whole thing. I hope FIU Captain Vinnie doesn't cover it up.

After making the rounds with Andy Herrera, Maya went back outside.

- "what else can you tell me about how the day went?"

-"We got the call and were the first on the scene. That gave us a head start. We ran timed courses and probie James briefly got lost in the simulator, but lieutenant Cooper found him and they finished the exercise two minutes faster than our station.

The atmosphere was good-natured and we decided to bet on the outcome of the exercise, with the losers paying for the winners' drinks. Cooper helped Beckett install the incinerators in the last run, Cooper on our side and Beckett on the 88's side; and then the captain went back inside to, among other things, install the dummy.

Ben and I were selected, but I don't know why Beckett chose this youngster, James. Ben may be pretty new for his age, but he's been a fireman for seven years now... He put in a probie for station 88... Anyway, after that you already know what happened... Captain Khalidi wasn't there, he came as a civilian and called you... He wasn't far away, he didn't hesitate to come and go to the hospital with his firemen."

- "okay, thanks An-Herrera. You can all go back to your stations, I'll wait for FIU and deal with them. I'll tell them to come and see you at your respective stations."

Maya had waited for Captain Vinnie and his team, explained what had happened according to Lieutenant Herrera and, after several hours, finally left for Station 88. She would visit those she had missed because of this incident the following day. It was already quite late when she arrived at station 88. She had started to make the rounds when Captain Khalidi had arrived with Lieutenant Askey and firemen Brown and Hellerstein, followed by James, the probie.

-"ah, Captain Khalidi, I've been waiting for you, what's going on?" Maya asked as they all reached the reception desk.

-"the... Lieutenant Cooper he... He died of his wounds" replied the captain, trying not to crack in front of his superior.

-"Oh, no, I... I'm sorry for your loss. Is there anything I can do for your barracks? For you?"

-"I... I know you started today, but... Would you mind accompanying me to warn his partner ?"

-"No, of course not."

-"I... I'll put on an outfit... I'll put on my uniform... I'm... coming"

-"No worries Captain Khalidi. I'll call headquarters and ask them to redirect your interventions on forty-six and forty-two. You can tell your teammates to go home".

-"you... Are you sure?"

-"I know what it's like to lose a member of your family. I wish someone had done it for us..."

-"Thank you Battalion Chief"

Captain Khalidi had gone to change before holding a short meeting where he introduced Maya and invited the firefighters home for the evening, before they both left in the blonde's company car to visit the late Lieutenant Cooper's partner. They were both in the car. Captain Khalidi was concentrating on not crying, so Maya, who had noticed him clearly, did what she usually did: she just talked to herself.

Why does it have to be me, and why on my first day? I've been battalion for not even twelve hours and already I have to tell a woman she's a widow. He looks like he's going to faint or throw up, I have a feeling it's going to be me who has to do the talking. In the end, the day I learned I was going to become captain of a Neanderthal station wasn't the worst day of my life. Today is probably in my top five. One of the best, but also one of the worst day of my life.
