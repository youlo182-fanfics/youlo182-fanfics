The pouring rain had made visibility almost nil by the end of the day on September thirty. By 5 p.m., Seattle's main north highway was saturated with rush-hour traffic. Maya had reached Station 82, which had already been called out to a multi-vehicle pile-up involving two motorcyclists. Blue and red flashing beacons blinked in the grayness, reflecting off the wet asphalt as the trucks from 82 made their way through the stopped cars.

She had crossed the barricades with her firefighters: Godin, Ferranti, Wilson and Ramos, all already equipped and ready to intervene. Maya, always attentive to the smallest details, had inspected the scene in a single glance. Cars crashed into each other, shards of metal and glass strewn across the road, and in the middle of it all, the two overturned motorcycles, one of them still on fire, emitting thick smoke despite the rain.

-"Godin, Ferranti, take care of the red car. Check the wounded and prepare the evacuation," ordered Maya, while turning to Ramos. "Ramos, Wilson, you with me. We've got to put that motorcycle out before it gets any worse."

The firefighters had immediately dispersed, each knowing their role. Godin and Ferranti had rushed to the car, while Maya, Ramos and Wilson had deployed the fire hose to bring the motorcycle's flames under control. The sound of pressurized water, mixed with the crackling of the fire and the constant lapping of the rain, formed an oppressive background.

-"De Sousa is on her way to station 46 for reinforcements," Ferranti had announced into her walkie-talkie, while helping to pull a badly injured woman out of the wrecked car.

Maya had nodded in agreement, while concentrating her efforts on the motorcycle. Once the flames had been extinguished, she straightened up, her eyes scanning the extent of the damage. She could already see an inert figure on the ground next to another motorcycle, probably one of the bikers. The other was being stabilized by the paramedics, conscious but in a state of shock.

-"Wilson, check with the paramedics, they need arms to move the wounded man," ordered Maya.

Wilson rushed over while Maya, with Ramos at her side, advanced towards the figure lying next to the second motorcycle. The biker was badly injured. She had checked his pulse before shaking her head.

-"He's gone," she murmured, but there was no time to linger. Other victims needed help.

Godin and Ferranti were extracting a couple from the red car, badly injured but still alive. Screams and sirens dominated the scene, and Maya kept her calm, her voice clear above the chaos.

-"Ferranti, take care of the traffic, we need to clear a corridor for the ambulances," she ordered.

Ferranti complied without missing a beat. Shortly afterwards, De Sousa and her team from Station 46 arrived. Maya quickly beckoned them to join her.

"De Sousa, you take care of the cars behind the pile-up. We need more personnel for stabilization," she had ordered, her voice authoritative but calm, her gestures quick and precise.

De Sousa, who had gone from 88 to lieutenant in another and was «in charge» of station 46 today, had immediately taken command of her team, heading for the accordion-like cars at the rear of the scene. The reinforcements were finally allowing us to take a step back from the situation. But every minute counted.

Suddenly, another car had started to catch fire. The firefighters rushed to it before the flames spread any further.

-"Ramos, get the extinguisher. Godin, help him with the hose," ordered Maya as she coordinated the other extrication efforts.

The rain, cold and relentless, complicated the task, making every gesture more difficult, every surface more slippery. But for Maya and her team, nothing was more important than controlling the situation and saving as many lives as possible.

Finally, after long minutes of relentless effort, the last victims had been taken care of, and the flames brought under control. Maya glanced around her. The chaos seemed to have calmed down, with paramedics tending to the survivors, while emergency vehicles continued to pour onto the now partially cleared road.

She took off her helmet, felt the rain on her face and let out a long sigh. There had been deaths, but at least the majority of the injured had been taken care of in time.

- "Good work, everyone," she said, her voice hoarse with exhaustion. "We did what we could."

Her firefighters had looked at her in silence, their faces covered in sweat and rain, but with the satisfaction of knowing they'd given it their all.

As stations 46 and 82 prepared to leave the scene of the pile-up, Maya, soaked and exhausted, had glanced at her team. Their faces were scarred by the effort, but there was a sense of duty done. The worst seemed to be behind them.

The battalion was about to give the order for Station 82 to return after their long intervention when her walkie-talkie crackled again, interrupting the brief moment of respite.

-"Battalion Bishop, this is Cook from Station 42. We've got a big problem... a critical situation on I-5. A school bus with high school students has collided with a hazardous materials transport. There's a chemical leak on the scene, and no one can approach without proper protection. We need your expertise to assess the situation before intervening."

Maya had clenched her jaw. This kind of accident combining young victims and dangerous substances called for immediate expertise and meticulous risk management. She knew exactly why she was called in. Her specialized training in hazardous materials management made her indispensable in this kind of critical situation. She turned to the Station 82 team, exhausted but ready for anything.

-"You can go home now. Good work today," she had told them in a firm but calm tone. "I'll take over with Station 42."

- "Okay, Chief," Godin had replied, before joining the others.

Without wasting any time, Maya had climbed into her command vehicle, switched on the lights and headed alone to the scene of the incident. The journey on the freeway had seemed interminable, even though she knew that every second counted. Cook's descriptions had already allowed her to assess the gravity of the situation in her head: overturned bus, hazardous materials, potentially injured high-school students or worse.

By the time she'd arrived on the scene, the driving rain and falling darkness had made the atmosphere even heavier. The flashing lights of fire trucks and emergency vehicles had illuminated the scene with a bluish glow. The school bus was on its side, its windows shattered, and a few meters away, the hazardous materials truck, ripped open, was leaking a whitish liquid under the driving rain. The firefighters from Station 42 were standing back, awaiting instructions.

Lieutenant Cook had waited for her at the entrance to the secure perimeter.

- "Chief Bishop," he'd said, stepping forward, his face serious under the downpour. "The truck was carrying concentrated hydrogen peroxide. We had to move the perimeter back because of the risk of reaction with the rain. Raveendran is already on site to secure the area, but we need your instructions before intervening any closer to the bus. We have no idea of the condition of the bus passengers. There has been some movement, but no communication for several minutes. We can't get any closer because of the leak."

Maya had nodded and assessed the scene at a glance. Concentrated hydrogen peroxide could be extremely dangerous, especially with the rain still falling. It increased the risk of chemical reactions and the release of toxic fumes.

-"You were right to stay away. Concentrated peroxide is highly unstable, especially with water. We'll have to act quickly to prevent the situation from worsening, and perhaps even establish a wider perimeter. Have you secured the immediate surroundings?"

-"Yes, and we've even extended the exclusion zone. Lee and Galarza are ready to move in as soon as you give the go-ahead."

- "Where exactly is Raveendran?"

- "She's out reinforcing the perimeter with Gilmore and Danes," Cook had replied, while indicating the location with a nod.

Maya had approached Ragi Raveendran, who was already wearing her specialized hazmat gear. She was finishing attaching signs warning of chemical hazards on the site.

-"Raveendran," Maya had called to her.

Ragi had turned and nodded when she saw Maya coming.

-"Chief. The leak's under control for now, but with the rain, it's getting more and more complicated. We've still got some kids on the bus, some visible through the windows. They're not moving."

Maya had weighed up the options in an instant. It was too risky to send a team directly near the bus without proper equipment. But if the youngsters were still alive, they wouldn't last long in these conditions. The wind was blowing hard, the rain wasn't letting up, and the conditions were making the situation even more critical.

-"Okay, we'll have to send a specialized team to evacuate them, but first we have to make sure the area around the bus is safe. You're in charge of gas and vapor detection. Use the equipment to measure the concentration of peroxide in the air before anyone gets near the bus."

- "At your service, Chief," Ragi had replied before heading for the truck to retrieve the detection equipment.

Maya had returned to Cook.

- "Once we've secured the area around the bus, we'll evacuate the youngsters in small groups. You prepare the stretchers and make sure the paramedics are ready. I want everything coordinated down to the millimeter."

Cook had nodded.

- "We'll get on it right away."

Maya had watched the firefighters from Station 42 methodically deploy in the pouring rain. Raveendran, equipped with her detector, scanned the area, carefully scrutinizing the results displayed on the device's screen.

After a few minutes of waiting, Ragi returned to Maya.

-"Concentration levels are still high, but stable. We can get close by limiting exposure, but we'll have to move fast."

Maya had nodded. She knew they didn't have the luxury of taking their time.

-"Okay, let's go. Cook, get a team ready. Henderson and Anderson come with us to evacuate the youngsters. Raveendran, you continue gas monitoring. If the levels change, you get us out immediately."

Maya had moved cautiously forward with Lee and Galarza toward the bus, assessing every meter they traveled. Station 42 firefighters Gilmore and Danes were already on the ground at a safe distance, watching for the slightest change.

Everyone had moved quickly into position, knowing exactly what they had to do. Maya had approached the bus with Anderson, Lee, Garlarza and Henderson, trying to locate the youngsters still trapped inside. Through the windows, she could see faint movements, a sign that some were still alive, but the situation remained critical.

-"Lee, check the conditions of access to the bus. We need to get in without creating new risks," ordered Maya.

-"There's a possible opening near the side door, but the metal is very deformed. We can try to get through, but it's still risky," he said.

- "Let's open that door and start evacuating," she ordered.

Maya had thought quickly. Every second counted. If the hydrogen peroxide continued to react with the humidity, the passengers inside risked poisoning.

With Anderson's help, they forced open the deformed bus door. The intense chemical smell that had escaped had alerted them immediately, but they hadn't had time to retreat.

-"I'll stay here to monitor the leak and alert you if anything changes. Lee, Henderson, Garlarza and Anderson, you go in. You must get these youngsters out as quickly as possible."

They approached the bus cautiously and managed to squeeze inside. The space was narrow and cluttered with debris, but they could see the silhouettes of the teenagers, some conscious but traumatized, others visibly injured.

-"Cook, this is Bishop. We're about to start the evacuation. Send stretchers near the opening, but stay outside the perimeter until we've secured the wounded."

-"Copy that, Bishop. We'll take care of the stretchers right away," Cook replied from outside.

Anderson and Henderson had entered quickly and started to extract the high-school students, one by one. Maya had stayed behind, surveying the scene, always ready to intervene if the situation turned ugly. Seeing that the bus was full and that they were taking a long time to get the youngsters out, the battalion decided to lend a hand. She entered the bus and asked Ragi to take her place.

Maya leaned over to a young boy, his face covered in blood from a cut to the head.

-"We're here. You're going to come out of there, okay? Stay calm," she told him gently but firmly.

She had exchanged a look with Lee.

-"Stabilize those who are able to move. We'll have to evacuate them quickly. The fumes could be dangerous if they're exposed too long."

Lee had begun to tend to the wounded, while Maya assessed the condition of the other passengers. One of the teenagers was moaning weakly, his deformed arm indicating a fracture. She knew that time was against them.

The next few minutes had been a race against time. Maya, Henderson and Lee were evacuating the teenagers one by one, while Cook and his team maintained security around the stage. Gilmore and Danes worked tirelessly with Anderson and Galarza, who had been requisitioned from outside to contain the chemical leak, preventing the worst from happening.

As the teenagers were removed from the bus, they were attended to by paramedics who waited a little further away, out of harm's way.

Finally, after what seemed like an eternity in the torrential rain, and thanks to an intense and methodical effort, all the teenagers had been extracted from the bus. Some were injured, others shocked, but they were alive. Ambulances quickly took over, transporting the injured to hospitals. Maya's adrenalin had slowly subsided as she watched the scene stabilize. The team's swift and efficient coordination had prevented an even greater tragedy. The rain continued to fall heavily, but they had averted catastrophe.

Raveendran had been constantly monitoring the peroxide levels in the air, and Maya knew that their safety depended on his vigilance.

Maya had taken a deep breath and turned to Cook.

-"Excellent work. You handled that impeccably."

Cook, soaked but relieved, had nodded.

-"Thank you, Chief. Without your expertise, it could have been a lot worse."

Maya had also saluted Ragi before preparing to leave the premises.

-"Raveendran, your vigilance made all the difference today. Well done."

Ragi had smiled slightly, while removing her mask.

-"Thank you, Chief. It's been an honor to work under you."

Maya had nodded, then, after taking one last look at the secure scene, headed for her vehicle. Another difficult day was drawing to a close, but a life saved always justified every effort. Her shift was far from over, and she had a feeling it was going to be an intense night.

On the way back to Station 82, she had called Carina on the road. Her lover was her haven and she needed to hear her voice. She told her about the two interventions that had just taken place.

After a few minutes, Maya had received news by radio. Their colleagues had managed to evacuate several residents from the second floor, but a few were still trapped above.

Once the intervention was over, and after checking one last time that everything was in order, Maya had started heading back to the station, satisfied that she had been able to help.

No big fire tonight, but a good reminder that even a simple leak can become an emergency situation.

she mused, as she headed for the truck.
