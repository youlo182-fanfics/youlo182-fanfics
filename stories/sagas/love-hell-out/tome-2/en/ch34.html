On the twenty-first of January, morning light filtered softly through the curtains of the couple's bedroom. Maya had risen, feeling a slight pressure in her chest, a foreboding that had been with her since the weekend. Danielle's birthday, celebrated on the nineteenth and twentieth of January, had been a joyous one, but the reality of work had quickly caught up with her.

As she was getting ready for the day, her phone had vibrated on the kitchen counter. She glanced at the screen, and her heart sank. A message from one of her colleagues informed her of the death of a battalion chief during an intervention the day before. The words »horrible accident» echoed in her mind, and a shiver of anguish had run through her.

- "What's going on?" asked Carina, emerging from the bathroom with a look of concern on her face.

-"A battalion chief died yesterday, Robert Sullivan. They need me to manage all his stations today" replied Maya, her voice trembling. "There are only eight of us left."

Carina had approached, taking her hands in hers.

-"I'm sorry, bambina. It's terrible."

Maya had nodded, but worry was growing inside her. The weight of responsibility was getting heavier on her shoulders. She had always been the capable battalion leader, the strong woman, but this news was affecting her deeply. She suddenly felt overwhelmed by expectations.

Over the next few days, the pressure only increased. Calls for help poured in, and every station she had to deal with seemed like an insurmountable mountain. At every turn, she fought against the anxiety that had built up in her mind. She tried to stay focused, but doubts were beginning to creep in.

As she watched her teams work, she remembered her father's words, criticisms that still echoed in her ear. « You're playing the boss instead of getting into the thick of things » he'd once said, and she'd always sought to prove otherwise. But now, in the midst of this storm, she felt like an impostor, unable to cope with the situation.

Sitting in her office, she often found herself lost in thought, scanning the emptiness before her. Childhood memories returned, tinged with pain and judgment.

I'm not goodenough. she thought, a tear rolling down her cheek. This feeling of not being good enough assailed her, like a dark cloud hovering over her.

That evening, on the way home, she had lost herself in her own thoughts, her heart heavy. She had tried to hide her stress from her wife, not to let her know that she was in the grip of doubt. But deep down, she knew that anxiety was intertwined with her professional and personal responsibilities. She had to be strong for her family, for Carina, but how could she be when she felt like she was wavering?

Two weeks after her colleague's death, Maya had sunk deeper into her work. Every day, she found herself dealing with increasingly stressful situations, making difficult decisions for her teams. The weight of responsibility was becoming overwhelming, and a gulf seemed to be slowly opening up between her and her wife. Meanwhile, Carina's career was taking off. The opening of her practice was on the horizon, and every day she saw her professional dreams come true.

One evening, after a successful operation at the hospital, the brunette had returned home, happy and overexcited. She had helped a woman give birth to triplets, an experience that had filled her with happiness. As she entered the room, she began to talk enthusiastically about the details of the birth, but the bathroom was silent, although the light was on and the water from the shower could be heard. Intrigued, she headed for the entrance, hoping to find Maya there.

To her surprise, she had discovered her wife sitting in the shower, water running down her body, but her tears were far more powerful than the trickle of water. The scene was a stark contrast to Carina's excitement.

"Bambina! What's going on?" she asked, worried. She had stepped almost fully clothed into the shower to take her wife in her arms.

Maya had looked up, her face marked by distress.

-"I... I don't know if I can," she managed to articulate between sobs.

"My father was right. I'm not good enough to be a battalion. I don't belong here."

Carina had tightened her embrace, her heart breaking at seeing her wife so vulnerable.

- "No, no, listen to me." she'd told her gently.

"You're the best battalion Seattle has ever known. I'm so proud of you. I love you, and I'm going to support you like you supported me. You don't have to carry this weight alone."

Carina's words echoed in her wife's mind, but the pain was stubborn.

-"It's just that I... I can't go on like this. I feel overwhelmed, like I'm not up to it."

The brunette had stepped back slightly so that she could plunge her gaze into the battalion's.

-"Listen, I'm going to ask to leave the hospital early. I'll take care of the kids and give you time to work without all this pressure on your shoulders. You've got to stop thinking your father's words were the truth. They weren't. I love you, more than anything, and I'm sorry I didn't realize how unhappy you were."

A shiver had run through the blonde's body despite the warmth of the water, as she realized that Carina really saw her, that she understood her struggle. She'd clung to her, feeling love and support envelop her bruised heart.

-"I... I don't know what I'd do without you."

Carina had smiled through her wife's tears, her heart filling with hope.

- "We'll do it together, as always. This is our year, and we'll be there for each other."

Together, they huddled under the spray, their fears slowly dissolving as they realized they still had much to build together, united in the face of adversity.

On the morning of February fourth, soft light filtered through the curtains, enveloping the kitchen in comforting warmth. Carina had already risen, freshly brewed coffee perfuming the air. She had prepared some fluffy French toast, but had left the table empty, not daring to break the heavy silence that fell over the blonde.

Maya sat at the table, elbows resting on the glass top, staring at her plate without really seeing it. Her thoughts were swirling, gathering like the clouds of an impending storm. She knew her wife was watching, but she remained frozen in deep contemplation.

Carina, for her part, found it hard to conceal her concern. She wanted to give her companion space, but the anguish of not knowing what was going on in her mind assailed her. She wanted to reach out, to ask a question, but she was afraid of pushing Maya to speak before she was ready.

After a moment that had seemed interminable, the blonde had looked up slowly, meeting the Italian's gaze. Anxiety had mingled with a gleam of determination in her eyes.

-"I think I'll go and see Diane Lewis" she announced at last, her voice tinged with seriousness.

The brunette's heart had quickened at this statement. Diane, the fire department psychologist.

-"Why? Is something wrong?" she asked, fear creeping into her voice.

Maya had sighed, absentmindedly playing with her cup of coffee.

-"For the past few weeks, I've been giving sessions to all the firefighters under Robert's command. But I, too, need to talk to someone... someone I'm not in love with." She had paused, the sincerity of her words floating in the air. "I want to preserve our love, our beautiful reality. With Diane, I'll be able to reveal my worst demons, the ones I can't share with you."

Carina had nodded, her heart clenching at the thought of Maya feeling this way.

-"But I want to know everything about you," she murmured.

Maya had given her a sad, almost apologetic look.

- "Before I talk to you about all this, I need to sort out my past and everything that's happening today. I don't want to hurt you or say the wrong things."

The declaration had weighed heavy in the air, a promise of vulnerability. The Italian, though worried, had understood.

-"I understand," she replied softly. "I'll be there, no matter what you choose to share. I just want you to be okay."

The battalion had smiled at her, grateful. OB-GYN was an anchor in this storm. It would be a long road, but she was ready to take it, one step at a time.

The firefighter had entered Diane Lewis's office, her heart heavy but determined. The space was warm, with walls painted in soothing hues and comfortable armchairs. Diane, a woman in her fifties with a kindly air, had greeted her with a reassuring smile.

-"Hello, Maya. How are you feeling today?" asked Diane, inviting her to sit down.

Maya had taken a deep breath, searching for the right words.

-"I... I feel overwhelmed," she finally replied, her voice a little shaky.

"There have been so many changes recently, with Sullivan's death and everything that's happened at work..."

Diane had nodded, encouraging her to continue.

-"I understand. Talk to me about it."

Maya had rubbed her face, as if trying to clear her dark thoughts.

-"It's not just the workload. I've got three more stations to manage now, but that's not what scares me. I'm afraid I'm not up to it."

Diane had looked at her empathetically. "Maya, you've been doing a great job for over five years. And you only have these stations temporarily until a new battalion is appointed. What's making you doubt today?"

A heavy silence had fallen. Maya had let herself go, aware that this was the moment to free herself of her burdens.

-"Carina, my wife, is opening a private practice. She manages all our family life while I'm swamped with work. She recently stopped working at the hospital while she set up her practice, because I couldn't manage between my personal and professional responsibilities. I feel like I'm in a never-ending race."

Diane had noted the tension in her voice.

-"It must be difficult juggling so many responsibilities. You mentioned Sullivan's death. How did that affect you?"

Maya had inhaled deeply, her thoughts drifting to recent events.

-"Since his death, I've been feeling this enormous pressure. In addition to the overdue files, I've picked up those of three stations. And now I'm having nightmares again... nightmares about my father. He's telling me I can't be a good chief."

The words had escaped her, sadness and anxiety mingling in her voice.

"I thought I'd turned that corner. I thought I could feel entitled to be loved, but with all this stress, this feeling of having lost my wife for months, it's thrown me back into torment. I don't feel up to Carina, my job as a Battalion, or even being Danielle and Andrea's mother."

Maya had lowered her eyes, tears pooling under her eyelids.

"I tell myself I can't excel at everything."

Diane had listened attentively, then replied in a calm voice.

-"Maya, it's normal to feel overwhelmed, especially after suffering a loss. But remember all the times you've overcome obstacles. You have Carina's skills, determination and support. What you're feeling right now doesn't define who you are."

Maya had looked up, searching for strength in Diane's words.

- "But how can I handle everything without breaking down?"

- "You don't have to handle everything alone," Diane replied, with a warm smile. "Seek the support you need, talk to Carina about how you feel. Being vulnerable isn't a sign of weakness, it's a sign of strength. And I'm here to help you through it."

Maya had nodded slowly, a slight weight lifting from her shoulders. For the first time in a long time, she had felt a small ray of hope break through the dark clouds that surrounded her.

A few days later, on Valentine's Day, as they had finished yet another dance, Maya had backed away slowly, her hands sliding up Carina's arms. She had caressed her wife's cheek tenderly, her heart heavy with memories.

- "I had another appointment with Diane today," she'd begun, her voice soft but laden with emotion. "We talked about my childhood, about why I always want to win."

Carina had looked at her intently, her eyes full of understanding.

- "And what marked you, baby?"

Maya had taken a deep breath before continuing.

-"I remember one particular moment when I was only three. It was a family member's race, and my cousin, Blake, was competing. When Blake won, my father, Lane, was so proud of her... But when she lost, he was almost ashamed, as if he were disowning her. That day, I understood that I always had to win. It was as if my love and acceptance depended on my victories."

Tears had begun to well up in her eyes, and she'd continued:

"And then, when I almost lost your presence in my life with all the excitement that followed our wedding, it gave me this terrible feeling that I didn't deserve your love."

Carina had listened, her throat tight. She had taken Maya's hands in hers, caressing them gently.

-"Sweetheart, you never came close to losing my love. I love you more than anything. You're the most incredible person I know."

Maya had nodded, acknowledging her wife's sincerity.

-"I know. I know, but deep down, this fear persists. During my session, Diane helped me understand that I had to talk to the little girl inside me. She told me to forgive her, to tell her that I loved her, and that she could rest now. That I was loved for who I am, not for what I achieve."

Carina had bowed her head, her eyes full of compassion.

-"And how can I help you, my love?"

Maya had hesitated a moment before asking.

-"I need you to be there for me. For you to show me that you're with me in these difficult moments, when everything becomes too much to bear."

Carina had smiled softly, her eyes shining with affection.

-"You know what I'm going to do? I'm going to sing you our song." She'd taken a deep breath and started singing the lyrics to «Love the Hell Out of You» her voice echoing around the room. The words described her commitment, her promise to be there for Maya every step of the way, in darkness and light.

The blonde had closed her eyes, letting herself be enveloped by the melody and the love emanating from her wife. When Carina had finished, she had caressed the battalion's cheek before kissing her gently, sealing the pact of their love. In that vulnerable moment, Maya felt a wave of peace wash over her. She knew that together they could overcome all obstacles, face the hard knocks and turn failures into lessons.

A few days later, Maya had another session. When she returned home after her session with Diane, the smell of the dinner Carina had prepared wafted through the air, warming the atmosphere of the house.

The blonde had approached the brunette, who had smiled tenderly.

"So, how did it go today?" she asked as she set the table.

Maya had sighed, before taking a deep breath.

-"Diane talked about the pressure I put on myself to excel. She made me realize that I'm not alone, and that it's normal to feel insecure, especially after everything that's happened recently."

Carina had given her an encouraging look.

-"That's good to hear. You know, I've always seen you as an incredible battalion. But what's worrying you most right now?"

Maya had looked down, playing with the bracelet she wore.

-"I told her about my fear of disappointing you, of not measuring up. Sometimes I wonder if I deserve all this love." Maya's voice was both soft and full of doubt, an echo of her anxieties.

Carina had approached, gently taking her wife's face between her hands.

-"You're worthy of all this love, baby. I've never considered you less than what you are. You know I'm here for you, no matter what. How about we talk about what you learned at Diane's? I could even accompany you one day, if you'd like."

Maya had thought for a moment, then nodded.

-"Maybe that would be nice. But there are some things I want to work on first. I don't want to hurt you with my doubts, not when everything seems so beautiful between us."

The days leading up to the practice opening had been a race against time. Carina stood in her office, surrounded by scattered files, decorating plans and medical equipment. The walls were taking shape, painted in soothing hues, and the furniture had finally been delivered, adding a touch of warmth to the space. But despite the palpable excitement, Carina felt a growing distance from her companion.

Every morning, the Italian woke up with the hope that this day would be different. She wanted to rediscover the complicity they'd had before all this. But the battalion often seemed lost in thought, and the discussions that had followed her sessions with Diane had become fleeting fragments. There was a new weight on her shoulders, a melancholy she couldn't quite break through.

That day, as she adjusted the last chairs in the waiting room, Carina had paused, watching the telephone that sat on her desk. She wondered if she should call Maya and suggest a quiet evening, just the two of them, away from obligations and stress. But she hesitated, afraid of putting too much pressure on her wife.

A little later, Maya had finally entered the office, her hair slightly disheveled and her look a little tired.

- "Hi," she said, a tired smile on her lips. "How's it coming along?"

- "We're almost ready," Carina replied with feigned enthusiasm, hiding her concerns behind a smile.

"There are still a few finishing touches. I thought tonight we could order something to go and spend some time together. What do you think?"

Maya had looked down, a flicker of regret in her gaze.

- "I... I don't know, Carina. I've got a session with Diane tonight. She wants us to discuss some things that are weighing on me. I think it's important."

Carina's heart clenched.

-"I understand," she replied, trying to mask her disappointment. "But I want you to know that I'm here for you. I wish I could help you through this."

Maya had smiled weakly at her, acknowledging her wife's patience and understanding.

-"I know that, and it means a lot to me. But I also want to be honest with myself, and that includes the fact that I need some time to find myself."

Carina had nodded, even though the idea that her wife might be talking more to Diane than to her troubled her.

- "Okay, but promise me we'll find a time to talk. I really want to know what's going on in your head."

Maya had come up and taken her in her arms, tightening her embrace.

-"I promise. Just a little more time, please."

As Maya broke away, Carina had watched her face, a wave of love and concern washing over her. The foundations between them were moving forward, but she knew that connecting with her wife also required effort, last-minute adjustments in their relationship. She hoped that, like the practice, their love would mend in time.

Sunlight shone through the cabinet's large windows, casting warm reflections on the freshly painted walls. It was finally the big day. Carina, dressed in an elegant blouse, stood in front of the mirror, her heart racing. Opening her reproductive medicine practice, among others, in Seattle had been a dream long in the making, but now it was about to become a reality.

Gabriella was there, taking a photo of Carina while smiling.

-"You're beautiful, bella. This day is yours."

Investors from New York and Seattle had already arrived, chatting in the lobby. Jo Wilson, her obstetrician, stood near the entrance, a big smile on her face, while Cosima Nieuhaus, the new gynecologist specializing in reproductive health, examined a document on the table.

-"Is everything ready?" asked Jo, her eyes sparkling with enthusiasm.

- "I think so," replied Carina, her stomach knotting in anticipation. "I just hope everything goes well."

Maya had entered by then, her presence bringing some comfort to her wife. Although she had her own concerns, she had made the effort to be there to support her wife.

- "How are you?" she asked, seeing the anxiety in OB-GYN's eyes.

- "A little nervous," admitted the Italian. "But I'm ready."

The firefighter friends, along with their fellow doctors from Grey Sloan and Northern Women's Healthcare, streamed into the room, transforming the space into a vibrant place of warmth and joy. The atmosphere was charged with palpable energy, everyone smiling and sharing well wishes.

Carina had taken a deep breath, listening to the soft murmur of conversation. She stood at the forefront of the inauguration, ready to cut the ribbon. It was a symbol of so much effort, sacrifice and determination.

Maya, though surrounded by smiles, felt a slight distance. She watched her wife, dazzled by the way she shone in the midst of her friends and colleagues, and felt a touch of melancholy. She wanted to be there, but the emotional challenges she'd been facing for weeks seemed to be keeping her away.

When the moment for the inauguration had finally arrived, Carina had spoken, her voice resonating with palpable passion.

"Thank you all for being here today. This practice represents not only my dream, but our dream as a family and community. I can't wait to see the good we can do together."

The applause had resounded, and the Italian had turned to her wife, who had given her an encouraging smile. It was a pivotal moment, a celebration of their love and commitment to each other. Carina knew that even if the road ahead was full of pitfalls, they would face them all together.

The party was in full swing, punctuated by laughter and lively discussion. The Italian woman moved among her guests, greeting each one with overflowing joy. But deep inside, she could sense her partner's concern, a dark cloud hovering over their happiness.

Later that evening, as the guests sat down to eat, Carina made her way to the battalion.

-"How are you feeling, bambina?" she asked, placing a hand on her arm.

Maya had smiled, but it seemed to her that something was missing.

- "It's incredible, my love. I'm really proud of you." She'd paused, her eyes brightening slightly. "But... I feel a little left out of it all."

Carina had looked at her, her heart clenching.

- "I want you here with me, sweetheart. I can't do this without you."

Maya had inhaled deeply.

- "I know. And I'm here, I am. But I think it's time for me to take a step forward. I want to see Diane, with you. I'm ready, and I'd like you to be there with me."

Carina had smiled, tears in her eyes.

-"I'll come with you. I'll be there, every step of the way... Bambina, I'd go to the ends of the earth for you..."

As the evening progressed, the lights twinkled, the sound of laughter and chatter surrounded the two women. This moment marked the beginning of a new chapter for them, a symbol of their collective strength. Together, they were ready to face the challenges ahead, united in their love and determination.
