The flight back from Africa had been a mixture of fatigue and anticipation. Carina and Maya, still bathed in the glow of memories of their trip, felt like adventurers returning from an enchanting journey. Images of majestic landscapes, thrilling safaris and unforgettable moments of intimacy still floated through their minds, creating a striking contrast with the urban landscape of Seattle that had greeted them on their arrival.

As soon as they set foot on the airport tarmac, a shiver of apprehension ran down Carina's spine. The familiar sound of airport announcements and the smell of coffee from the airport stores were immediate reminders of the daily life that awaited them. The bright colors of the African sun were slowly fading, replaced by the usual gray of Seattle clouds. Reality seemed almost overwhelming.

On the way home, conversation slowly dried up as they crossed the city. Memories of days gone by, filled with laughter and discovery, were beginning to fade into nostalgia. The car, once full of laughter and exclamations of wonder, was now silent. The children were daydreaming, tired from the adventures of the previous days, while Carina and Maya were lost in thought.

The transition to everyday reality had not been as smooth as they had hoped. The days were filled with obligations, meetings and preparations for the opening of Carina's practice. The warmth of African memories contrasted sharply with the logistical worries of returning to normal life. Carina sank back into her work, rediscovering the files scattered across her desk, the calls from clients and the worries of opening her practice. Every document, every e-mail, reminded her that she had to resume her role as future boss, but she found it hard to leave behind the euphoria of their vacation.

Maya, for her part, was beginning to feel the pressure of her own responsibilities with the Seattle Fire Department. She needed to immerse herself in work to distract herself from the emotional distance that had built up between them. Emergency calls, training and operations were piling up, but a little voice inside her whispered that she also needed to find a balance with her new family life. She sorely missed the memory of their children's laughter on the African plains and evenings spent gazing at the sky under the stars.

The first few days in Seattle had been marked by a reunion with the banality of everyday life: shopping, household chores and financial concerns. The children, though excited to be back in their rooms and with their toys, were having a hard time adjusting after so many adventures. Andrea and Danielle, once full of energy, seemed tired and irritable. This weighed heavily on the family atmosphere.

Evenings, once devoted to cuddling and laughter, were now invaded by stress. The Italian worked late in her office, and the blonde lost herself in thought on the way home from the barracks, wondering how they were going to balance their careers and family life. The passion that had characterized their romance seemed to fade in the face of the reality of responsibilities and expectations.

One night, when they were finally together again after a long day, Carina had sat down on the sofa, exhausted. She turned her head towards Maya, who had just returned, and a sigh escaped her lips.

-"I feel so overwhelmed. It's as if every moment of joy we experienced in Africa has evaporated."

Maya had nodded, her own heart heavy with feeling.

-"I understand. It's hard to come back to this life after so much magic."

This conversation had marked the beginning of a shared realization: they had to find a way to reintegrate moments of joy and connection into their daily lives, even in the most mundane moments. It was a struggle, but they were determined not to let routine consume them.

After a few days' reflection, they found themselves in their kitchen, surrounded by memories of their trip to Africa. The laughter of children playing in the garden had echoed in the background, but there was a palpable tension between them, resulting from the rush of reality that had engulfed them. The brunette had turned to her wife, her eyes seeking to capture her attention.

-"We've got to do something," Carina began, her voice full of sincerity.

"I don't want us to lose the magic we found together in Africa. That connection, that happiness... it's precious."

Maya had nodded, her heart clenching at the thought of what they stood to lose.

-"I agree. I don't want everyday life to rob us of all this. We've had such special times, and I want to keep it that way."

They had then taken a moment to commit to working hand in hand.

"We need to be aware of our needs, make time for each other," Maya offered.

"No matter how busy our lives are, we need to make space for each other. Maybe a weekly rendezvous, just the two of us, to discuss our dreams, our fears, and our future."

Carina had smiled, relieved by this promise.

-"And I also want us to keep the little things that bring us together. Lovers' dinners, game nights with the kids, or even impromptu getaways whenever possible."

Maya had nodded, feeling the warmth of her partner's support.

- "Then let's do it. We have to fight for this magic."

However, despite their pact, the return to reality had come quickly, and it hadn't taken long for everyday life to take over again. The gynecologist was immersed in preparations for the opening of her Seattle practice, a task that absorbed most of her energy and time. By morning, she was dealing with architects, contractors and suppliers, immersing herself in the details of her project.

The first few weeks went by in the blink of an eye. The brunette spent hours on the phone or in meetings, her enthusiasm palpable. The idea of creating a space where she could offer exceptional care to women and children filled her with immense joy. Every time she talked about her practice, her eyes sparkled with excitement. She could already see the walls decorated in soothing colors, the welcoming waiting rooms, and above all, the familiar smell of coffee waiting for patients.

Yet with this passion came growing stress. Deadlines were piling up, contractors were slow to meet their commitments, and she often felt caught in a vortex of uncertainty. She found herself worrying about every detail: the materials to be used, the layout of the examination rooms, the medical supplies. She kept a notebook in her hand, scribbling down ideas, reminders, action plans.

One evening, when she returned home after a particularly exhausting day, she found Maya preparing dinner. The delicious smell of tomato sauce filled the kitchen, but Carina couldn't help but feel the weight of worry.

- "How was your day?" asked Maya, as she stirred the pasta.

- "As usual" replied Carina, dropping into a chair with a tired look on her face. "We had a meeting about the layout of the practice. But I'm starting to worry. Everything doesn't seem to be moving forward as quickly as I'd like."

Maya had approached, laying a comforting hand on her wife's shoulder.

-"You know, it's normal to feel this way. You've got a lot to deal with right now. But remember, you're incredibly capable, and it's all worth it."

Carina had sighed, acknowledging her partner's effort to support her.

-"I know, but I don't want this to affect our relationship. I don't want us to get lost in the stress."

- "We'll get through this," Maya had assured her, with an encouraging smile. "We made a pact, didn't we? So let's stick to it. We'll spend time together, even if it's just a moment at the end of the day. It'll help us keep this magic alive."

Carina had nodded, determined not to let the pressure of work alter their love. Together, they had set about preparing dinner, letting the children's laughter echo through their home, a gentle melody reminding them that, despite the challenges, their life together continued to be an adventure.

As the days passed, the brunette had enthusiastically shared her ideas and dreams about opening her practice. She spent hours discussing the layout, the services offered and even the atmosphere she wanted to create. The battalion, for her part, had listened with admiration, touched by the passion shining in her partner's eyes. However, she had also begun to feel a slight shift. OB-GYN seemed so absorbed in her projects that she became almost unapproachable, and at times, the firefighter felt a little lost in this new dynamic.

-"I've been thinking about what we could do in the waiting room," Carina had suggested one evening, her hands full of excitement.

"I want the patients to feel at home, surrounded by warm colors, maybe even plants. It could really bring a welcoming atmosphere!"

Maya had nodded, forcing herself to smile.

-"That's a lovely idea. I know how much it means to you." Yet behind that smile, she questioned herself.

How could I find my place in all this? She admired Carina's dedication, but a little voice in her head whispered that she wasn't up to the task.

Maya rose slowly from the table, feeling a heaviness in her heart. The brunette was still immersed in her preparations for the practice, absorbed in plans and diagrams, as evening slowly fell in Seattle. The American hesitated for a moment, then headed for the bedroom, leaving her wife to her work. She needed solitude to soothe her tormented thoughts.

Once lying in their bed, she had stared at the ceiling, losing herself in a flood of emotions. The subdued light from the bedside lamp cast shadows across the room, creating a soft but heavy atmosphere.

I thought Gabriella's arrival in our lives had been the worst period, she said to herself, thinking back to that time.

When she came to Carina with the idea of opening a practice in New York, I thought it would upset our equilibrium. But now, look where we are...

She had closed her eyes for a moment, recalling the moment when the project had evolved.

I came up with this idea of turning this notion into two practices... One in New York for Gabriella and one in Seattle for my wonderful wife. I was delighted not to lose Carina, to keep her close to me. But I hadn't thought that I would feel so alone again...

A shiver of loneliness had invaded her.

I've never felt so alone since she came into my life. Especially in the last two years...

She had begun to think about their life together.

Why do I have this feeling that we're drifting apart? I know she loves me, but every day I feel like she's slipping through my fingers. The euphoria of our wedding and honeymoon is fading faster than a cloud after rain.

Her thoughts became darker and darker.

Do we really have anything in common? Are we really made for each other?

Fear gripped her heart. She felt overwhelmed again by the pressure of everything around her.

Why do I always have to feel out of step? Carina talks about things that are beyond me, and I feel like I'm not up to it. This distance between us scares me. What's really going on between us?

Tears had begun to flow, mingling with her doubts.

How could I live without Carina?

The very thought was unbearable. The pain almost suffocated her, making each breath harder than the last. She imagined their lives separated, and it frightened her to the core. Maya had closed her eyes, trying to banish these dark thoughts, but they persisted, making the loneliness of this moment even more poignant.

She remembered their bursts of laughter, the projects they'd shared, and the warmth of Carina's hand in hers. That connection now seems so fragile.

I need my wife to be close to me, to rediscover our complicity. But for now, only the darkness of the bedroom keeps me company, reinforcing this sense of loss.

The conversations had often taken a passionate turn, but it had become clear that something was going on beneath the surface. Carina was so involved in her project that she had begun to neglect their time together. The one-on-one dinners they had so enjoyed were being replaced by evenings spent making calls or polishing presentations. Maya had begun to feel a slight jealousy, not towards Carina, but rather towards what her work represented: a success she hadn't yet achieved in her own career.

One evening, as they sat down to dinner, the Italian, still planning her next day, let slip a remark.

-"I really must finish this presentation by tomorrow. It's important to me."

Maya, exasperated by this one-sided concentration, had reacted.

-"Carina, you're still at work. When are we going to spend time together? I thought we'd agreed to spend quiet evenings, just the two of us."

The brunette had frowned, surprised by her wife's reaction.

-"But I can't overlook this! This is my dream come true, and I need to get involved. You know how much this means to me."

The battalion had then felt a lump of emotion in her throat, both frustrated and hurt.

-"I understand, but I also want our relationship to count. Just because you have a dream doesn't mean you can forget everything else. I don't want to become a distraction for you."

The words had echoed between them, and a heavy silence had fallen. Carina had realized how devalued Maya felt, not because of a lack of affection, but because of the way she'd let herself get caught up in her ambition. She had tried to reassure her wife, but the tension had built up, turning a simple conversation into an argument.

Finally, Carina had taken a deep breath, trying to refocus.

-"I'm sorry, my love. I don't want you to feel left out. It's just... there's so much to do and I don't want to fail."

Maya had looked at her wife, seeing the fatigue in her eyes, a fatigue that spoke to the intensity of her commitment.

-"I don't want you to fail either. I want you to succeed, but I also want us to succeed together. We need to find a balance."

Carina had realized that, despite her enthusiasm, she needed to be aware of the impact her work was having on their relationship. So they had agreed to re-establish moments for the two of them, promising to respect those moments, not to be overwhelmed by the stress of preparations.

This moment of tension had been a turning point, a reminder that love and success could coexist, but only if they both gave it their all. They decided to fight to maintain the magic of their relationship, while pursuing their respective dreams.

By mid-September, as autumn slowly began to settle in Seattle, Carina had devoted herself to the creation of her practice with passion. Renovations had been underway for a few weeks, but the opening date was fast approaching. She had imagined every detail, every wall color, every piece of furniture, but she hadn't anticipated the unexpected.

One morning, on her way to the site for an inspection visit, the Italian had received a call from the contractor. Her usually assured voice trembled slightly.

-"Doctor DeLuca, we have a problem. One of our employees accidentally damaged a water pipe. We have a flood."

Carina's heart had begun to beat faster.

Un'alluvione? [A flood?]

She'd tried to keep her composure, but the image of water running over the new floors she'd carefully chosen haunted her.

-"How long will it take to fix?" she had asked, her voice betraying her concern.

The contractor had sighed.

-"I'm afraid it will take at least two weeks to solve the problem and get things back in order. We'll have to dry everything out, and that could delay our schedule."

Frustration and stress had overcome her.

Due settimane! [Two weeks!]

That meant she'd have to push back the opening of her practice, and each day's delay meant a step backwards in her dream. She hung up the phone, her stomach in knots with anxiety, wondering how she could manage this situation while maintaining her personal equilibrium.

For the rest of the day, she had juggled calls with contractors, finding temporary solutions and working on her current projects, as well as appointments with her patients at the hospital. Her patience had been tested, and every little incident, every delay in the work, had only added to her stress. She felt trapped in an endless cycle of problems to solve, which reinforced her feeling of being overwhelmed.

The firefighter had tried to be supportive, but the tension in her voice was palpable.

Lei non capisce quanto mi riguarda. [She doesn't understand how this affects me], she thought.

È molto più di un semplice ritardo: è in gioco il mio sogno. [It's so much more than a simple delay; it's my dream that's at stake].

The walls that were to house her new practice now seemed insurmountable barriers, and every moment lost reinforced her insecurity.

Carina had let herself be overwhelmed by a flood of emotions. Why did it all seem so out of reach? She had always dreamed of this practice, this space where she could exercise her passion, but with every complication, every delay, this dream seemed to grow further away. She had looked at Maya, who stood there, ready to support her, but a feeling of incomprehension had assailed her.

Forse non si rende conto di quanto questo significhi per me. Tutto ciò che deve fare è diventare capo battaglione e basta. Ma mi sento perso in questa ricerca. [Maybe she doesn't realize how much this means to me. She has her own path all mapped out; she just has to be battalion chief, and that's it. But I feel lost in this quest].

A wave of guilt had hit her.

Maya non c'entra nulla. Non dovrei arrabbiarmi con lei. Ha fatto così tanto per integrare le donne in tutte le stazioni del suo distretto e io sto lasciando che le mie frustrazioni abbiano la meglio su di me. [Maya has nothing to do with it. I shouldn't be angry with her. She's done so much to integrate women into all the stations in her district, and I'm letting my frustrations get the better of me].

Carina had felt a deep fatigue, not only physical, but also emotional.

Sono così stanca. [I'm so tired] she confessed in the silence of her mind.

Tra il mio lavoro alla Northwest Women's Healthcare, questo progetto pratico, la mia condizione di donna, la mia vita familiare... Come posso gestire tutto questo?

Per fortuna c'è Maya. Anche se a volte sembra distante da ciò che sto vivendo, è sempre presente per sostenermi. Si è occupata dei bambini da quando siamo tornati dall'Africa per darmi il tempo di realizzare i miei sogni. So che porta un peso sulle sue spalle. È incredibile; non so cosa farei senza di lei.

[Between my work at Northwest Women's Healthcare, this practice project, my status as a woman, as a wife, my family life... How can I manage it all?

Thank goodness for Maya. Even if she sometimes seems distant from what I'm going through, she's always there to support me. She's been looking after the children since we got back from Africa, giving me time to make my dreams come true. I know she carries a weight on her own shoulders. She's incredible; without her, I don't know what I'd do].

As she lost herself in thought, the world around her seemed to fade away, but in an instant, reality had caught up with her. She had looked at her wife, deeply, and a surge of love had overwhelmed her.

Ti amo.

She had thought, sincerity embracing her heart.

Ti amo così tanto. [I love you so much.]

She had come closer and placed her hands on Maya's cheeks, looking into her eyes, feeling the warmth and tenderness that bound them together.

-"Dispiace". [I'm sorry]
She whispered inwardly, her voice trembling with emotion.

"I'm so sorry I'm not the woman you married right now." She had kissed her tenderly, their way of reminding each other that they meant everything to each other.

The gesture had sealed a moment of deep connection, a silent promise to stand by each other through thick and thin.

anche se i giorni sono difficili, il nostro amore può superare tutti gli ostacoli. [Even if the days are difficult, our love can overcome all obstacles]. She thought, hoping that the strength of their feelings would carry them through the uncertainties.
