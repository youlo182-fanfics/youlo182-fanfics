They had to save it, for the memories of the days spent at Snoqualmie Falls with Carina and the children.

The fire had surrounded them, escaping every attempt to contain it. Maya had donned her gloves, tightened her helmet, and thrown herself into the middle of the action, unable to stand back as her teams fought with all their might. The firefighters were pushing back the flames meter by meter, battling against the stifling heat and dense smoke that made every breath painful.

Suddenly, a scream pierced the din of crackling flames. A faint but desperate female voice had called for help. Maya had exchanged a glance with De Sousa, her face covered in soot, her gaze determined.

"Did you hear her?" cried Maya.

De Sousa had nodded, and both had begun to search, scanning the surroundings with their lamps. Then, without warning, a tongue of fire shot out, isolating Maya in the middle of an infernal circle.

She felt her heart freeze. The fire surrounded her, cutting off all escape. The flames drew closer, devouring everything in their path. Maya had looked around in panic for De Sousa, but her teammate's silhouette was blurred and distant. She could hear nothing but the muffled roar of the flames closing in on her.

No, no, no, this can't be. It can't end like this. Not today, not here, not this way... especially not today.

Maya felt panic grip her as the flames danced around her, threatening her, closing in.

I've been with Carina for ten years now. Ten years... I loved her long before that, for three whole years, in silence, before we were together. I've loved her for thirteen years, and we've been married for eight. I can't die now, not on our anniversary.

She had drawn in a shaky breath, feeling the weight of the years behind her and all those she still wished to live.

I've waited so long for this moment, this life together. Ten years, and it's not enough. I want twenty years, fifty, an eternity. Carina, my soul mate, my everything... I want to grow old beside her, to feel her hand in mine when we're wrinkled and gray.

Maya had closed her eyes for a moment, trying to gather her thoughts as images of her children invaded her mind.

And my babies...

She had felt a dull pain pierce her.

Andrea, my little prince, already almost fifteen, with his dreams and hesitations between becoming a firefighter like his mother or a video game designer. Andrea, my son, so brave and so sweet... And Danielle, my little thirteen-year-old princess, mischievous and confident, with her dream of becoming a marine animal vet. How could I abandon them?

She had struggled not to lose her footing, but her mind kept reminding her of all she risked losing.

And my little Stefania, my little angel, the flesh of my flesh, barely four years old. She's so young... she won't have any memory of me, she'll only remember photos, stories told by others. I can't leave them. And Carina, my Carina, the one I promised I'd always come back to. That promise... it keeps me going every day. It's what brings me back every time.

She had seen scenes of a future that seemed about to slip away: sunny mornings with Carina by her side; the laughter of children running through the garden; even moments shared at Snoqualmie Falls. A life so beautiful and full of promise.

I can't abandon them. I can't break the promise of growing old with her, of staying by her side until we're very old. I can't... not now...

A vision crossed her mind, cruel and unbearable:

Who's going to carry my coffin? Will it be my team-mates, firefighters from my own stations, or my friends from Station 19? Who will ring my last three bells? Maybe Andy... she's my best friend, and she's a captain, so it would be an honor. But... how could Carina bear it? She hadn't even been able to utter a word for Danny. Would she find the strength to say anything for me?

The thought of Carina, standing alone in front of her coffin, had wrenched an almost physical pain from her then.

No, no...

she thought in despair.

I can't think about my funeral, or whether my parents and brother, despite the years of silence, would come for this final farewell. Would I be entitled to a service with bagpipes, a guard of honor? Why bother?

Maya had shaken her head, struggling to banish these fateful thoughts. She had a promise to keep.

I promised my wife that we'd grow old together, that we'd leave, hand in hand, when we were very old. I can't die here, not now. Carina, my children... I've got to find a way out of here, I've got to come back for them.

The flames continued to crackle around her, a ring of burning fire that threatened to engulf her at any moment. She curled up, eyes closed, concentrating with all her might on the image of her family, on their love that gave her the strength to fight on.

Suddenly, in the midst of the chaos, the voice of Captain De Sousa, who had recently become captain, had finally reached her, piercing her dark thoughts:

-"Chief DeLuca-Bishop! Get down! Now!"

Without thinking, Maya had dropped to the ground, her instincts taking over. A muffled roar had sounded above her, and she'd suddenly felt an icy rain fall on her. The rotocanadere had released its water just then, extinguishing the ring of fire around her.

Maya had remained motionless for a few seconds, trembling, struggling to catch her breath. She was alive. She would see her wife and children again.

Hours had passed, and the team's hard work had begun to pay off. The noise of the pumps and the crackle of the flames had finally given way to relative tranquillity as they had managed, with the help of the various captains, lieutenants and firefighters, to contain the fire on the western flank. Around 4 p.m., the sun had slowly declined, offering a spectacle of golden light over the devastated landscape.

When they returned to camp, they were greeted by thunderous applause and congratulations. Maya, still sweaty and covered in soot, had raised her arms to encourage her teams, her heart swelling with pride. She'd asked Ross where they could still help, eager to do more.

- "DeLuca-Bishop, you're coming home. It's your wedding anniversary," Ross had ordered in a firm tone. "Your teams can lend a hand to the Queen battalion on the eastern flank."

Maya had hesitated.

-"I can stay a little longer, I-"

- "That's an order," Ross had cut in. "Be proud of yourselves. You're an exceptional battalion. If the fire isn't contained tomorrow, you can come back. But tonight, you must dedicate this moment to your wife."

Maya had felt a mixture of gratitude and sadness.

-"Thank you, Chief."

She had then approached her teams, her voice embraced with emotion.

-"I'd like to thank you all for your courage and dedication. You've been incredible. Stay safe and give everything you've got, as you always do, I have every confidence in you."

The firefighters had nodded, some exchanging tired but satisfied smiles. She had watched them walk away to their trucks, knowing that they would continue to battle the flames with their characteristic determination.

Maya had left the camp, her heart heavy but soothed. Looking in her rear-view mirror, she had caught sight of the flames, still present but far less threatening than this morning. The smoke, though still visible, no longer stretched for miles. It was a heartbreaking yet comforting sight.

As she drove home, she thought of Carina and their children. Once again, she had not broken her promise to them. She would go home to them. However, one thought had assailed her:

It hadn't gone far this time.

A light breeze had caressed her face, and she had promised herself to cherish every moment with her family. This evening would be dedicated to their love, and nothing could take that away from her.

Maya had lowered her head as she felt her wife's heart. She knew this story wasn't over. She had kissed her trapeze, whispering «I love you so much». She had closed her eyes and inhaled her scent.

Andrea had ended up playing video games, Danielle had gone to call her friend Aspen, who was coming from Los Angeles for a week in a few days, and Stefania had gone to sleep while they had stayed under the stars.

A few days later, while Maya was in her office at Station 46, she'd received a phone call from Natacha Ross asking her to come to SFD headquarters the next day for her one-on-one interview. It had been less than six months since her last interview, and the blonde had begun to feel anxious. What if asking Maya to leave after the forest fire north of Seattle had been a test and she'd failed?

Suffice to say, she hadn't really slept that night.

The hallway was bathed in light, the walls adorned with photos of fire crews over the years. She had felt a slight nervousness, an anticipation mingled with apprehension. She had always admired Natacha Ross, a strong woman who had made her mark in a male-dominated environment.

Maya took a deep breath before entering Fire Chief Ross's office. When she crossed the threshold, Natacha greeted her with a nod.

-"DeLuca-Bishop, thank you for coming. Please take a seat."

The blonde had sat down, her heart beating slightly faster.

Maya had crossed her fingers, weighing her words.

-"Thank you, Chief Ross. I'll think about the opportunity."

Leaving the office, Maya had felt a mixture of excitement and anxiety. What could have been a simple meeting had turned into a potential turning point in her career. She now knew she was facing a choice that could redefine her life.

The next day, after working through the night, as they sipped coffee on a terrace at Pike Place Market, Maya had shared her thoughts with Carina.

Their eyes had met, and Maya had felt a wave of warmth invade her heart. She knew that, despite the challenges, they had always been able to find a balance. The future was uncertain, but with Carina by her side, she felt ready to face it.
