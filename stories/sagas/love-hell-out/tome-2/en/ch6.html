/Start ofthe nightmare - Flashback/

In the darkness of sleep, Maya found herself transported to a familiar but tension-filled place. She was standing on the high school racetrack, surrounded by classmates who had always admired her for her speed. The heat of the afternoon sun had weighed on her shoulders as she prepared for a race that should have been a victory. But that day, she wasn't feeling well. The symptoms of illness had overwhelmed her, and despite her best efforts, she had finished well behind the others.

The echo of applause had given way to a heavy silence. She had seen her father, his imposing silhouette standing out from the crowd, the features of his face distorted by anger. He had approached her, and the tone of his voice had resounded in her ears like a thunderclap.

-"You've let our whole family down," he'd spat, his voice laden with contempt. "You're nothing but a weakling."

Maya, frozen in place, had felt the weight of his gaze on her, every word hitting her like a stone. She remembered the fear that had overcome her as he continued to scream, ignoring her condition and her efforts. He'd never raised a hand to her, but his anger had always had a paralyzing effect.

In this nightmare, she had been transported to another time, imagining her father in their dining room, at a meal, throwing dishes against the walls in a black rage. She'd seen the shattering of dishes, the smell of food mingling with terror.

And then she'd had a vision of herself, repeating the cycle. She had imagined being that mother, screaming at Andrea and Danielle, making them tremble with fear. She had seen Carina, devastated, trying to calm the situation, her face scarred by the pain of not being able to protect their children. The thought of repeating this pattern had made her nauseous, but she hadn't been able to shake it.

/End ofnightmare - end of Flashback/

Maya had woken with a start, her heart racing. The night was dark and silent, but the echo of her father's screams still echoed in her mind. She understood that these fears had followed her to that moment, raising doubts about her future commitment.

When she awoke, Maya had breathed deeply, still troubled by the nightmare. Tears had misted her eyes as she tried to banish the terrifying images from her mind. It wasn't just the dream that had disturbed her, but the realization she'd experienced during the night.

She had realized that she had always been afraid of becoming like her father, of giving in to that dull anger that had poisoned her childhood. The idea of being in her father's shoes, treating Danielle and Andrea with contempt when they failed, had been like a slap in the face. The prospect of showing them that she wasn't proud of them, that she could inflict on them the same psychological pain he'd inflicted on her, was unbearable. She had always fought to be the loving, caring mother, but this nightmare had dug a hole in her confidence.

Maya had also thought of Carina, of the way her father had treated her mother. She had wondered if, in moments of stress or fatigue, she might forget to show Carina all the love she had for her. The fear that one day Carina would come to fear her, that she would feel obliged to shave the walls to avoid her emotional storms, had chilled her. She'd never wanted it to be that way.

This revelation had left her in a state of deep anguish. She knew she should talk to Carina, share her fears, but an inner voice urged her to remain silent, not to add weight to an already delicate situation. Yet she couldn't continue to carry this burden alone.

Carina had woken up to Maya's sobs. She had blinked, realizing that she was no longer lying beside her, but sitting with her back against the headboard. Worried, she had straightened up and turned to Maya.

-"What's going on, amore?" she asked, her voice soft and gentle.

Maya had tried to calm her distraught heart and wipe away her tears. The words had come out on their own, like a painful release. She had begun to recount her father's angry outbursts when she was young, her memories tinged with frustration and fear. She had explained that now, as they were about to get married and she would officially become Andrea and Danielle's mother, she had this nagging fear of reproducing the pattern of emotional abuse in which she had grown up.

She recognized that she had never really shared these memories with Carina. She knew her fiancée was aware that she no longer spoke to her parents, but the weight of her past remained silent between them. Her father and brother were not «gay friendly», and her mother, Katherine, had separated from him for a time, before returning to his side, choosing, in her heart, to side with the man who had inflicted so much suffering on them. What Lane, her father, had done to them had profoundly marked their childhood, and this story worried her more than anything.

Carina had placed a comforting hand on Maya's shoulder, encouraging her to continue. She had listened attentively, her heart clenching with each revelation.

-"Listen", she had begun tenderly, "there's no way you'll end up like your father. You're exceptional with children. You love them far too much."

Maya had looked up, seeking comfort in Carina's gaze.

-"You have to trust yourself," Carina had continued, her voice soft but firm. The fact that you have this fear is already a good start. It shows that you're aware of how you feel, and that you want to be different. I know you'll never do to them what your father did to them."

The tears had stopped rolling down Maya's cheeks, and a slight smile had begun to form on her lips.

"I trust you" she had whispered.

Carina had smiled, taking her into her arms for a comforting hug. Together, they had begun to build walls of safety around their fears, strengthening their love and determination to create a family that would never know the pain they had experienced.

Carina had taken a deep breath, feeling the emotional connection between them grow stronger as she shared her own past.

"I understand how you feel," she'd begun, her voice filled with compassion.

"I myself grew up with a bipolar father. He spent the family's money on his crazy ideas about improbable medical advances. He spoke badly to us, often in fits of rage. It was hard to live in that atmosphere."

Maya had looked at her, her eyes bright with tears, but imbued with understanding.

"That's why," Carina had continued, "I pushed my little brother, Andrea, to go and live with our mamma when Lucia, my mother, and Vincenzo, my father, split up. My father wanted to keep one of his children, and I decided to sacrifice myself to keep my little brother away from him. I didn't want him to go through what I'd been through... but as you know, when I left for university, he rebuilt his life and cut Andrea and me off..."

Memories had resurfaced in Carina's mind, but she'd found comfort in focusing on the strength of their love.

"I know you'll never be like Lane or Vincenzo," she'd asserted with conviction.

"You're far too aware of what it means to be a parent. Your love for Andrea and Danielle is so strong, so genuine. You are already a wonderful mother to them, and I have no doubt that you will remain so. You and I are going to break the family patterns we grew up in... together, hand in hand."

Maya had nodded, touched by Carina's vulnerability. The idea of seeing similarities between her and her father had faded, replaced by the conviction that love could break the chains of the past. She had taken Carina's hands between hers, a silent gesture of gratitude and commitment.

- "Thank you" she'd whispered, her voice trembling.

"I'm going to do my best to be the mother they deserve, just like you."

- "When Andrea missed his goal last week at soccer and was sad, you hugged him and told him you were proud of him for trying and that that was the main thing, that it wasn't a failure... bambina... you're not your father."

The blonde had smiled, a glimmer of hope lighting up her face. Together, they had decided not to let their pasts define them, but to build a future where love, trust and understanding would reign.

The Warren-Bailey home had been beautifully decorated for Thanksgiving. Autumn leaves surrounded a long table where a sumptuous feast was ready to be served. Maya and Carina, accompanied by Andrea and Danielle, had been warmly welcomed by Ben, who smiled at them while wearing a sauce-stained apron.

- "You're right on time, come in, come in!" he said, amused.

Miranda Bailey, busy watching the turkey cook, had given them a smile before waving a welcome. In the living room, laughter had erupted as Andy and Victoria had teased each other, their respective companions laughing beside them, Jack a former firefighter who now worked in dispatch and Dylan, Hughes's current boyfriend. Victoria's best friend Travis, drink in hand, had nodded to Maya and Carina while chatting with Jo and Meredith. Meredith's children were playing with Dean Miller's daughter, happily under the loving gaze of Ben and Miranda.

After a few moments chatting with Andy and Victoria, Carina spotted Josephine Wilson and approached with a sincere smile.

-"Jo, I really wanted to thank you," she began, placing a hand on her friend's arm.

Jo, surprised but touched, had looked at her curiously.

-"Thank me? What for?"

- "The idea you suggested three weeks ago to my wonderful fiancée, that of opening a private practice here in Seattle... it saved my dreams and, in a way, my marriage too" Carina had explained, her eyes shining with gratitude.

"I've started working on this project, and it's taking shape. I feel like I can finally reconcile my passion for medicine with a better life balance, both for myself and my family."

Jo had sketched a warm smile, obviously touched by this revelation.

-"Oh, Carina... I'm so happy to hear that! But all credit to you for having the courage to take the plunge."

Carina had nodded, grateful.

-"And now that I see this project taking shape, I wonder... Would you consider coming to work for me, even part-time? I know you're very attached to Grey Sloan, and I understand if you want to stay, but I'd love to have someone as talented and multi-skilled as you on the team."

Jo had thought for a moment before smiling broadly, a burst of enthusiasm in her eyes.

-"Is it serious? I'd be really interested. I just need to see how to arrange it with Grey Sloan. Maybe start part-time, as you say, and then see. And who knows, maybe one day, after I've proven myself and gained even more experience, I too could become an associate... but, for now, it would be a great honor to work for you."

Carina had smiled, delighted and relieved at the same time.

-"It would mean so much to me to have you on the team. I'm sure you'll find the right balance."

Jo had smiled with all her teeth, as the warmth of the moment mingled with the festive atmosphere of Thanksgiving. They had been interrupted by Maya, who had come over to kiss Carina gently on the cheek.

- "I agree, you'd be perfect in this practice... thanks again" she added with a wink.

Jo had laughed and nodded, while the rest of the house buzzed with laughter, chatter and the delicious aroma of roasting turkey.

Dinner had reached its climax, and around the large table a feeling of warmth and conviviality reigned. Conversation had gradually died down, and Miranda, as attentive hostess, had suggested that everyone share a moment of gratitude, a tradition she particularly enjoyed at Thanksgiving. One by one, everyone rose to their feet, expressing what they were grateful for, each with a personal touch.

Andy had thanked her friends, whom she considered family, for their constant support. Travis had spoken of his unshakeable friendship with Victoria, and the strength of their bond at Station 19. Jo, with her usual lightness tinged with sincerity, had spoken of her gratitude for the professional opportunities she'd had at Grey Sloan, while throwing a knowing wink at Meredith and Link. The other guests, including Meredith herself, had shared their own gratitudes, focusing on family, health and loved ones.

Then it was Maya's turn. She had taken a deep breath before smiling at Carina, who was seated beside her.

"This year" she had begun, her voice slightly moved,

"I'm grateful for many things. But most of all, I'm grateful for Carina. Not only has she given me her love, she's also given me a family."

She had cast a tender glance at Andrea and Danielle, who were happily eating at the children's table.

"Thanks to her, I never feel alone, I feel... complete. These two little treasures have become a part of me, just like Carina. I'm so grateful to have all three of them in my life."

Glances had softened around the table, some guests nodding with understanding. Maya had taken Carina's hand and squeezed it gently, a smile full of tenderness on her lips.

When it was Carina's turn, she had exchanged a look with Maya, her eyes shining with emotion.

- "I'm grateful for a lot of things too" she'd begun, smiling at Maya.

"But what I want to express tonight is first and foremost my gratitude for Maya's unfailing love. She has always been there for me, with unwavering support, in happy times and in the most difficult." She'd squeezed Maya's hand a little tighter, then swept her gaze around the room, pausing for a moment on Jo.

"I'm also grateful for the opportunities life has given me these past few weeks." At this point, she had tilted her head slightly towards Josephine, without saying a word, but the hint had been clear to the latter, as well as to Maya. Jo had smiled in return, fully understanding Carina's discreet acknowledgement.

The brunette had then looked at all the faces around the table, a warm smile on her lips.

"I'm also very grateful for these new friends... no, this new family. You have all, through Maya, also found a place in my heart, and now you are my heart family."

Finally, she had turned to her children, Andrea and Danielle, who were listening intently.

"And of course, I'm infinitely grateful for my two wonderful children. You are the light of my life."

Around the table, the emotion was palpable. Maya and Carina's words had touched everyone, adding depth to this shared moment. Conversations had resumed gently, imbued with a warmth and sincerity that had intensified with each exchange.

The round table of gratitudes ended in a gentle, benevolent atmosphere. The sound of cutlery and conversation resumed softly, mingled with the laughter and murmurs of children fidgeting in their chairs. Danielle had tried to grab a piece of bread on the large table that was much too big for her, which had provoked a few bursts of laughter from the adults.

Ben had stood up, tapping his glass lightly to get everyone's attention.

- "Well, before we move on to the pumpkin pie; and I know you're all looking forward to it..." he'd quipped with a mischievous grin,

"I just wanted to thank you all for being here today. For us, Thanksgiving is more than a meal. It's a time when we realize how lucky we are to be able to share these kinds of moments together, and frankly, I wouldn't trade it for the world."

Miranda, sitting beside him, had nodded with a smile. She had spoken in turn, in her characteristic tone, firm but imbued with affection:

- "I want to say how grateful I am to see so many familiar faces, and to be able to call each of you «family»." Her gaze had lingered briefly on Dean Miller's daughter, who was eating next to Andrea, a fond smile on her lips.

"We've all been through a lot, but together we always manage to come out stronger. So, thank you all."

The room had resounded with approving murmurs and light applause before the conversation resumed.

Across the table, Andy and Travis had begun a lively discussion, mixing memories of Station 19 with a few well-placed jokes.

- "Remember that time you tried to cook for everyone?" Travis had challenged Andy, a mocking smile on his lips. "It was supposed to be a «safe» dinner, but I think you almost poisoned half the barracks."

Andy had rolled her eyes, falsely annoyed.

-"It wasn't my fault the recipe was poorly explained!"

Travis's laugh had been infectious, and soon the whole table had shared a moment of levity, enjoying this welcome break after the emotional moments.

Later, as the meal continued, Carina had met Jo's gaze across the table. This silent moment had been enough to remind Carina of everything that had played out over the last few weeks; without this idea, she, they wouldn't be here, they wouldn't be so happy today. She got up, pretending to need a glass of water, and as she passed Jo, she stopped.

- "Thanks again, Jo," she'd said softly.

"Your idea... it's changed more than I could have imagined. I can't wait to see it all come together. And who knows, maybe this project will come to fruition sooner than we think."

Jo had smiled, a mischievous twinkle in her eye.

-"Let me know when you're ready, but I'll be here. I'll be happy to work for you." She'd shrugged good-humoredly.

"Anyone would be extremely lucky to have this privilege."

Carina had nodded, grateful, laid a hand on Jo's shoulder before giving her hand a gentle squeeze and returning to her seat. Maya had given her a curious look, having clearly understood the exchange, but she hadn't said anything. They had simply smiled at each other, accomplices.

Shortly afterwards, an adorable moment had occurred: Andrea and Dean's daughter had timidly approached Miranda with sheets of paper decorated with drawings.

-"This is to say thank you," Andrea had said proudly. "We drew a turkey!"

Miranda, surprised and touched, had taken the drawings with a big smile.

- "This is the best present I've received today. Thank you, artists!" This had made everyone laugh, adding another touch of levity to the evening.

The meal was drawing to a close, and as everyone prepared to enjoy the pumpkin pie, Ben had stood up once more, this time with a more solemn expression. He had raised his glass and looked around the table, taking a moment to savor the company of loved ones before breaking the silence.

- "Before we feast on hot drinks, pecan and apple pies" he began, his voice slightly deeper:

"I think it would be appropriate to raise a toast to those who are no longer with us. To those who have been part of our lives, who have marked us, and who continue to live on through us."

A respectful silence had settled around the table as everyone raised their glasses, ready to honor their departed.

Jack, his eyes fixed on Pruitt Arike, Dean's daughter, had taken a deep breath before speaking first.

"To Dean Miller, my best friend, my brother," he had said, his voice laden with memories.

"An exceptional firefighter, a big-hearted man, and a devoted father. His spirit lives on through his daughter." He had briefly stroked Pruitt's hair, who listened earnestly despite her young age.

Carina had been the second to speak.

-"To my little brother Andrea" she had whispered, her voice full of emotion. She'd felt the warmth of Maya's hand close gently around hers, a silent but unwavering support.

"He's gone far too soon, but I think of him every day."

Andy had then raised her glass, her gaze lost in memories.

- "To my father, Captain Pruitt Herrera," she said with a slight tremor in her voice.

"He died saving our lives. I wouldn't be here today without his sacrifice."

Victoria had nodded, her gaze turned blank, gathering her thoughts.

"To Lucas Ripley, my fiancé," she made softly, a gleam of sadness in her eyes, despite Dylan's presence: "He's gone because of toxic exposure doing what he loved. I miss him every day."

Then Amelia, seated next to Meredith, had spoken, her voice laced with sweetness and melancholy.

-"To Derek, my brother. A brilliant neurosurgeon, a loving husband, and an exceptional father." Meredith, at her side, had squeezed her hand, memories flashing through her mind.

Meredith had taken over, speaking with a serene calm that barely hid the pain still present.

-"To Lexie, my little sister. She died in a plane crash, but she lives on in our hearts and in every smile of her nieces and nephews."

Travis had then raised his glass, his eyes shining with emotion.

"To Michael, my husband. Died in a forest fire. I miss him more than I can express, but every day I strive to honor his memory."

There had been a respectful silence, but Maya, sensing that something remained to be said, had gently lifted Carina's hand and shaken it. She had then spoken, her voice soft but strong, turning slightly to Carina.

- "To Danny" she'd whispered, her voice trembling with emotion but also laced with gratitude.

"He went out a hero, and in his departure, in a way, he gave me Carina... and these two wonderful children. I'm grateful for every moment with them."

Carina had squeezed Maya's hand, a sad smile on her lips, and the others had nodded silently, moved by this moment of shared truth.

A heavy silence had fallen over the room, imbued with respect and memories, but also with comfort. They were no longer alone in their grief. Together, they had formed a family, made up of bonds woven by love, mutual support and the trials they had been through together.

When the toast was over, the glasses clinked gently, each taking a sip in memory of the departed. This shared, emotional moment marked the end of the meal and the evening, but it left an indelible mark on their hearts.

Ben broke the silence with a smile.

"Well, these desserts aren't going to eat themselves," he had quipped, provoking a light laughter in the room, gently bringing the lightness back after the solemnity of the toast.

The evening had continued in a gentler atmosphere, but the memory of loved ones still floated in the air, enveloping the room in a special warmth, that of love and sharing.
