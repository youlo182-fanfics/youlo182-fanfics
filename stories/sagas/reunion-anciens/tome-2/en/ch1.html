Tome 2:


Maya stood in front of the hotel room window. It was night and she was looking out over Catania, illuminated by the city lights. The city that had taken back the sublime Italian she had loved just over fifteen years ago.

Sailors were preparing their boats for fishing, and she could see the boat's light flickering with the waves.
But she wasn't concentrating at all on what was happening. Her eyes had stared at the light and her mind had gone back to their conversation tonight and all the events that had led to her having a secret for Carina.

A secret that was no longer a secret.

She had blinked and thought back to what the Italian had said to her just before she proposed, because obviously, that's where it all started.

It had all begun at that moment, with those words.

She had put her hand on the glass in front of her, to keep herself upright, her gaze still lost in her memories, staring at that light that was pitching to the rhythm of the waves. She had blinked and a tear had run down her cheek.

he Italian had come to place a last kiss on Maya’s lips before closing her eyes, taking her bag, her suitcase and turning to leave, leaving the blonde alone in the middle of the crowd.

 

She had handed her plane ticket and her passport to the agent for the security checkpoint queue. She had moved forward in the snaking queue and when she arrived behind the last person in front of her, she had turned around.

 

Maya’s eyes were fixed on her. Her face was closed, probably the most closed she had ever seen the one she loved, except for the look in her eyes that betrayed her sadness and the broken state of her heart.

 

That was the last vision Carina had of Maya. She had arrived in Seattle with a certain Maya, she had seen the blonde blossom into the smiling woman she had spent almost a year with and now she was leaving a soulless and broken Maya. Maybe even broken forever, the joy of life had been packed into Carina’s suitcase and she could only find it again if she found the love of her life.

 

By the time the Italian had disappeared down the tiny security corridor, Maya's soul had left her body.

 


Her body was no longer able to let her speak; it was as if anesthetized. All her movements were coordinated by a mechanical intuition. She had left the terminal after a few minutes and headed for the parking lot, then to the car where Katherine and Mason were waiting.


She wasn't crying any more, just like the word, she was now incapable of crying.
She got into the car and sat down. Kathy had turned her head to speak to her daughter, but at the first syllable she saw that whoever had just gotten into the car, it wasn't Maya.

Physically, Maya was there, seated, but her daughter, the Maya of the past year, was probably still in the terminal, facing security, staring at Carina's shadow, her hand on the handle of her suitcase, both trapped in the last look they'd exchanged.

What Katherine didn't know was when this Maya would return, or even if she would ever return.

The drive to the Bishops' house had been a real torture, the car smelling of the brunette's perfume.

As soon as the handbrake was applied, the blonde got out of the car in a hurry. She breathed in the outside air as if she'd been deprived of air.
She was still lost in nothingness, but she was tachycardic.

Then she'd had the sensation of suffocating, the pain in her chest, the one that had put her into tachycardia because of Carina's intoxicating scent, was nothing compared to the sensation she now had of suffocating without her scent.

Her head was spinning, wondering if she was going to vomit.

Mason saw the panic in her eyes and hurried to the door. His sister had her hands on her knees in the front garden, and it felt as if she was going to faint.

-"Maya"

She'd heard her brother's voice pronounce her first name, coming from a distance close to the house, and she'd run inside the dwelling. She'd run into her bedroom and curled up on her bed, Carina's pillow trapped in her arms, and felt she could breathe again.

Minutes, hours, days in the same position. Mase, Katherine, Lane, Andy, Ryan, all had come, without success.
She wasn't even sleeping, she was always in a state of lethargy, her eyes staring into space, her body never changing position.

Carina's pillow curled up against her. She'd held it so tightly, for so many hours, that he probably didn't feel the Italian anymore, but for her it did.

After almost thirty-six hours like this, Maya had begun to experience auditory hallucinations.

Carina had whispered 'bambina' in her ear and she could hear her smile behind that word, behind that pronunciation.

Carina trying to wake her up.

'per sempre' Carina whispering that she would love her for life right after making love, against her lips, which she could now feel against hers again.

- "per sempre" Maya had whispered, blinking back tears. It had been almost three days since she'd last cried.

That evening, when Lane had come home from work, an argument had broken out on the first floor, and the father of the family had stormed upstairs.
The door to her bedroom had made a deafening noise as it hit the wall, and Maya hadn't even batted an eyelid.

She had felt her father's hand grip her bicep and lift her off the bed. She clung to the pillow but he'd pulled her away. She hadn't even had the strength to protest.

He had dragged her to the bathroom and put her in the shower, before turning on the cold water.

-"Your circus has gone on long enough! This isn't a bum house. You're going to give me the pleasure of a shower, and right now! And you'd better come to the table tonight!"

He had come out of the bathroom and Katherine had entered a few seconds later. 
Maya was curled up in a corner of the shower, appalled, as if she didn't know why or how she'd gotten there, the cold water still running over her body.

Her mother had turned on the hot water before helping her undress, telling her everything would be all right, then helped her shower.
Once washed, she went outside and sat down on a chair in the bathroom, while her mother went to get a tracksuit and a t-shirt for her daughter.

She had helped her get dressed and Maya had once again acted mechanically. She got up and brushed her teeth. Her body was weak, but it was as if she didn't live in it anymore, as if she didn't need it anyway.

-"Maya, sweetheart, you've got to have something to drink."

-"hum" she said, putting down her toothbrush and returning to her room.

Entering her room, the one that had seen her share her nights with Carina for the last eleven months, she had been gripped by panic.

"No! No! No!!!" She had run to the laundry room in the basement, almost falling down the stairs; driven only by the adrenaline of the disappearance of her sheets and Carina's pillowcase; and she had pulled on the door of the washing machine like a slave, but to no avail, trying to open it when it was already running, the water already present, mechanically mixing the sheets from the two beds in her room, along with some clothes.

All she managed to do was fall to the floor on her bottom, and start crying as she watched her sheets spin.
The sheets, the last vestiges of the Italian's presence in her bed.

Lane had come downstairs.

-"Didn't you finish your comedy?!" he said irritably.

- "you had no right!!!"

- "Your room stinks up the whole house! You haven't had a shower in three days.
Your sheets were smelly, I don't want others... Pests... squatting in this house.
I already had trouble getting the last one out of your room." He said with an air of satisfaction.

-"Is that Carina you're calling a 'pest'?" Maya asked, standing in front of her father with clenched fists, clenched jaw and bloodshot eyes.

-"Think twice, young lady. You haven't eaten or drunk in three days.
Punching me would take so much energy out of you, you'd probably faint." He was making himself laugh, and he seemed proud of it.

-"I hate you!" Said Maya as she turned away from him and sat down in front of the washing machine.

- "That's good, use that rage next time you're on a runway....
Really, Maya, putting yourself in such a state for a girl isn't worthy of a Bishop" he added as he began to climb the stairs.

-"Well, I guess I'm not one!"

- "You're certainly not a Shein, your mother's family are all weak-kneed."

Lane had gone upstairs and Katherine had come down.
She'd crouched behind Maya and slipped onto her daughter's shoulders the embroidered Italian soccer team sweater that, four days ago, still belonged to Carina.

The blonde had clutched the sweater in her fist, at the collar, to bring it up to her nose, to smell the Italian, and she'd started crying again.

She'd felt her mother's hands leave her shoulders and she'd grabbed them, wrapping them around her body.

-"How come you never told me it hurt this much?" Maya asked, almost accusingly, between sobs.

Katherine could hear through her weak, almost hoarse voice, her tone, her sobs, that her daughter's heart was broken; it was literally broken.

-"I'm sorry, sweetheart," her mother replied, resting her cheek close to her daughter's ear.
"I'm so helpless in the face of your pain... I wish I could take it away from you and you wouldn't feel it anymore..."

- "it hurts so much, I ache in every fiber of my body, in every muscle, in every nerve, I'd rather run until my legs can no longer support my weight, than feel this oppressive pain, caused by her absence."

-"I've never been lucky enough to know the love of my life, and it breaks my heart to know that I can't even imagine the pain you're feeling right now, and to be unable to do anything for you. I don't even know what I can do to make your life bearable. I feel useless."

-"I don't know if I'm lucky to have known her. I feel as if my heart has been ripped from my chest and yet I can still feel it beating in my body, each pulse more painful than the last. Each minute more insurmountable than the one before; each second more unbearable than the pre-existing one."

Her eyes had been caught by the spinning motion of the washing machine and she was back in her ethargic state.

- "Maya?"

But the blonde wasn't answering anymore. She stood in front of the machine her legs drawn up against her chest, her hands now barely holding her mother's on her arms, her elbows encircling her knees.

'Ciao' Carina said as she approached Maya, letting go of her bag and the handle of her suitcase to take her in her arms.

-"Hi", Maya whispered, as if the Italian were standing in front of her, hypnotized by the movement.

Katherine felt a tear roll down her cheek and stood up, leaving her daughter alone in the laundry room.

A few minutes later, it was Mase's turn to come downstairs.
He'd sat down next to Maya, looked at her, then at the machine, then back at his sister, he couldn't understand what she was looking at.

He told himself it probably had something to do with the object the hypnotists were spinning; and that Maya's state of fatigue and dehydration provoked the same sensation in her as the hypnotic spiral, but with the drum of the washing machine spinning.

He handed her a glass of water.

-"Dad said you had to drink this."

She'd taken the glass and drunk it all in one gulp, without even thinking about what she'd just ingested, then handed it back to him.

"'Maya's very beautiful, and I like her a lot...' that was the first thing Carina said to me about you, well not the first, first, but the first that meant she liked you."

The interlocking word, 'Carina', had been spoken.

The blonde had turned her head towards her brother and looked at him, hoping his sentence wouldn't end there.

She had turned her head back to the washing machine and Mason had put his head on her shoulder.

They'd stood there for a moment without speaking, then Katherine had called them to eat. They'd gone upstairs. Maya had followed just so her father wouldn't come back to see her.

She had sat at the table, her fork playing with one of her vegetables. She hadn't eaten, but her mother had asked her to at least drink.

Then, when her parents' and brother's meal was almost over, she asked if she could leave the family table. She went back upstairs and curled up on her mattress.
The bed wasn't even made up, but she found that even without the pillow head, it still smelled of the brunette.

She had closed her eyes and pressed the pillow hard against her nose. She had the sensation, thanks to the sweater, that her arms were encircling her body.

After a few days, Maya had finally eaten, but probably because her mother had taken some lasagne out of the freezer that Carina had made a few weeks ago.

She'd eaten and couldn't stop her eyes from watering. It was the Italian's birthday. She'd gone on Facebook and looked at her wall, full of people who'd left words for the brunette on her eighteenth birthday. Plenty of messages and no reply, yet with the nine-hour time difference, the seventeenth was about to end in Sicily.

Was the brunette in the same state as her? In any case, that was the question Maya was asking herself. She didn't hope so, for Carina own well-being.

A few days later, the blonde's father had gone to see Andy and asked her if she could invite Maya to a party.
She'd gone and the blonde was still wearing Carina's sweater, despite the heat.

Maya had agreed to leave her room and go to her best friend Ryan's, to see her friends; she'd sat on the sofa and hadn't moved all evening. She could see everyone moving around her and the hours ticking by, like in a music video from the two-thousandths, or a movie where the protagonist is questioning his whole life during a night out with friends.

-"...I dare you to kiss our zombie".

Addison had approached, sat down next to Maya, still inert on the sofa, and come to rest her lips on hers.

The blonde had pushed her violently and stood up, throwing her against the back and armrest of the sofa, making her practically lie down, her legs still on the floor.

-"Never again in your life! Never again  you do that!" The runner had felt the rage 'the Lane rage' seeping into every pore of her skin. Her finger pointed at her. Carina's lips were no longer the last to touch hers.

-"Maya, it was just a game," Todd said, putting his hand on her shoulder.

The blonde had pushed him back with both hands on his chest.

- "You stay away from me!" her eyes were bloodshot.

She had come out just as everyone else had stopped and looked at her.

She'd gone to the side of the house, where just two weeks ago she and Carina had made love.

She was with her back against the structure of the house. Her hand had caressed the wall behind her, her fingers had closed little by little and she had felt a new wave of tears rise up inside her. Then she'd run and gone home.

When she got home, she realized that she hadn't felt as alive since Carina's departure as she had just now, when she had felt a pain other than the one in her heart and body caused by Carina's absence.
A pain not linked to the Italian, but to the pain of having run without warming up, the pain of not having run for over two weeks.

So she started running again. She'd run the two and a half miles to the Public Market, then back home again.

When she got home, she wanted to run again, but she hadn't run in over two weeks and her legs were shaking. Her diet consisted of drinking water, with her father occasionally slipping sugar cubes into her glass. And a few forks of whatever was on her plate every fourth meal.

So this was the pain she'd told her mother about, the one that would be better than the pain caused by Carina's departure. The pain caused by the inability of her legs to support her weight.

She had come home and gone to the shower. She sat in the shower tray and let the cold water run over her body.
The adrenalin from the race was leaving her body, and she had passed her hand over her lips.
She'd started to cry, then put her hand behind the products on the shower rack and sent everything tumbling out. She'd retrieved her bath towel and started screaming into it, mixing the Italian's first name with her cries of pain.

She lay down on the shower floor, the cold water pounding her body. She'd finally fallen asleep. It was Katherine, almost an hour later, who had woken her up, after turning off the water.

-"Maya, Maya, wake up. Maya you're going to be sick, how long have you been lying there?"

- "Is Carina still gone?"

- "Sweetheart..." said her pained mother.

- "I could catch pneumonia and die."

- "no"

She'd helped her up and into her pyjamas. Then she'd helped Maya into bed and given her the Italian's sweater as if it were a cuddly toy. It was soaked and she'd sweated in it, but in her mind it smelled of her perfume.

-"Thank you, Mom", Maya said like a five-year-old before falling asleep with the sweater against her face.

When she woke up a few hours later, she had dressed and gone downstairs. She had eaten a banana, then a few grapes, then a kiwi under her mother's astonished eyes. She put her hand on an apple and stopped. She had looked at her hand on the fruit for a moment. And she'd seen Carina bite into her apple again, almost a year before.

She'd changed her mind about the apple.

"I, I'm going for a run."

- "really?" Katherine asked.

- "yes"

Maya had gone out for a run after warming up this time.
When she returned an hour and a half later, her whole body ached. So much so that she enjoyed the pain emanating from her body, trying to concentrate on the ones that were everywhere but in her chest; and on the one that wasn't caused by Carina's absence, but rather by the run she'd just completed.

She'd gone for a shower and climbed down into Carina's sweater.

She'd looked at the fruit bowl and stared at the apple again.

-"I made you some pasta," Kathy said as she entered the kitchen.

- "thanks Mom"

She had sat down on one of the kitchen stools to eat. She'd seen herself with Carina the day after their first time. She'd looked at the now empty seat. Then she'd looked at the end of the center island, where she'd tackled the Italian a few weeks ago, after Mason had drooled at seeing her girlfriend in ridiculously skimpy shorts.

She'd eaten her pasta, reliving a year's worth of memories in that kitchen with the woman who haunted her thoughts day and night.

The time she'd helped Carina cook arancini, when they'd started talking again after making love for the first time. When she'd lifted the Italian onto the center island. Or when they'd only met again for Valentine's Day.

The pain that lived in her body and heart at all these memories could only mean one thing.

Carina was real.
