That year, they had celebrated fourteen years of marriage. The ceremony for Maya's handover had been presented by Mayor Montgomery.

Of course, Maya and Carina's daughters were allowed to attend. Vittoria talked about baseball all the time, and the firefighters from Station 19 with whom they sat at the table were impressed by her knowledge of the subject. In addition to being a member of Seattle's junior team, she watched all the games, and sometimes even went to see them with her family. In fact, it was one of her favorite activities, when they would all put on their jerseys and go to a match, all five of them matching.

As for Giulietta, she was always very sensitive, a true artist who loved to sing, but also to draw, like her uncle Mason. She drew really well, and what she liked best of all was drawing her loved ones. On top of that, she had a great imagination and loved making up stories, sometimes getting lost in her own world.

And last but not least, Jordan had a gift for dance. She loved to dance, no matter what kind of music it was playing, and couldn't wait for the evening to get underway so she could let off steam on the dancefloor.

All three had put on a dress. Carina was in a suit and her wife in formal wear, her class A.
Many of their friends, at least one of whom was a firefighter, were present, as this was an official ceremony.

There were journalists, «important people», a few captains, the battalions, Natacha Ross and Robert Sullivan ; and her family. And, of course, the firefighters, invited by the blonde. 

Carina had gone to the bar to get a drink and had been approached.

-"you look familiar..."

- "you're a very beautiful woman"

- "thank you, and sorry... but I love my wife"

- "is it really serious between you two? "

- "and there's no possibility of us ever seeing each other, just you and me? "

Carina's eyes had fallen on the firefighter in front of her. It had to be said that she was a very beautiful woman; even more than that.

"I can see in your eyes that you're not indifferent to me..."

- "save me a dance... and we'll see what we can do... but first of all, would you have a first and last name to go with such... beauty?"

- "wow... even your name and surname are perfect..." replied the Italian, grabbing her wife's tie and tugging at her, biting her lip with a smile as she came to kiss her.

After they'd returned from the bar, they'd all been invited to the table and Travis had made a speech thanking the voters for having voted for him; he'd also informed them that this evening was very important for him, but also for someone dear to him. He was referring, of course, to Maya.

Then it had been Ross's turn to speak, she'd said her goodbyes to Seattle, then invited another person to come on stage.

-"... To introduce the Fire Chief who will replace me, I invite her wife, Carina DeLuca-Bishop, to join me on stage. Please give her a round of applause."

Maya was unaware of this part of the ceremony. Indeed, the Italian had been contacted by Ross through Travis, to come and give a speech for her wife's introduction to her new position.
Without saying a word, the blonde had watched the brunette leave in the direction of the stage.

-"As Natacha told you, I'm the wife of Seattle's future Fire Chief.

I've known my wife for over thirty years, so we're not getting any younger, but I met her when she was fifteen. Even back then, one of her favorite places in the world was Station 19, the station of Pruitt Herrera at the time, the father of her best friend, none other than Captain Herrera of Station 23.

For Halloween, a few months after we met, when she was sixteen, Maya dressed up in Andrea Herrera's young firefighter clothes, and as a joke, ... Or not, I told her she'd make an excellent firefighter... Granted, at the time it was mainly because she looked stunning in that uniform, which she still wears perfectly to this day.
As for me, before she turned seventeen I had to go back to Italy, as you may have guessed from my accent, but I wasn't born here.

Anyway, I saw Maya again two years later, and she told me she was a volunteer firefighter and wanted to go to the Seattle Fire Academy...

The reason I was so happy to see her again was that we were in London at the same time, me for my studies and my wonderful wife, who wasn't yet, to win her Olympic medal. My wife is the most impressive person I know. When she has a goal in mind, she doesn't let go, she goes right to the end until she achieves it.

And her first big victory was this medal, and I'm happy to have been there for her. She won first place by beating her own record, with a sprain, which she got partly because of me.

Then we were separated again, but Maya pursued her career goals; indeed, after her medal she entered firefighter school and graduated a few months later.

Within a few years she had become a lieutenant, but unfortunately I wasn't there to see her rise, and it's one of the things that saddens me most, not having been there for every one of her victories.

A year before she and I met again, she became a captain, still with Station 19, the one she'd been a lieutenant in, a firefighter, a probie, a volunteer firefighter, the one she'd come to when she was a teenager and even before that, the one she'd broken an arm in while trying to climb up against the pole... yes, yes while trying to climb up, you heard me right...
Station 19 was her family for a long time. For thirty years.

In two thousand and twenty-five we met again and many victories followed. And I hope to be one of them. Our marriage, the birth of our three wonderful daughters who are as perfect as their mother, right up to her time as Battalion Chief.

Her life wasn't always simple, an oppressive father, but one who contributed to her champion spirit, our separation, life's trials, interventions gone wrong and the year she became Battalion Chief, she even nearly lost her life, while pregnant with our twins."

They never talked about it in front of their daughters. They knew something had happened because of her mother's scars; Jordan's trauma, even many years later; but the subject was quite taboo, they preferred to think of that date as their daughters' birth, not her potential death. Their potential death.

"For you, my wife is an ambitious woman who was able to make all her classes in the same firehouse, who won an Olympic medal and who is the first woman to have gone from volunteer firefighter, to today's Fire Chief, only in Seattle.
It was another of her goals in life, her greatest objective, her greatest victory, another of her dreams come true, if not to say, the greatest; the fulfillment of her entire professional life, her entire adult life...

She's the first Seattle resident to become Seattle's Fire Chief, even if she is the second woman. For you, my wife is an exemplary firefighter, one who doesn't hesitate to put the lives of her family and her teams first. She was the first female captain of Station 19, the youngest captain in Seattle, the youngest Battalion Chief in Seattle.

But for us, for our daughters and for me, she's not just a superhero who runs into buildings as everyone else runs out, for us she's the woman who makes sure to get out of those burning buildings to come home to us every night. For me, Maya is the fifteen-year-old girl who made me fall in love with her for the first and last time in my life.

She's the woman who ran after me in a hotel lobby the night before the Olympics and sprained her ankle; she's the woman who told me she'd be the person I'd need her to be for me, after we hadn't seen each other for thirteen years. This is the woman who proposed to me countless times. This is the woman who let me heal at my own pace for the loss of my son. She's the woman who sat on the floor of a hallway outside my bedroom when we sometimes yelled at each other before going to bed. This is the woman who agreed to marry me on a beach in Sicily. This is the woman who arranged a secret wedding for me, after fifteen years of jotting down ideas in a little black notebook, a wedding that any woman would dream of having. She's the woman who made me a mother again.

And for our daughters, she's the mother who cries when they're sick, who stays up all night when they have a fever. Who will put down for a day if one of them tells her she wants to stay with her. Who doesn't hesitate to take our girls back to bed with us if they're having a nightmare. Who plays with them every night, reads them a story and makes sure she can tuck them in every day since they were born. The one who has only once, since our eldest was born, agreed to our daughters not sleeping with all three of us at the same time, and still went out to get them in the middle of the night.

For you, my wife is the future Fire Chief, but for us, she's our whole world. She's the best mother in the world and the most extraordinary wife. Please welcome to this stage the woman who does her utmost every day to give me the life of my dreams.
The mother of my children, the love of my life.

Fire Chief Maya DeLuca-Bishop".

Everyone applauded, and Maya rose from her chair, wiping under her eyes. She had hugged each of her daughters and kissed them on the head before joining her wife on the dais. She had slid her hand down her cheek and come to kiss her.

- "I love you my love"

-"thank you... Sorry, I didn't expect my wife to speak. I'm a little emotional.

Mayor Montgomery, Chief Ross, thank you for having confidence in me and entrusting me with a position which, as my wife told you, is one of my goals in life and has been for a long time. I hope I never disappoint my teams, from my battalions to my probies.

I could never have made the choices I made, the decisions I made, if I didn't think about the repercussions of my actions on them. The consequences of my actions reflect not only on me, but also on them.

I hope that I will be an excellent fire chief for Seattle just as Chief Ross was; but most of all, I hope that I will remain the best version of myself as a mother and as a wife. Thank you to everyone here tonight. You have all contributed in your own way to my achievement to this position."

Carina had slipped her hand into her wife's and they had returned to their table.
