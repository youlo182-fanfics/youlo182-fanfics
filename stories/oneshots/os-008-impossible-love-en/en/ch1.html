Maya had just turned sixteen, she had been out of school for a year and a half and was helping her mother, Katherine, with the chores that women had to do.

In their community, the Amish, this is how it was done. Children went to school until they were fourteen, and after that, girls helped their mothers and boys helped their fathers.

A different way of life did not appeal to her, she had never known anything other than the life she lived today. She lived in the community of Elkhart County, Indiana, with her mother, father Lane, and brother, Mason.

Typically, Amish families had many more children than that. But a health problem had left Maya's mother infertile.

From now on, Maya had four years to find a husband. It was customary for women to get married at twenty.

She knew, however, that her parents had a preference on Jack, who was a year older than her. He came from a good family, he was the son of the priest of their community and he was very popular. However, this was not particularly problematic since his community still practiced polygamy.

Maya was a simple girl, she got good grades in school, when she was there, she was even promised a teaching position, if she decided to stay in the community, after her Rumspringa, the gap year, during which she could go out into the world and decide whether or not she wanted to be baptized and stay with the Amish. However, if she made this choice she would only be a teacher until she married. After the wedding, life was dedicated to the family.

She could choose a husband from outside her community. From another community, even a non-Amish, as long as he became one for their engagement.

She could hardly believe that within a few hours she would be taking off her white bonnet and blue dress and putting on a different outfit. She had already seen the clothes that were offered to the girls for their Rumspringa and she was not necessarily attracted by them.

It is especially her mother who had insisted that she made this caesura. She wanted to be sure that her daughter would not regret her choice. She hadn't dared to do it herself and sometimes wondered what her life would have been like if she hadn't made the choice to be baptized. Or to marry Lane.

He was a good father and a good husband. He was a hard-working man who didn't look for another wife when his own was struck down by an illness that made her infertile, even though he had every right to do so.

Mason was still young, he was two years younger than Maya. He would soon leave school to join his father in the fields. However, he was not the kind of boy who wanted to work the land. He was more interested in simple gardening or embroidery with his mother. He liked everything that required delicacy, he had the soul of an artist.

Maya was a young blonde girl, very pretty, despite the fact that she didn't know it, and quite shy. Unlike her brother, she enjoyed spending time with her father, plowing the fields, despite her asthma.

Allergies and asthma were quite common among the Amish and they knew exactly how to treat them. They were allowed to go to the doctor, but they were not big on vaccinations.
